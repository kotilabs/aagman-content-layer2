# Instagram Carousel Draft — Signal `india-fo-microstructure-reset`

**Format:** Standalone Instagram carousel  
**Slide count:** 8  
**Voice:** Financial Times leader column meets Matt Levine — dry, confident, structurally argued, lightly literary.

---

## Slide 1 — Hook

**Kicker:** Shrunk on purpose

**Headline:** The world's largest retail options market just printed its smallest month since 2023.

**Body:** Options volumes halved in FY26. In August, NSE equity-derivatives turnover fell to ₹34.48 lakh crore — a 21-month low. Neither was an accident.

**Hero stat:** ₹34.48 lakh crore — NSE August 2026 equity-derivatives turnover, lowest since Nov 2023 (ET, 2 Sep 2026)

**Design note:** The number "₹34.48 lakh crore" as oversized type on near-black, with a thin descending line underneath. One accent colour. Nothing else.

**Speaker note:** Names the shift upfront — a designed contraction, now visible in the tape.

---

## Slide 2 — The toolkit, and the response

**Kicker:** Two years, six levers

**Headline:** SEBI didn't ban the trade. It tripled the price of a ticket.

**Body:** Contract sizes up from ₹5–10 lakh to ₹15–20 lakh. One weekly expiry per exchange. Premiums paid upfront. Margins watched intraday. STT on futures up 150% from April 2026. Contracts on the two exchanges fell from 16 billion in October 2024 to 4 billion by March 2025; global options volume dropped 51% that quarter, mostly India.

**Hero stat:** 16 bn → 4 bn contracts in five months (FIA, May 2025)

**Design note:** A vertical rule-timeline, Oct 2024 to Apr 2026, each measure as a dated stamp, with one steep falling contract-count line beneath. Document aesthetic.

**Speaker note:** The mechanism and its scale in one frame — capital and cost per trade, not prohibition.

---

## Slide 3 — The bill, then the scorecard

**Kicker:** Why they did it — and what year one delivered

**Headline:** 93% of retail F&O traders lost money. Then fewer traders lost more each.

**Body:** SEBI's own studies: more than one crore individuals, 93% of them losing across FY22–24 — ₹1.8 lakh crore in all. In FY26, 4.6 million traders stopped trading. Aggregate net losses fell 18% to ₹91,685 crore. The average loss rose to ₹1.17 lakh. 87.7% still lost.

**Hero stat:** Active individual F&O traders: 10.62 mn → 8.75 mn, first fall since FY16 (SEBI via Informist, Aug 2026)

**Design note:** Two numbers facing each other — "-18% total losses" in green, "+2% loss per trader" in amber — under a small "93%" ghosted in the background.

**Speaker note:** The regulator's case and the first verdict in one frame: protection by subtraction.

---

## Slide 4 — Pivot

**Kicker:** The reframe

**Headline:** This isn't a safer retail market. It's a market retail exited.

**Body:** The 77% of traders with under ₹1 lakh of margin ran 8% of turnover. The top 10 prop desks run 85% of options activity. Prop profits fell 3%. FPI and mutual-fund profits halved.

**Hero stat:** Top-10 prop desks: ~75% of prop profits, ~85% of options activity, FY26 (SEBI via Informist; 77/23 split from a 5,050-trader indicative sample)

**Design note:** Bold typographic slide. "Retail exited." top half; "The desks stayed." bottom half. No charts, maximum contrast.

**Speaker note:** Names the thesis — institutionalized, not fixed.

---

## Slide 5 — The plumbing, and the last hour

**Kicker:** What the flow did

**Headline:** Half the hedging engine is gone. A new closing auction is thinning the rest.

**Body:** Dealers who sell options hedge in the underlying stocks; retail's small tickets aggregated into a continuous liquidity engine and the fastest way to price an RBI decision or an election. Since 3 August, one official closing price is set in a 20-minute auction from 3:15 pm — so thin-margin traders square off by 3:10 and the last hour drains away. Daily avoidance compounded into August's turnover, the lowest since November 2023.

**Hero stat:** "Brokers are squaring off positions earlier by 3.05–3.10" — Axis Securities, via ET (2 Sep 2026); expiry-day share of options turnover 70% → 59%, FY25 → FY26 (SEBI via Informist)

**Design note:** A pipe diagram (retail ticket → dealer → hedge orders, the final pipe at half width) beside a clock-face of the trading day, the CAS window in one accent colour.

**Speaker note:** Why a derivatives reset changes how the cash market itself trades.

---

## Slide 6 — The exchange war

**Kicker:** Repriced duopoly

**Headline:** In April, BSE beat NSE in F&O turnover for the first time. On price.

**Body:** BSE charges zero on futures and a seventh of NSE's options fee. Its share hit 55.4%. NSE still holds two-thirds of options premium — but its fee growth slowed from 92% to 2%.

**Hero stat:** BSE 55.4% vs NSE 44.6% notional F&O share, Apr 2026 (Trade Brains)

**Design note:** Two crossing lines, NSE falling, BSE rising, the crossing point marked "April 2026."

**Speaker note:** Shows the economics of the duopoly being rewritten mid-flight.

---

## Slide 7 — The leak

**Kicker:** Where the appetite went

**Headline:** Record SIPs on one side. Offshore futures marketing on the other.

**Body:** SIP contributions hit a record ₹32,087 crore in March — the intended destination, though stoppage ratios crossed 100% that spring. US prop firms now pitch displaced Indian traders micro futures in what they themselves call a FEMA grey zone. That flow is marketed. It is not measured.

**Hero stat:** SIP record ₹32,087 crore, Mar 2026 (AMFI via Finnovate)

**Design note:** A forked arrow: left branch to a mutual-fund icon with the SIP number, right branch to a US futures icon labelled "unmeasured." Even visual weight — the asymmetry is in the labels.

**Speaker note:** Frames the unresolved core: retired risk versus exported risk.

---

## Slide 8 — Open question

**Kicker:** What decides it

**Headline:** SEBI removed the flow. Whether it removed the *risk* — or moved it off the domestic tape — is still open.

**Body:** Korea ran this experiment in 2012: volumes fell 63% and never came back. China's 2015 clampdown weakened price discovery itself. India's version is two years in. Watch September's turnover, the next event day, and where the 4.6 million exits went.

**Hero stat:** KOSPI 200 options: ~1.58 bn contracts (2012) → ~580 mn (2013) — Korean market data

**Design note:** The question as large centred type on plain background; three small watch-items listed beneath. No CTA, no link.

**Speaker note:** Closes on the distinction that decides the policy's verdict — protection or destruction.

---

## Deck arc summary

The carousel opens with the lowest-since-2023 turnover print and the fact that the shrinkage is policy, not cycle. It fuses the rule mechanics with the global scale of the response, then pairs the regulator's justification with the FY26 scorecard: fewer losers, losing more each, in a market now run by concentrated algo capital. The back half maps the second-order effects — the hedging engine withdrawn from the cash market with CAS draining the last hour, BSE's fee-priced coup over NSE, and the unmeasured offshore leak — before closing on the question of whether the risk was retired or merely relocated.
