# LinkedIn Carousel — India's F&O Microstructure Reset

*Standalone carousel: full argument resolves within the deck. No blog.*

---

## Slide 1 — Hook

- **Kicker:** The deliberate shrink
- **Headline:** The world's largest retail options market is being dismantled on purpose — and the side effects are now measurable.
- **Body:** Options volumes on NSE and BSE fell 51% in FY26 after SEBI's two-year reset. In August, NSE's monthly equity-derivatives turnover hit ₹34.48 lakh crore — the lowest since November 2023.
- **Hero stat or quote:** Options volumes -51% in FY26 — SEBI annual report 2025-26 via ET, 6 Aug 2026; ₹34.48 lakh crore, lowest since Nov 2023 — ET, 2 Sep 2026.
- **Design note:** A single descending bar series labelled by fiscal year, with FY26's bar roughly half of FY25's. Muted navy; the halved bar carries the whole slide.
- **Speaker note:** Names the shift: this is not a cycle, it is policy — and the policy is working on its own terms.

---

## Slide 2 — The rules, and the response

- **Kicker:** Six levers, two years
- **Headline:** SEBI didn't ban speculation. It priced it out.
- **Body:** Minimum contract size tripled from ₹5–10 lakh to a ₹15–20 lakh band — the Nifty lot went 25 to 75 to 65. One weekly expiry per exchange. Full option premium upfront from February 2025. Intraday position monitoring. A 2% extra margin on expiry-day short options. Then, from April 2026, STT on futures went from 0.02% to 0.05%. The tape answered: contracts on India's two exchanges fell from 16 billion in October 2024 to 4 billion by March 2025, dragging global options volume down 51% year-on-year in Q1 2025.
- **Hero stat or quote:** India two-exchange volume: 16 bn → 4 bn contracts, Oct 2024 → Mar 2025 — FIA MarketVoice, 23 May 2025.
- **Design note:** A vertical timeline from Oct 2024 to Apr 2026, each rule as a dated stamp; beneath it, one falling contract-count line. Institutional, document-like.
- **Speaker note:** The mechanism and its scale in one slide: every rule raised the capital or the cost per ticket, and the response bent world statistics.

---

## Slide 3 — The bill, then the scorecard

- **Kicker:** Why SEBI did it — and what year one delivered
- **Headline:** 93% of retail F&O traders lost money before the reset. After it, fewer traders lost — and each lost more.
- **Body:** SEBI's case: 93% of over one crore individual traders lost money across FY22–24, an aggregate ₹1.8 lakh crore; in FY25, 91% lost, with net losses of ₹1.05 lakh crore. The FY26 scorecard: active individual traders fell 18% to 8.75 million — the first decline since FY16. Aggregate net losses fell 18% to ₹91,685 crore. The average remaining trader lost 2% more, ₹1.17 lakh. 87.7% still lost.
- **Hero stat or quote:** 4.6 million FY25 traders did not trade in FY26; avg loss ₹1.17 lakh (+2%) — SEBI analysis via Informist, 20 Aug 2026.
- **Design note:** A paired panel: left, the pre-reset bill (93%, ₹1.8 lakh crore); right, the FY26 scorecard with "losses -18%" in green against "loss per trader +2%" in amber. The tension is the slide.
- **Speaker note:** The regulator's case and its first verdict in one frame: protection arrived as subtraction, not as better outcomes.

---

## Slide 4 — Pivot

- **Kicker:** The reframe
- **Headline:** This isn't a safer market for retail. It's a market retail can no longer afford to enter.
- **Body:** 77% of traders used under ₹1 lakh of margin — they ran just 8% of turnover. The 23% with more capital now run 92% of it. The top 10 proprietary desks took ~75% of prop profits and ~85% of options activity. Proprietary profits fell 3%. FPI profits fell 55%.
- **Hero stat or quote:** Top-10 prop desks: ~75% of prop gross profit, ~85% of options activity, FY26 — SEBI via Informist. (77/23 split from SEBI's 5,050-trader sample report; SEBI flags it as indicative.)
- **Design note:** A single stacked bar: a thin grey 77% slice labelled "8% of turnover" against a thick dark 23% slice labelled "92% of turnover."
- **Speaker note:** Names the thesis explicitly — the market was institutionalized, not fixed.

---

## Slide 5 — The plumbing, and the last hour

- **Kicker:** What the flow was doing
- **Headline:** Half the hedging flow is gone. A new closing auction is thinning what remains.
- **Body:** Options sold by dealers must be delta-hedged in the underlying; millions of small retail tickets aggregated into a continuous bid-offer engine in Nifty and Sensex stocks, and expiry day — 70% of options turnover in FY25, down to 59% — was the fastest channel for pricing events. On 3 August a second change landed: the Closing Auction Session now sets one official closing price for F&O stocks between 3:15 and 3:35 pm. Brokers report squaring off by 3:05–3:10 to avoid the window. Daily avoidance compounded into August's turnover print, a 21-month low.
- **Hero stat or quote:** "Brokers are squaring off positions earlier by 3.05–3.10" — Rajesh Palviya, Axis Securities, via ET, 2 Sep 2026.
- **Design note:** Two panels: a flow diagram ("retail option ticket" → "dealer book" → "hedge orders in cash market," the last pipe at half width) beside a market-day clock with the 3:15–3:35 CAS window in a single accent colour.
- **Speaker note:** The two second-order liquidity effects — one structural, one behavioural — that the August number is now measuring.

---

## Slide 6 — The exchange war

- **Kicker:** Duopoly, repriced
- **Headline:** In April, BSE overtook NSE in F&O turnover for the first time — on price.
- **Body:** BSE's notional F&O share hit 55.4% versus NSE's 44.6%. BSE charges zero fees on futures and 0.005% on options; NSE charges 0.0355%. NSE still holds ~66% of options premium — the real money. Meanwhile NSE's options fee growth has slowed from +92% to +2%, and Angel One's quarterly profit fell 49% when volumes first cracked.
- **Hero stat or quote:** BSE 55.4% vs NSE 44.6% notional F&O share, Apr 2026 — Trade Brains, 4 May 2026; NSE options fees +2% vs +92% — Bloomberg via FIA.
- **Design note:** Two crossing share-of-turnover lines, NSE descending, BSE ascending, crossing at April 2026.
- **Speaker note:** Shows the duopoly's economics being rewritten mid-flight — volume that remains is contestable on price.

---

## Slide 7 — The displacement question

- **Kicker:** Where did the appetite go
- **Headline:** Some of it went into SIPs. Some of it is being marketed offshore.
- **Body:** Monthly SIP contributions hit a record ₹32,087 crore in March 2026 and delivery ratios rose to ~30% — the intended destination, though SIP stoppage ratios crossed 100% that same spring. And US futures prop firms now market directly to displaced Indian traders: simulated funded accounts, evaluation fees under $100, in what the vendors themselves call a FEMA grey zone. Nobody has measured that flow.
- **Hero stat or quote:** SIP record ₹32,087 crore, Mar 2026 — AMFI via Finnovate; Tradeify pitch to Indian F&O traders, 1 Aug 2026 (vendor material).
- **Design note:** A split arrow from "exited F&O trader": one branch to a mutual-fund icon labelled with the SIP record, one branch to a US-flag futures icon labelled "unmeasured."
- **Speaker note:** Frames the unresolved core: was the risk retired, or exported?

---

## Slide 8 — The analogue, and the open question

- **Kicker:** What to watch
- **Headline:** SEBI retired the flow. Whether it retired the *risk* — or moved it off the domestic tape — is still open.
- **Body:** Korea ran this experiment in 2012: a fivefold multiplier increase cut KOSPI 200 options volume ~63% within a year, and the market never came back. China's 2015 futures clampdown weakened price discovery itself. For India, watch September's turnover to separate CAS adaptation from structural decline. Watch event days — RBI policy, earnings — for slower price discovery. And watch whether the 4.6 million who exited went to SIPs or to offshore leverage. The answer decides whether this was investor protection or liquidity destruction.
- **Hero stat or quote:** "CAS is here to stay" — SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey, Aug 2026, via ET; KOSPI 200 options ~1.58 bn contracts (2012) → ~580 mn (2013) — Korean derivatives-journal data.
- **Design note:** The three watch-items as a minimal checklist on plain background, with a small Korea reference card in the corner. No chart, no CTA, no link.
- **Speaker note:** Leaves the argument hanging on the distinction that matters, with structural memory as perspective rather than prediction.

## Deck arc summary

The deck opens with the fact that the collapse is deliberate and measured (51% FY26 volume decline, August's 21-month-low turnover), walks through the rule mechanics and the global scale of the response in one frame, then pairs the regulator's justification with the FY26 scorecard: protection arrived as exclusion, and the surviving market is concentrated algo capital. The back half maps the second-order costs — hedging flow withdrawn from the cash market compounded by CAS draining the last hour, the exchange duopoly repriced on fees, and the unresolved question of whether retail risk appetite went into mutual funds or offshore into US futures. It closes on the Korea analogue and the three observations that will separate protection from destruction.
