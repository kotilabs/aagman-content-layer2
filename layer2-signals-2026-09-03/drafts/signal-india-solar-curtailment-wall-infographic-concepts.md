## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: Surplus at noon, shortage at dusk — one day on the Indian grid
- **Central insight:** In the record-demand month of May 2026, India was simultaneously refusing solar power at midday and hitting an all-time demand record in the evening — the curtailment problem is a time-and-place problem, not a generation problem.
- **Data to include:**
  - May 2026 solar curtailment: **3,235 GWh** (MNRE / Grid Controller of India, 28 Jul 2026).
  - All-time peak demand record: **270.82 GW on 21 May 2026** (The Hindu BusinessLine, 1 Jun 2026).
  - RTM price shape: **near zero in solar hours, ₹10/unit ceiling post-sunset** on selected summer sessions (NDTV Profit, 28 Jul 2026 — label as session-level, not average).
  - IEX DAM average: **₹3.8/unit FY26 vs ₹4.4/unit FY25** (ICRA, May 2026).
- **Visualization type:** Dual-axis intraday curve — solar generation potential vs. delivered generation (the shaded gap is curtailment), with the exchange price line below it showing the midday collapse and evening spike.
- **Composition notes:** The reader's eye lands first on the shaded "refused solar" wedge at midday, then follows the evening price spike to the ₹10 cap. A single caption line carries the record-demand date. Payoff: the same grid, hours apart, saying "too much" and "not enough."
- **Sources:** Moneycontrol (28 Jul 2026), The Hindu BusinessLine (1 Jun 2026), NDTV Profit (28 Jul 2026), Fortune India/ICRA (7 May 2026).

### Concept 2: The buildout vs. the wires — a five-year scoreboard
- **Central insight:** India added renewable capacity at record pace while meeting only ~80% of transmission targets — the constraint inverted from panels to corridors.
- **Data to include:**
  - RE capacity added: **50.9 GW FY26 vs 28.7 GW FY25** (+77%) (ICRA, Jun 2026).
  - All-source capacity added: **52,537 MW, Apr 2025–Jan 2026**; solar ~34,955 MW (Ministry of Power via Kotak Neo, 22 May 2026).
  - Transmission attainment: **~80% of annual targets, 5-yr avg**; FY27 ISTS target **25,146 ckm**; **1 in 4** schemes ≥1 year late; **20 GW** facing >4-month connectivity delays in FY27 (Ember, 19 May 2026).
  - Pooling stations: Khavda ~35.2 GW, Bikaner ~32.3 GW, Bhadla ~27.6 GW (Ember).
- **Visualization type:** Timeline / twin-track chart — an accelerating "capacity added" line against a flat "transmission built vs target" bar series, with the 8,133 GWh curtailment quarter as the collision point at the end.
- **Composition notes:** Left-to-right reading: the lines diverge steadily, and the final frame is the curtailment bar. The 18-months-vs-5-years build-time callout sits beneath the divergence. Payoff: the collision was scheduled years in advance.
- **Sources:** ICRA (Jun 2026), Kotak Neo (22 May 2026), Ember (19 May 2026), Moneycontrol (28 Jul 2026).

### Concept 3: The curtailment map — where the power dies
- **Central insight:** Transmission-driven curtailment is geographically concentrated — the Northern and Western grids lost 300 GWh in Q1 2026 while the Southern grid lost nothing, showing the problem is synchronisation, not renewables per se.
- **Data to include:**
  - Q1 2026 transmission-driven curtailment: **NR 178 GWh, WR 122 GWh, SR 0 GWh** (Ember, 19 May 2026).
  - Total Q1 2026 curtailment: **~470 GWh** (~300 GWh transmission, ~170 GWh inflexibility).
  - Single-day loss: **34 GWh on 30 March 2026** (~5 million urban households' daily use).
  - Emergency TRAS: **15.76 GWh/day** average in April; **80.5 GWh on 5 April** (Grid India via NDTV Profit).
- **Visualization type:** Geographic — a schematic India map with regional bubbles sized by curtailment, plus a small "one bad day" inset for 30 March / 5 April.
- **Composition notes:** The zero in the South must be visually prominent — it is the control group that turns a complaint into a diagnosis. A footnote is required context: the South's clean record partly reflects slower RE buildout there. Payoff: same country, same rules, different planning outcomes.
- **Sources:** Ember (19 May 2026), pv magazine India (20 May 2026), NDTV Profit (28 Jul 2026).

### Concept 4: The financial loop — from curtailed electrons to costlier capital
- **Central insight:** Curtailment is not just wasted energy — it is a tax on project cash flows that is already thinning auctions, stalling PPAs and slowing the next round of buildout.
- **Data to include:**
  - ReNew: **up to 15%** of solar output curtailed on certain days (ET/Bloomberg, 6 May 2026).
  - RE auctions: **14.7 GW FY26 vs 40.2 GW FY25 vs 47 GW FY24** (ICRA, Jun 2026).
  - **~45 GW** of awarded projects stalled awaiting PPA/PSA signing (ICRA / MNRE relief package).
  - RE investment: **$3.37 bn in Q1 2026, -65.8% YoY** (IEEFA via Hindu BusinessLine — label the high-base caveat).
  - FY27 RE addition forecast: **~40 GW**, down from 50.9 GW (ICRA).
  - Discom gross debt: **₹7.1 trillion**; cash gap **30–33 paise/unit** FY27 (ICRA).
- **Visualization type:** Circular flow diagram (four stations: curtailment → cash flow → contracting → cost of capital) with the discom debt block sitting underneath as the shared counterparty.
- **Composition notes:** Each station carries one number and one source label. The loop should read clockwise and land back on "slower, costlier next gigawatt." Payoff: a physical constraint becomes a financing condition.
- **Sources:** ET/Bloomberg (6 May 2026), ICRA (Jun 2026), Hindu BusinessLine (28 May 2026), Fortune India/ICRA (7 May 2026).

### Concept 5: The 3–4 GW fix — storage economics that already clear the bar
- **Central insight:** The near-term fix is small relative to the problem: 3–4 GW of two-hour batteries could have absorbed most transmission-constrained curtailment, and stored solar already beats peak procurement costs — what is missing is the regulatory path, not the economics.
- **Data to include:**
  - **3–4 GW two-hour BESS** absorbs most constrained curtailment; **236 GW** plug-and-play headroom at pooling stations (Ember).
  - Operational BESS: **<2 GWh** vs **43.7 GW** ISTS solar at pooling stations (Ember).
  - Delivered stored solar: **~₹7–8/kWh** (LCOS ₹4.0–4.5 + solar ~₹2.5/kWh) vs **₹9–10/kWh** peak procurement (Ember).
  - BESS bid tariffs: **₹10.84 lakh → ₹2.0–3.0 lakh/MW/month** with VGF, Aug 2022 → H1 2026 (ICRA).
- **Visualization type:** Ratio/comparison — one small bar (the 3–4 GW fix) against the large bars of stranded capacity and the price comparison beneath.
- **Composition notes:** Scale honesty is the whole graphic: the fix bar must be drawn to scale against the problem bar. The ₹7–8 vs ₹9–10 comparison sits below as the economic stamp. Payoff: the gap is institutional, not technological.
- **Sources:** Ember (19 May 2026), ICRA (Jun 2026).

## What to avoid

- A "solar is failing" framing — generation costs are near ₹2.5/kWh and the record buildout is real; the constraint is downstream of the panel. Any visual that reads as anti-renewable misrepresents the signal.
- Visualising the ~12 GW / 7%-of-fleet / 21 GW T-GNA figures — the primary Bloomberg article was inaccessible at research time and only secondary outlets corroborate; keep it out of graphics or label it explicitly as reported-unverified.
- The EcoNiti "one full week of national consumption" equivalence — it contradicts NDTV Profit's ~1.5-day figure and government demand data; use the ~1.5-day calibration.
- Treating the RTM near-zero/₹10-cap sessions as a season average — it is session-level evidence; the DAM annual averages (₹3.8 vs ₹4.4) are the hard series.
- Presenting IEEFA's -65.8% investment decline without the high-base caveat — Q1 2025 was inflated by large acquisitions, and IEEFA itself attributes the fall only partly to grid concerns.
- A Germany/UK/China curtailment comparison chart — those figures (8% German solar, £1.4bn UK compensation) come from a single CSEP blog and lack the primary sourcing depth of the Indian data; useful in prose, not as a standalone visual.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with **Concepts 1, 2, 3 and 5**; hold Concept 4 as a strong candidate if a fifth is commissioned.

1. **Concept 1** is the lead — the noon-surplus/evening-shortage paradox is the most counterintuitive verified fact in the artifact.
2. **Concept 2** is the structural backbone — five years of divergence that made the quarter predictable.
3. **Concept 3** is the diagnostic — the Southern zero turns the story from complaint to planning lesson.
4. **Concept 4** (hold) is the deepest but densest; it duplicates carousel content most heavily.
5. **Concept 5** is the hopeful close — the fix is small, cheap and blocked by process, which is exactly the kind of ending Layer 2 pieces should visualise.

If only two are commissioned: **Concept 1** (the paradox) and **Concept 2** (the divergence), because together they carry the ticket's editorial wedge — transmission, not panels, is the constraint — using only hard, dated, primary-sourced numbers.
