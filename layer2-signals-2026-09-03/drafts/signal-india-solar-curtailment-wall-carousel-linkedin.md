# LinkedIn Promo Carousel — India's Solar Curtailment Wall

**Signal:** india-solar-curtailment-wall  
**Format:** Promo carousel for long-form essay  
**Platform:** LinkedIn  
**Slide count:** 8

---

## Slide 1 — The hook

**Kicker:** Grid constraint

**Headline:** India held back 8,133 GWh of solar power in a single quarter — its cheapest electricity, refused at the gate.

**Body:** Between April and June 2026, grid operators ordered solar plants to reduce output because the transmission system could not safely absorb what the panels could produce. The government disclosed the number in Parliament on 28 July.

**Hero stat/quote:** 8,133 GWh of solar curtailed in April–June 2026: 2,417 GWh in April, 3,235 GWh in May, 2,481 GWh in June. — MNRE reply to the Rajya Sabha, citing Grid Controller of India, 28 July 2026

**Design note:** A solar farm behind a closed gate; the number 8,133 GWh set in the largest type on the slide.

**Speaker note:** Names the shift — the transition's constraint has moved from generation economics to grid plumbing.

---

## Slide 2 — The paradox

**Kicker:** Same month

**Headline:** The record curtailment month was also the record demand month.

**Body:** In May, India curtailed 3,235 GWh of solar — while national peak demand hit an all-time high of 270.82 GW on 21 May during a severe heat wave. Surplus at noon, shortage at dusk. The same electrons, separated by a few hours and a few hundred kilometres of wire.

**Hero stat/quote:** Peak power demand: 270.82 GW, all-time record, 21 May 2026; May consumption +11.55% YoY. — The Hindu BusinessLine, 1 June 2026

**Design note:** A daily load curve with a fat midday solar hump spilling over a red "corridor capacity" line, and an evening peak shooting past it.

**Speaker note:** Kills the "low demand" explanation — this is a time-and-place problem, not a demand problem.

---

## Slide 3 — The trend was visible

**Kicker:** It started earlier

**Headline:** The first quarter of 2026 already showed the crack: ~470 GWh curtailed, two-thirds of it for lack of wires.

**Body:** Ember estimates India lost ~470 GWh of renewable energy in January–March 2026, of which ~300 GWh stemmed directly from transmission constraints. The Northern grid lost 178 GWh, the Western grid 122 GWh. The Southern grid lost none — its buildout was better synchronised, and slower. On 30 March alone, 34 GWh went unused.

**Hero stat/quote:** 34 GWh lost on 30 March 2026 — the daily power use of ~5 million urban middle-class households. — Ember, 19 May 2026

**Design note:** A regional bar chart — North 178, West 122, South 0 — the zero bar doing the explanatory work.

**Speaker note:** Establishes this is a trend with a geography, not a one-summer event.

---

## Slide 4 — The buildout, and the thesis

**Kicker:** The race

**Headline:** FY26 added 50.9 GW of renewable capacity — a 77% jump the wires were never scheduled to carry.

**Body:** Developers raced to commission projects before the full waiver on inter-state transmission charges expired on 30 June 2025. A solar plant takes ~18 months to build; a transmission line takes five-plus years. The panels won a race the wires were never entered in. India's ~288 GW renewable fleet — targeting 500 GW by 2030 — can now generate more clean power than the grid can move. The binding constraint has shifted from panels and capital to corridors.

**Hero stat/quote:** RE capacity added: 50.9 GW in FY2026 vs 28.7 GW in FY2025. Transmission targets met: ~80% for five straight years. — ICRA, June 2026; Ember, May 2026

**Design note:** Two horizontal bars — "solar plant: ~18 months" vs "transmission line: 5+ years" — the length mismatch is the graphic. A small line beneath: "generation ≠ delivery."

**Speaker note:** The sequencing error and the essay's thesis in one slide.

---

## Slide 5 — The mechanism

**Kicker:** Must-run, except

**Headline:** "Must-run" solar can still be switched off — and the contract says nobody has to pay for it.

**Body:** The Grid Code lets the National Load Dispatch Centre back down plants to protect grid security, via Emergency TRAS. In April that mechanism curtailed 15.76 GWh of solar per day; on 5 April alone, 80.5 GWh — roughly the daily consumption of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh. Technical curtailment is typically uncompensated under standard PPAs.

**Hero stat/quote:** Solar curtailed under Emergency TRAS: 15.76 GWh/day average; 80.5 GWh on 5 April 2026. — Grid India data, via NDTV Profit, 28 July 2026

**Design note:** A plain contract clause next to a grid operator's console — the legal stack against the physical one.

**Speaker note:** Shows the loss lands on the generator by design of the contract stack.

---

## Slide 6 — The price shape

**Kicker:** The duck curve

**Headline:** On Indian exchanges, solar hours now trade near zero — and evening hours hit the ₹10 ceiling.

**Body:** Real-time prices dropped to nearly zero during daylight on several summer sessions, then climbed to the ₹10/unit regulatory cap after sunset. The annual average hides the split: FY26 day-ahead prices averaged ₹3.8/unit, down from ₹4.4. A merchant solar plant is squeezed twice — fewer units, at lower prices, in its own hours.

**Hero stat/quote:** IEX day-ahead average: ₹3.8/unit in FY26 vs ₹4.4/unit in FY25; RTM near zero by day, ₹10/unit cap after dark. — ICRA, 7 May 2026; NDTV Profit, 28 July 2026

**Design note:** An intraday price curve shaped like a deep valley with a sharp evening spike; label the valley "solar hours."

**Speaker note:** Makes the financial compression visible as a price shape, not an abstraction.

---

## Slide 7 — The loop and the off-takers

**Kicker:** Financial loop

**Headline:** Curtailment → weaker cash flows → thinner auctions → costlier capital for the next gigawatt.

**Body:** ReNew reports up to 15% of solar output curtailed on some days. Auctions fell to 14.7 GW in FY26 from 40.2 GW in FY25; ~45 GW of awarded projects sit stalled awaiting PPA/PSA signatures. And the buyers expected to sign those PPAs — state discoms carrying ₹7.1 trillion of debt, with tariff orders in only 17 of 28 states — are the target of the draft National Electricity Policy 2026: automatic tariff indexation and cost-reflective pricing, aimed at the political economy that created the debt.

**Hero stat/quote:** "You're asked not to generate, and that is losing us power." — Sumant Sinha, CEO, ReNew (Bloomberg / Economic Times, 6 May 2026)

**Design note:** A circular flow diagram with four stations — curtailed electrons, cash flow, auctions, cost of capital — and the ₹7.1 trillion discom debt block sitting underneath as the shared counterparty.

**Speaker note:** How a physical constraint becomes a financing condition, and who sits underneath it.

## Slide 8 — The open question + CTA

**Kicker:** Open question

**Headline:** The next 50 GW is not the question. Whether the grid can absorb the 50 GW already built is.

**Body:** The fix exists on paper: 3–4 GW of two-hour batteries could have absorbed most transmission-constrained curtailment, and stored solar delivers at ₹7–8/unit against ₹9–10 peak procurement costs. India has under 2 GWh of operational storage against 43.7 GW of pooling-station solar. The essay asks what happens to the price of the next gigawatt of capital if absorption lags construction.

**Hero stat/quote:** 3–4 GW of two-hour BESS could absorb most constrained curtailment; operational BESS today: <2 GWh. — Ember, May 2026

**Design note:** A balance scale: "50.9 GW built" vs "<2 GWh of storage." A small, clean link line at the bottom.

**CTA:** Read the full piece on Substack.

**Speaker note:** Leaves the reader with the essay's central tension and one reason to click through.
