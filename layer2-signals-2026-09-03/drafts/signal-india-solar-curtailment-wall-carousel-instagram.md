# Instagram Promo Carousel — India's Solar Curtailment Wall

**Signal ID:** india-solar-curtailment-wall  
**Format:** Instagram promo carousel (promotes the long-form essay)  
**Slide count:** 8

---

## Slide 1 — Hook

- **Kicker:** Grid constraint
- **Headline:** India switched off 8,133 GWh of solar power this summer. On purpose.
- **Body:** The grid could not safely carry what the panels could make. The government said so in Parliament on 28 July.
- **Hero stat or quote:** 8,133 GWh of solar curtailed, April–June 2026. (MNRE / Grid Controller of India, 28 Jul 2026)
- **Design note:** Off-white background, one large black number: 8,133 GWh. A thin sun icon behind a closed-gate line. Nothing else.
- **Speaker note:** Names the shift — the constraint is now the wires.

---

## Slide 2 — The paradox

- **Kicker:** Same month
- **Headline:** Record curtailment. Record demand. Same month.
- **Body:** May saw 3,235 GWh of solar held back — while peak demand hit an all-time high of 270.82 GW in a heat wave. Surplus at noon. Shortage at dusk.
- **Hero stat or quote:** Peak demand: 270.82 GW, 21 May 2026 — an all-time record. (The Hindu BusinessLine, 1 Jun 2026)
- **Design note:** A single load curve: midday solar hump spilling over a red capacity line, evening peak towering. High contrast, minimal labels.
- **Speaker note:** This is a time-and-place problem, not a demand problem.

---

## Slide 3 — The buildout, and the thesis

- **Kicker:** The race
- **Headline:** A solar plant takes ~18 months to build. A transmission line takes five years.
- **Body:** India added 50.9 GW of renewables in FY26 — up 77% — much of it rushed in before the inter-state transmission-charge waiver expired on 30 June 2025. The fleet can now generate more clean power than the grid can move. The binding constraint moved from panels to corridors.
- **Hero stat or quote:** RE capacity added: 50.9 GW in FY2026 vs 28.7 GW in FY2025. (ICRA, Jun 2026)
- **Design note:** Two bars side by side — a short yellow bar (18 months) against a long grey bar (5+ years). The mismatch is the graphic. A small line beneath: "generation ≠ delivery."
- **Speaker note:** The sequencing error and the thesis in one comparison.

---

## Slide 4 — The mechanism

- **Kicker:** Must-run, except
- **Headline:** "Must-run" solar can be ordered off — and no one has to pay for it.
- **Body:** Grid security rules let the operator back down plants via Emergency TRAS. April's average: 15.76 GWh of solar curtailed per day. On 5 April alone: 80.5 GWh.
- **Hero stat or quote:** 80.5 GWh curtailed on 5 April 2026 — roughly the daily use of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh. (Grid India via NDTV Profit, 28 Jul 2026)
- **Design note:** A console-switch motif: a row of solar icons, one flipped to OFF in red. Sparse.
- **Speaker note:** The loss lands on the generator by contract design.

---

## Slide 5 — The price shape

- **Kicker:** The duck curve
- **Headline:** Solar hours now trade near zero. Evening hours hit the ₹10 ceiling.
- **Body:** Real-time exchange prices collapsed toward zero at midday on several summer sessions, then spiked to the ₹10/unit cap after sunset. FY26 day-ahead average: ₹3.8/unit, down from ₹4.4.
- **Hero stat or quote:** IEX day-ahead average: ₹3.8/unit in FY26 vs ₹4.4/unit in FY25. (ICRA, May 2026)
- **Design note:** A deep intraday valley and a sharp evening spike; the valley labelled "solar hours" in small type. One accent colour.
- **Speaker note:** The financial compression made visible as a price shape.

---

## Slide 6 — The loop

- **Kicker:** Financial loop
- **Headline:** Curtailed electrons become costlier capital.
- **Body:** ReNew reports up to 15% of output curtailed on some days. Auctions fell to 14.7 GW in FY26 from 40.2 GW. ~45 GW of awarded projects sit stalled awaiting PPAs. ICRA expects FY27 additions to moderate to ~40 GW.
- **Hero stat or quote:** "You're asked not to generate, and that is losing us power." — Sumant Sinha, CEO, ReNew. (Bloomberg / ET, 6 May 2026)
- **Design note:** A thin circular arrow linking four small labels: curtailment → cash flow → auctions → cost of capital. Generous negative space.
- **Speaker note:** How a physical constraint becomes a financing condition.

---

## Slide 7 — The off-takers

- **Kicker:** The buyers
- **Headline:** The utilities buying this power owe ₹7.1 trillion.
- **Body:** ICRA expects a discom cash gap of 30–33 paise per unit in FY27. The draft National Electricity Policy 2026 proposes automatic tariff revisions and cost-reflective pricing — the sharpest distribution reform push since 2005.
- **Hero stat or quote:** Discom gross debt: ₹7.1 trillion as of March 2025. (ICRA, May 2026)
- **Design note:** A heavy ledger block on the left, a slim policy document on the right, a question mark between them.
- **Speaker note:** Curtailment sits on top of the older discom problem.

---

## Slide 8 — Open Question + CTA

- **Kicker:** Open question
- **Headline:** The next 50 GW is not the question. Absorbing the 50 GW already built is.
- **Body:** 3–4 GW of two-hour batteries could have soaked up most of the constrained curtailment. India has under 2 GWh of operational storage against 43.7 GW of pooling-station solar.
- **Hero stat or quote:** Operational battery storage: <2 GWh. ISTS solar at key pooling stations: 43.7 GW. (Ember, May 2026)
- **Design note:** A balance scale: "50.9 GW built" outweighing "<2 GWh of storage." Small clean line at the bottom: "Read the full essay on Substack."
- **Speaker note:** The essay's central tension, plus the single CTA.

---

## Deck Arc Summary

Slide 1 lands the number; Slide 2 the paradox. Slide 3 carries the sequencing error and the thesis together. Slides 4–5 show the operational and price mechanisms. Slides 6–7 trace the financial loop and the discom layer. Slide 8 closes on the absorption question with the Substack CTA.
