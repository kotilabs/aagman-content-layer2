# Research Artifact: India's Solar Curtailment Wall — 8,133 GWh Held Back in a Single Quarter

Signal ID: `india-solar-curtailment-wall`  
Publication date: 2026-09-03  
Research completed: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Signal restatement

India curtailed 8,133 GWh of solar generation in April–June 2026, the government told the Rajya Sabha on 28 July 2026, citing Grid Controller of India data. It is the first quarter in which the grid operator has openly restricted solar generation at scale, and it arrived immediately after the fastest renewable buildout in the country's history — roughly 50.9 GW of renewable capacity added in FY2026, per ICRA, much of it rushed to commissioning ahead of the expiry of the full inter-state transmission (ISTS) charge waiver on 30 June 2025. The curtailment peaked in May (3,235 GWh), the same month national peak demand set an all-time record of 270.82 GW. The signal: India's energy-transition constraint has moved from the cost of panels and capital to the physical capacity of the grid — and the financial consequences (depressed merchant prices, weaker IPP cash flows, stalled PPAs, costlier capital for the next gigawatt) are only beginning to be priced.

## 2. Verified facts

- India curtailed 8,133 GWh of solar power between April and June 2026 — 2,417 GWh in April, 3,235 GWh in May and 2,481 GWh in June — because transmission infrastructure lagged renewable capacity additions, forcing grid operators to restrict generation to maintain grid security. Source: Minister of State for New and Renewable Energy Shripad Naik, written reply in the Rajya Sabha, citing Grid Controller of India Ltd; reported by Moneycontrol, "Solar curtailment explained | Why India had to hold back 8,133 GWh of solar power in April-June", https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/solar-curtailment-explained-why-india-had-to-hold-back-8-133-gwh-of-solar-power-in-april-june-13986155.html, 28 July 2026; corroborated by Press Trust of India, https://www.ptinews.com/detail/business/Solar-power-curtailment-of-8133-GWh-in-April-June-this-year-Govt/3907912, 28–29 July 2026, and Energetica India, https://www.energetica-india.net/news/india-curtails-8133-gwh-of-solar-power-in-april-june-amid-grid-constraints, 29 July 2026.

- The curtailed volume was equivalent to roughly one-and-a-half days of India's total power consumption during the quarter. Source: NDTV Profit, "India Curtailed 8,133 GWh Of Solar Power Despite Record Summer Electricity Demand", https://www.ndtvprofit.com/business/india-curtailed-8-133-gwh-of-solar-power-despite-record-summer-electricity-demand-11834897, 28 July 2026. (Note: a secondary outlet, EcoNiti, claimed "one full week" — this does not reconcile with government demand data and is treated as wrong; NDTV Profit's ~1.5-day figure is used.)

- India's peak power demand hit an all-time record of 270.82 GW on 21 May 2026, surpassing the previous record of 265.44 GW set a day earlier; May 2026 consumption grew 11.55% YoY to 164.98 billion units. Source: The Hindu BusinessLine, "India's power consumption grows 11.55% to 164.98 billion units in May", https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/indias-power-consumption-grows-1155-to-16498-billion-units-in-may/article71047710.ece, 1 June 2026; The Federal, 22 May 2026, https://thefederal.com/category/news/india-power-demand-heat-wave-scorches-northern-india-244018.

- Curtailment peaked in May (3,235 GWh) — the same month as the demand record — indicating that surplus daytime solar could not be moved to where and when it was needed, even amid record system stress. Source: MNRE reply (Moneycontrol, 28 July 2026) and The Hindu BusinessLine, 1 June 2026.

- During several summer 2026 trading sessions, real-time market (RTM) prices on the Indian Energy Exchange dropped to nearly zero during daylight hours, then climbed to the regulatory ceiling of ₹10 per unit in several post-sunset time blocks. Source: NDTV Profit, 28 July 2026 (URL above).

- Average day-ahead market (DAM) spot tariffs on the Indian Energy Exchange moderated to ₹3.8 per unit in FY2026 from ₹4.4 per unit in FY2025, amid slower demand growth and a large increase in renewable supply. Source: ICRA, reported by Fortune India, "Electricity demand to rise by 5.0-5.5% in 2026-27: ICRA", https://www.fortuneindia.com/economy/electricity-demand-to-rise-by-50-55-in-2026-27-icra/136400, 7 May 2026.

- Spot power prices fell a further 23.3% month-on-month to ₹3.19 per kWh as of 15 June 2026 (from ₹4.16) as monsoon cooling eased peak demand to 237 GW from May's 270 GW. Source: Moneycontrol, "Monsoon showers ease India's peak power demand to 237 GW from 270 in May, drags spot power prices down by 23% on-month", https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/power/monsoon-showers-ease-india-s-peak-power-demand-to-237-gw-from-270-in-may-drags-spot-power-prices-down-by-23-on-month-13951136.html, 16 June 2026.

- Grid India data showed solar curtailment under the Emergency Tertiary Reserve Ancillary Services (TRAS) mechanism averaged 15.76 GWh per day in April 2026; on 5 April 2026 alone, around 80.5 GWh of solar generation was curtailed — roughly the daily consumption of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh. Source: Grid India data, reported by NDTV Profit, 28 July 2026 (URL above).

- Ember estimated total renewable curtailment of ~470 GWh in January–March 2026, of which ~300 GWh stemmed from transmission constraints and ~170 GWh from system inflexibility. The Northern region accounted for 178 GWh, the Western region 122 GWh, and the Southern region none. On 30 March 2026 alone, 34 GWh of clean generation was lost — equivalent to the daily power use of about 5 million urban middle-class households. Source: Ember, "Transmission gaps are beginning to constrain India's rapid renewables integration", https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/transmission-gaps-are-beginning-to-constrain-indias-rapid-renewables-integration/, 19 May 2026; pv magazine India, https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/05/20/india-curtailed-300-gwh-of-renewable-energy-in-q1-2026-due-to-transmission-constraints-says-ember/, 20 May 2026.

- India has met only about 80% of its annual transmission targets over the past five years; the FY2026-27 ISTS target has risen to 25,146 circuit kilometres; one in four major transmission schemes is running a year or more behind schedule; and 20 GW of renewable capacity is likely to face connectivity delays of more than four months in FY2026-27. Source: Ember, 19 May 2026 (URL above); The Hindu BusinessLine, "Grid integration among challenges dragging down investments in India's RE sector in Q1 2026", https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/grid-integration-among-challenges-dragging-down-investments-in-indias-re-sector-in-q1-2026/article71034018.ece, 28 May 2026.

- India added 52,537 MW (52.5 GW) of total power generation capacity between April 2025 and 31 January 2026 — solar ~34,955 MW, wind ~4,613 MW — an 11.22% increase in installed capacity YoY, taking the total to 520.5 GW, with renewables crossing 50.5% of installed capacity. Source: Ministry of Power data, reported by Kotak Neo, "India Adds 52.5 GW Power Capacity in FY26; Solar Leads", https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/india-power-capacity-addition-fy26-52537-mw/, 22 May 2026 (drawing on Tribune India / The Hindu). **Important correction to the ticket framing: 52.5 GW is the all-source capacity addition for April 2025–January 2026, not a solar-only figure. Solar contributed ~35 GW over those ten months.**

- ICRA estimates renewable energy capacity addition at 50.9 GW in FY2026 versus 28.7 GW in FY2025 (+77%), driven partly by a rush to commission projects ahead of the expiry of the full waiver on ISTS charges effective 30 June 2025. Momentum continued with 7.5 GW added in April–May 2026 (2M FY2027), but ICRA expects FY2027 additions to moderate to ~40 GW due to transmission and grid-connectivity delays. Source: ICRA, "Indian Renewable Energy Sector: Transmission infrastructure augmentation and evacuation readiness remain critical to sustain renewable energy capacity addition momentum", June 2026, https://www.icra.in/Rating/DownloadResearchSummaryReport/6989.

- RE tendering moderated sharply: ~14.7 GW auctioned in FY2026 versus 40.2 GW in FY2025 and 47 GW in FY2024, amid delays in signing power sale agreements (PSAs) between nodal agencies and state discoms, which in turn delay PPAs with winning bidders. Source: ICRA, June 2026 (URL above).

- MNRE has proposed a one-time relief package to revive ~45 GW of stalled renewable projects that were awarded but are awaiting PPA/PSA signing, with measures including ISTS charge waivers, deemed RPO/RCO, deemed tariff approvals and incentives for faster PPA execution by discoms. Source: ICRA, June 2026 (URL above).

- Investments in India's RE sector fell 65.8% YoY in January–March 2026 to ~$3.37 billion (₹31,812 crore) from ~$9.84 billion in Q1 2025, and fell 40.5% sequentially from Q4 2025 — a decline IEEFA attributes partly to "increasing caution amid grid integration challenges, curtailment risks, and transmission constraints" (and partly to an exceptionally high Q1 2025 base of large acquisitions and debt financing). Source: IEEFA data, reported by The Hindu BusinessLine, 28 May 2026 (URL above).

- India curtailed ~2.3 TWh of solar generation in calendar 2025 due to grid security concerns, with curtailment rising as renewable penetration increased. Source: IEEFA, reported by The Hindu BusinessLine, 28 May 2026 (URL above).

- ReNew Energy Global Plc is seeing as much as 15% of its solar electricity curtailed on certain days because transmission networks cannot integrate excess supply at midday peaks, according to CEO Sumant Sinha: "The fact is you're asked not to generate, and that is losing us power... The grid build-out has got delayed quite substantially and therefore we are just being told to back down." ReNew, roughly 5% of India's renewable fleet, plans to install ~4 GWh of battery capacity alongside its projects this year. On some days in 2025, nearly 40% of solar output was denied access to the national grid, according to Grid Controller of India Ltd. Source: The Economic Times / Bloomberg, "India's grid bottleneck forces ReNew to scale back power generation, limit losses", https://m.economictimes.com/industry/renewables/indias-grid-bottleneck-forces-renew-energy-to-waste-solar-power-threatening-profits/articleshow/130846399.cms, 6 May 2026.

- Gross debt of state-owned discoms stood at ₹7.1 trillion as of March 2025, down from ₹7.4 trillion as of March 2024; ICRA called such levels "unsustainable" given current revenues and profitability and retained a negative outlook on the distribution segment. Tariff orders for FY2027 had been issued in only 17 of 28 states as of April 2026, with muted hikes; ICRA expects the all-India cash gap to remain at 30–33 paise per unit in FY2027. Source: ICRA, reported by Fortune India, 7 May 2026 (URL above). ICRA earlier flagged elevated regulatory assets and a negative discom outlook in September 2025, when gross debt was ₹7.4 trillion (Fortune India, 16 September 2025, https://www.fortuneindia.com/economy/icra-maintains-negative-outlook-on-state-discoms-flags-elevated-regulatory-assets/126640).

- The Ministry of Power released the Draft National Electricity Policy 2026 for consultation on 21 January 2026 (to replace the 2005 policy). Key proposals: automatic annual tariff revision linked to an index if no tariff order is passed; tariff orders before the start of each financial year and regulatory proceedings concluded within 120 days; tariffs to fully reflect costs "without creating regulatory assets" from FY2026-27; progressive rollback of cross-subsidies so no tariff falls below 50% of average cost of supply (ACoS); possible exemption of discoms from the Universal Service Obligation for consumers with contracted load of 1 MW and above who can self-procure; phasing out distribution monopolies via multiple players, PPPs and listing of state discoms; parity in scheduling and deviation between RE and conventional sources by 2030 or earlier; discouragement of net metering beyond 5 kW; coal repositioned as flexibility/reserves; storage made a standalone market participant. Source: Energy Watch, "Govt floats draft NEP 2026, proposes automatic tariff revisions, signals end to cross-subsidy, Discom monopoly", https://www.energywatch.in/power/govt-floats-draft-nep-2026-proposes-automatic-tariff-revisions-signals-end-to-cross-subsidy-discom-monopoly, 21 January 2026; corroborated by BW Businessworld, https://www.businessworld.in/article/india-pushes-discom-reforms-under-draft-power-policy-2026-598649, 10 May 2026.

- India currently has ~288 GW of renewable energy capacity against a 500 GW non-fossil target for 2030. Source: Moneycontrol, 28 July 2026 (URL above). Separately, IEEFA put RE capacity at 275 GW as of 31 March 2026 (~51% of total installed capacity) — the difference reflects timing and definitional scope (inclusion of large hydro / nuclear), not a contradiction.

- Ember estimates roughly 3–4 GW of two-hour battery storage could have absorbed most of the curtailed, transmission-constrained generation, against 236 GW of plug-and-play BESS headroom already available at major pooling stations. Against ~43.7 GW of ISTS solar capacity at key pooling stations (Northern, Western, Southern regions), India has less than 2 GWh of operational BESS. Major pooling stations include Khavda in the West (~35.2 GW), and Bikaner (~32.3 GW) and Bhadla (~27.6 GW) in the North. India's BESS target is 34.7 GWh by FY2027. Source: Ember, 19 May 2026 (URLs above).

- Levelised cost of storage is ₹4.0–4.5/kWh and solar generation ~₹2.5/kWh, implying a combined delivered cost of stored renewable power of ~₹7–8/kWh including transmission — below the ₹9–10/kWh many states pay for peak power. More than 10–20 GW of demand went unmet on exchanges at the price cap in several instances in March–April 2026. Source: Ember, 19 May 2026 (URLs above).

- BESS bid tariffs discovered in SECI tenders fell from ₹10.84 lakh/MW/month in August 2022 to ₹2.0–3.0 lakh/MW/month (with viability gap funding) in the last six months; ICRA expects a storage requirement of ~50 GW by 2030 with 5–6 hours of storage. Source: ICRA, June 2026 (URL above).

- CSEP notes that curtailment for grid security is standard practice worldwide and embedded in India's Grid Code; PPAs typically distinguish technical curtailment (grid security/transmission constraints — usually uncompensated) from curtailment for other reasons (which may attract compensation or deemed-generation provisions). In H1 2025, Germany curtailed 8% of solar output and 5.3% of wind generation; the UK spent over £1.4 billion compensating curtailed wind farms; China's solar curtailment also rose as additions outpaced evacuation. Source: CSEP, "What does renewable energy curtailment signal for India's power system?", https://csep.org/blog/what-does-renewable-energy-curtailment-signal-for-indias-power-system/, 11 March 2026.

- India to become the world's second-largest solar market by annual installations in 2026 (industry expectation cited by NDTV Profit, 28 July 2026).

## 4. Mechanism

Solar curtailment in India works through three layers, and the signal is that all three are now binding at once.

**Layer 1: Physical evacuation.** Utility-scale solar is concentrated in a few high-resource states — Rajasthan and Gujarat above all — because ISTS charge waivers, land availability and structured connectivity procedures pulled projects there. Generation at pooling stations like Khavda (~35.2 GW), Bikaner (~32.3 GW) and Bhadla (~27.6 GW) now exceeds what the corridors out of those regions can carry during solar hours. Transmission lines take five-plus years of planning, approvals, right-of-way and construction; solar plants take eighteen months. India has met only ~80% of annual transmission targets for five years, and one in four major schemes is a year or more late. The result is a growing fleet of projects on temporary general network access (T-GNA) — commissioned, but without firm evacuation rights — that get backed down first when corridors bind.

**Layer 2: Grid security operations.** Solar and wind have "must-run" status, but must-run is conditional: the Grid Code lets the National Load Dispatch Centre order plants to back down to protect system security, and it does so through Emergency Tertiary Reserve Ancillary Services (TRAS). In April 2026 that mechanism was curtailing an average of 15.76 GWh of solar per day. The operator's legal mandate is grid stability, full stop; compensation for technical curtailment is typically not owed under standard PPAs. So the revenue loss lands on the generator, by design of the contract stack.

**Layer 3: The price shape.** When midday supply floods the market, exchange prices collapse toward zero in solar hours; when the sun sets and solar ramps off, evening scarcity pushes prices to the ₹10/unit ceiling. The annual average masked this: FY26 DAM averaged ₹3.8/unit, down from ₹4.4 — a 14% compression in the merchant price level, concentrated exactly in the hours when solar plants generate. A solar IPP selling into the market thus faces a double squeeze: fewer units delivered (curtailment) at lower prices in its own hours (cannibalisation).

**The financial loop.** Curtailment cuts IPP cash flows → weaker cash flows and PPA uncertainty (PSAs unsigned by discoms, ~45 GW stalled) chill tenders (14.7 GW auctioned in FY26 vs 40.2 GW in FY25) → lenders widen spreads on projects whose debt was underwritten against capacity that physically cannot deliver → the next gigawatt costs more capital → buildout moderates (ICRA: ~40 GW in FY27) even as the 500 GW-by-2030 target requires acceleration. The loop intersects the discom balance-sheet problem: discoms carrying ₹7.1 trillion of gross debt with a 30–33 paise/unit cash gap are the counterparties expected to sign the PPAs, pay for the power, and fund their share of the grid. The draft National Electricity Policy 2026 — automatic tariff indexation, cost-reflective tariffs, cross-subsidy rollback, ≥1 MW consumers freed to self-procure — is an attempt to break the off-taker half of the loop, but it asks states to raise tariffs into exactly the political economy that created the debt.

## 5. Competing interpretations

### Interpretation A: A timing mismatch, not a structural failure.
- **Who is making it:** Government framing (MNRE reply emphasises grid security and ongoing transmission expansion); parts of the transmission-utility sector.
- **Evidence supporting it:** The FY26 commissioning rush was distorted by the 30 June 2025 ISTS waiver expiry — a one-off pull-forward. June curtailment (2,481 GWh) already moderated from May (3,235 GWh) as monsoon cooled demand. Transmission targets, while missed, are large and growing (25,146 ckm in FY27). Germany and the UK absorbed similar growing pains.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** Ember shows curtailment was already material in Jan–Mar 2026, before the summer; 20 GW faces >4-month connectivity delays in FY27; the five-year transmission attainment rate is ~80%.
- **Why it might be wrong:** It treats a planning-model problem as a scheduling problem. If capacity keeps concentrating in ISTS corridors faster than wires arrive, each year's "catch-up" fails the same way.

### Interpretation B: The planning model itself is broken — generation-led transmission must become co-optimised.
- **Who is making it:** Ember (Duttatreya Das), CSEP, and an emerging consensus among grid-policy researchers.
- **Evidence supporting it:** RE is sited where land and waivers are, not where demand is; Southern region had zero transmission-driven curtailment because its buildout was better synchronised; Ember/CSEP both recommend congestion pricing, connectivity auctions, BESS-as-transmission and grid-enhancing technologies.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** The South's clean record partly reflects slower RE growth there; co-optimised planning is slow, institutionally hard, and offers no fix for capacity already stranded.
- **Why it might be wrong:** Correct diagnosis, uncertain politics. Zonal/congestion pricing shifts costs onto high-RE states and developers who already built in good faith under the waiver regime.

### Interpretation C: The curtailment signal is already metastasising into an investment chill.
- **Who is making it:** IEEFA (investment data), ICRA (tendering moderation, FY27 downgrade), ReNew's CEO at company level.
- **Evidence supporting it:** RE investment -65.8% YoY in Q1 2026 (albeit off a high base); auctions down ~63% in FY26; 45 GW stalled awaiting PPA/PSA; merchant price compression; named IPP reporting up to 15% output loss on some days.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** Q1 2025 was an unusually high base of acquisitions and debt financing (IEEFA's own caveat); ICRA's RE sector outlook remains "Stable"; module overcapacity is lowering build costs; the MNRE relief package could unblock the stalled pipeline.
- **Why it might be wrong:** Capital is choosier, not gone. Storage-linked and C&I projects still attract bids; the chill may be selective, concentrated in plain-vanilla merchant-exposed ISTS projects.

### Interpretation D: Some curtailment is economically optimal, and the goal should be managing it, not eliminating it.
- **Who is making it:** CSEP explicitly; system-modelling literature generally.
- **Evidence supporting it:** Building grid capacity for the absolute peak hour is more expensive than curtailing it; efficient RE pathways deliberately oversize generation; Germany/UK/China all run material curtailment in more mature systems.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** Optimal-curtailment logic assumes it is priced and compensated; in India technical curtailment is largely uncompensated, so the cost lands randomly on whichever generator is backed down — a risk-allocation failure, not a system optimum.
- **Why it might be wrong:** At 8,133 GWh in a quarter and ~7% of the fleet reportedly unable to run at full output (Bloomberg, partially verified), India is plausibly well past any "optimal" share; the argument can become a rationalisation for underbuilding.

## 6. Cross-asset implications

**Equities.** Renewable IPPs with merchant exposure and T-GNA connectivity face the direct cash-flow squeeze (ReNew is the named example); developers with firm connectivity and storage-linked PPAs are relatively insulated. Transmission utilities and EPC contractors face the opposite exposure — the FY27 ISTS target of 25,146 ckm is a multi-year order book, with execution risk. Module manufacturers face the ALMM cell mandate (June 2026) and a domestic-price premium, while module overcapacity compresses margins. None of this is a buy/sell signal; it is a map of where the constraint transmits.

**Credit.** Project finance repricing is the central channel: debt underwritten against capacity that cannot evacuate gets re-spread, refinancing risk rises for merchant-exposed plants, and ICRA's FY26 ratio of 65 upgrades to 22 downgrades (driven by ownership/holdco changes, not operations) may not survive a year of 15%-curtailment days. Discom debt (₹7.1 trillion) remains the system's contingent liability; the NEP 2026 tariff reforms, if enacted, are credit-positive for discoms and therefore for every PPA in the stack.

**Rates.** The grid buildout is a multi-year public-private capex cycle; transmission InvITs and green bonds are the financing instruments most directly levered to whether the 25,146 ckm target is funded.

**Commodities.** Curtailed solar is displaced energy: Ember notes the lost clean generation could have reduced reliance on costly gas imports at a time when spot gas prices were roughly double pre-West-Asia-war levels. Thermal's role is being reframed, not retired — coal PLF fell to ~65–66% in FY26, yet draft NEP 2026 and ICRA both reposition coal as flexibility and reserves; 6 GW of thermal addition is expected in FY27. Silver's rally feeding N-type module prices (8–9¢/W in Nov 2025 → 11–12¢/W in Feb 2026) is a second-order cost channel.

**FX.** Marginal: higher gas imports from unusable solar marginally worsen the energy import bill; a slower RE buildout extends the fossil import era at the margin.

**Market structure / derivatives.** The power exchanges are repricing time itself: near-zero solar-hour RTM prices and ₹10-capped evening blocks are the Indian duck curve made visible. This price shape is the business case for storage (arbitrage spread), for demand flexibility (CSEP's "missing ingredient"), and for BESS-as-transmission — and it is why plain-vanilla solar PPAs are getting harder to sign.

## 7. Historical analogues or structural memory

### Analogue 1: California's duck curve, late 2010s.
- **Episode:** CAISO began routinely curtailing midday solar in the late 2010s as utility-scale additions outpaced transmission and evening ramping capability; solar-hour wholesale prices sagged while evening ramps steepened — the same price signature now visible on IEX.
- **Key similarity:** A build-first, wire-later sequencing that produced curtailment before it produced a market redesign; storage and demand response eventually became the marginal solution.
- **Key difference:** California's curtailment was largely economic and compensated through market settlement; India's is mostly technical, under emergency grid-security authority, and largely uncompensated — a harder landing for the same physics.
- **Use for perspective:** It took California roughly a decade of storage buildout and market redesign to bend its duck curve. Sequencing errors of this kind are correctable but slow — useful for calibrating how quickly India's loop can resolve, not for predicting that it will resolve the same way.

### Analogue 2: India's own grid discipline history, post-July 2012.
- **Episode:** The 30–31 July 2012 blackouts — the largest in history by people affected — hardened the operating doctrine of the Indian grid: frequency discipline, strict deviation settlement, and operator authority to back down generation.
- **Key similarity:** Today's Emergency TRAS curtailment is that doctrine operating as designed — grid security first, economics second.
- **Key difference:** In 2012 the binding constraint was generation shortfall and indiscipline; in 2026 it is surplus clean energy and transmission scarcity — the mirror-image problem.
- **Use for perspective:** It explains why the operator's behaviour is non-negotiable and why the fix must come from planning, markets and storage rather than from asking the load despatch centre to be braver. Framing only; the 2012 system is not the 2026 system.

## 8. What is known, unknown, and unknowable

### Known
- 8,133 GWh of solar was curtailed in April–June 2026, per the government, citing Grid Controller of India; the monthly split and the grid-security rationale are official.
- Q1 2026 (Jan–Mar) already saw ~470 GWh of total RE curtailment, ~300 GWh transmission-driven, concentrated in the Northern and Western grids (Ember).
- FY26 renewable additions hit ~50.9 GW (ICRA) — a record, distorted by the ISTS waiver expiry; FY27 is expected to moderate to ~40 GW.
- Auction volumes, PPA signing, RE investment flows, merchant prices and at least one named IPP's output all show measurable stress in H1 2026.
- Discom gross debt is ₹7.1 trillion with a persistent 30–33 paise/unit cash gap; the draft NEP 2026 proposes the sharpest distribution reform since 2005.

### Unknown
- How much of the 8,133 GWh was transmission congestion versus local over-supply, equipment outage, or voltage constraint — the government data does not break curtailment down by cause or location. (CSEP calls cause-level transparency the missing first step.)
- Whether the MNRE relief package will actually unlock the ~45 GW stalled pipeline, and on what terms.
- Whether states will implement NEP 2026's automatic tariff indexation — the political economy that produced ₹7.1 trillion of discom debt has not changed.
- How much of the ~12 GW / ~7%-of-fleet figure (Bloomberg) is T-GNA-temporary versus structurally stranded.
- The FY27 transmission execution rate — will the 25,146 ckm target break the ~80% attainment pattern?

### Unknowable
- The "right" level of curtailment for India's system — the economic optimum depends on storage costs, demand growth and grid technology that are all moving.
- Whether the investment chill is a pause or a turn — it depends on policy execution that has not happened yet.
- The pace at which the evening-peak ₹10-cap blocks and near-zero solar-hour blocks will force a market redesign; price signals do not come with dates.

## 9. Open questions

1. Does the MNRE one-time relief package unlock the ~45 GW of awarded-but-unsigned projects — and does it merely defer the transmission constraint rather than resolve it?
2. Will any major state actually implement NEP 2026's automatic tariff revision, or does the draft join the long shelf of unenforced discom reforms?
3. Does India adopt a BESS-as-transmission procurement mechanism (Ember's two regulatory paths) before the FY27 connectivity delays strand another cohort of projects?
4. At what curtailment share does project-finance repricing show up in bankable PPA terms — and is 15% curtailment days (ReNew's experience) already past it?
5. Does the Southern region's zero transmission-curtailment record hold as its own RE buildout accelerates — i.e., is synchronised planning portable, or was it luck?
