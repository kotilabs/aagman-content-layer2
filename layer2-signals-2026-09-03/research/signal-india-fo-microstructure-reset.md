# Research Artifact: India's F&O Microstructure Reset — What a Deliberate Halving of the World's Largest Retail Options Flow Does to Price Formation

Signal ID: `india-fo-microstructure-reset`  
Publication date: 2026-09-03  
Research completed: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Signal restatement

Two years into SEBI's deliberate reset of the equity derivatives market, the numbers now show the reset working — and its costs surfacing. Options contract volumes on NSE and BSE fell roughly 51% in FY26 (SEBI annual report 2025-26, via ET, 6 August 2026), and NSE's August 2026 equity-derivatives turnover came in at ₹34.48 lakh crore, the lowest monthly print since November 2023 (ET, 2 September 2026). In August, a second structural change went live: the Closing Auction Session (CAS), which replaced the last-30-minute VWAP closing price with a 20-minute call auction for F&O stocks — and brokers report squaring off positions before the window, draining late-day derivatives activity further. The signal is not that speculation fell; that was the design. The signal is that the second-order effects — on cash-market liquidity, event-day price discovery, exchange economics, and the destination of displaced retail risk appetite — are now measurable, and nobody has mapped them. BSE briefly overtook NSE in notional F&O turnover in April 2026 on fee pricing; a subset of traders is migrating to US futures via offshore prop firms; and the first full-year SEBI profitability analysis under the new regime (FY26) shows fewer losers losing slightly more each.

## 4. Mechanism

**What the options flow was doing.** India's equity-options complex was not just a casino bolted onto the stock market; it was part of the stock market's plumbing. Three channels matter:

1. **Delta-hedging as a cash-market liquidity subsidy.** Every option sold by a market maker or prop desk creates a hedge in the underlying. Millions of small retail option tickets aggregated into dealer books that had to buy and sell Nifty/Sensex constituents and index futures all day to stay neutral. That flow was price-insensitive, continuous, and deep — a standing bid-offer engine in the cash market that existed because the options tail wagged it. Halve the options volume and you halve the hedging flow that rode on it.

2. **Event-day price discovery.** Options are the cheapest way to express a view on a discrete event — an RBI policy, an election result, an earnings surprise. Expiry-day and weekly contracts concentrated that expression: 70% of options turnover used to happen on expiry day (FY25). That concentration meant that around events, the fastest price discovery happened in the options tape and arbitraged into cash. With expiry-day share down to 59%, fewer expiries, and upfront premiums removing intraday leverage, the fastest market is now slower — information takes longer to get into prices, and more of it has to route through the cash market directly.

3. **Exchange and broker economics.** The duopoly's fee pools were built on retail ticket volume. When the regulator tripled the ticket size and capped expiries, ticket count collapsed — and because BSE priced futures at zero and options at roughly one-seventh of NSE's rate, the remaining flow was contestable. April's flip (BSE 55.4% of notional turnover) shows the economics being rewritten by price, not incumbency. NSE still holds two-thirds of options premium — the real money — but premium pools shrink when premium buyers leave.

**What CAS added in August.** CAS is a separate, defensible reform: replace a manipulable 30-minute VWAP close with a call auction that produces one hard closing price (which feeds NAVs, index levels, and derivatives settlement). But its side effect is behavioural. Arbitrageurs, scalpers and prop desks live on thin margins and cannot risk a 20-minute opaque window where the official close can gap away from the last traded price. They respond the way thin-margin traders always respond to unpriceable risk — they leave early. Positions get squared by 3:05–3:10, the last hour of continuous trading thins out, delta-hedging across cash and derivatives stops earlier, and a daily micro-loss of volume compounds into a monthly turnover number at a 21-month low. SEBI's chairman has said the mechanism stays; so the adaptation is the market's problem to solve.

**The displacement ledger.** SEBI's policy logic was that suppressed speculation would convert into ownership — delivery ratios did rise to ~30%, and SIPs are at record levels. But the FY26 profitability data complicates the victory: fewer traders, losing slightly more each, with the surviving pool dominated by larger accounts (23% of traders run 92% of turnover). And a visible subset of the displaced appetite is being marketed offshore — US micro futures via prop-firm evaluation accounts, in a FEMA grey zone. Regulation removed the flow from the domestic tape; whether it retired the risk or exported it is genuinely unresolved.

## 5. Competing interpretations

### Interpretation A: The reset is working as designed — speculation down, ownership up.
- **Who is making it:** SEBI, in its annual report and the FY26 profitability release; the finance ministry's earlier framing of retail F&O as "gambling instincts."
- **Evidence supporting it:** Options volumes -51% in FY26; expiry-day share down to 59% from 70%; delivery-to-traded ratio up to ~30%; aggregate individual net losses down 18%; loss-making share down to 87.7%; SIPs at record monthly levels.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** Average loss per remaining trader rose 2%; 4.6 million exited rather than converted; the survivor pool is more concentrated, not healthier; SIP stoppage ratio above 100% in March–April 2026.
- **Why it might be wrong:** It counts the disappearance of the trader as the disappearance of the risk appetite. If the appetite moved offshore or into other instruments, the protection was geographic, not behavioural.

### Interpretation B: The reset traded retail harm for market quality, and the August turnover low is transition pain, not damage.
- **Who is making it:** Exchange and market-structure specialists defending CAS; SEBI chairman Pandey ("CAS is here to stay").
- **Evidence supporting it:** Closing auctions are global standard practice; VWAP closes were demonstrably manipulable (the Jane Street order is built on expiry/close-window manipulation); early-squaring is an adaptation cost that fades as participants learn the auction; non-F&O stocks still trade normally, capping the blast radius.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** The behaviour change is not confined to the auction window — brokers report closing positions 20+ minutes before CAS even starts, thinning the whole last hour; arbitrage and delta-hedging withdrawn from the close are not obviously replaced.
- **Why it might be wrong:** It assumes the withdrawn flow was dispensable. If options-linked hedging flow was a real liquidity subsidy to the cash market, its removal shows up later, in event-day gaps and wider effective spreads, not in a turnover statistic.

### Interpretation C: The reset institutionalized the market — it didn't fix retail outcomes, it removed retail from the table.
- **Who is making it:** Retail-trading commentators; the FY26 SEBI data read bearishly; the prop-firm industry's marketing pitch.
- **Evidence supporting it:** 87.7% still lose; average losses per trader rose; the 23% of traders with >₹1 lakh margin now run 92% of turnover; top-10 props take 75% of prop profits and 85% of options activity; 99% of FPI/prop profits involve algos. The market didn't get fairer — it got more expensive to enter, and the sophisticated end barely noticed (prop profits -3% while FPI profits -55%).
- **Evidence contradicting it:** Aggregate harm fell; participation by the smallest traders (the sub-₹10,000 turnover cohort) shrank most, which is precisely the cohort the rules targeted; concentration among survivors may simply reflect that scale always won.
- **Why it might be wrong:** It treats access as the virtue. If the pre-reset market was functioning as a transfer from 9-in-10 retail losers to algo desks — ₹1.8 lakh crore over FY22–24 — then shrinking access is the fix, not the bug, even if the survivors' statistics look uglier.

### Interpretation D: The risk was exported, not retired.
- **Who is making it:** The Tradeify/US-prop-firm marketing machine, with a commercial interest in the narrative; implicit in SEBI's own monitoring of offshore channels.
- **Evidence supporting it:** Documented, actively marketed pipeline from SEBI's rule changes to US micro futures via evaluation-fee prop accounts; the trader psychology (small capital, high leverage, daily action) is unchanged by geography.
- **Evidence contradicting it:** No independent measurement of the offshore flow's size exists; prop accounts are simulated with capped downside (only the evaluation fee), which is categorically less ruinous than leveraged options; SIP data suggests much of the money did go domestic and boring.
- **Why it might be wrong:** Vendor marketing is not flow data. The offshore migration may be a loud niche rather than a material leak.

## 6. Cross-asset implications

**Equities (cash market).** The core question of this signal: how much of the cash market's late-day and event-day liquidity was options-hedging flow in disguise. Watch effective spreads and depth in the final hour of continuous trading under CAS, and price gaps on event days (RBI policy, earnings) where options used to intermediate the information. SEBI's own data shows delivery ratios rising to ~30% — a composition shift toward ownership even as speculative churn falls.

**Equities (index level).** The turnover collapse is happening into a market where FIIs returned in August (₹29,631 crore, per the parallel digest signal) and the Nifty trades ~21x earnings. A thin derivatives tape into a flow-driven rally means less hedged positioning — sharper air pockets if the flow reverses.

**Derivatives industry economics.** NSE options-fee growth already slowed from +92% to +2% (year through April 2025); BSE bought share with price; discount brokers (Angel One) took a ~49% profit hit a year ago. The FY26 SEBI data shows the pain migrating: FPI derivatives profits -55%, mutual funds -54%, props only -3%. The industry's profit pool is concentrating in a handful of algo prop desks.

**FX / capital account.** The Tradeify-style offshore channel, if material, is a small but novel leak: retail risk capital remitted under LRS to trade US futures. Not macro-significant at current scale, but it reverses the direction regulators intended — domestic savings were supposed to move into domestic ownership, not offshore leverage.

**Rates / policy.** None direct. Indirect: a household-savings channel redirected from derivatives losses (~₹1.05 lakh crore/yr at FY25 pace) toward SIPs and delivery ownership is a real, if slow, change in the composition of domestic demand for equities — supportive of the structural domestic-bid thesis this research program has tracked (`dii-wall-fii-exodus`, `india-vix-compression`).

## 7. Historical analogues or structural memory

### Analogue 1: Korea, 2012 — the KOSPI 200 options de-rating.
- **Episode:** KOSPI 200 options were the world's most-traded derivatives contract — 43.4% of global equity index futures-and-options volume in 2011. In 2012, Korean authorities, worried about retail speculation, raised the contract multiplier fivefold (KRW 100,000 → 500,000, June 2012). Contract volume fell from ~1.58 billion in 2012 to ~580 million in 2013 — roughly a 63% collapse in a year. Korea later cut the multiplier back (March 2017) after the market's diminished state became its own problem.
- **Key similarity:** The world's largest retail options market, deliberately shrunk by contract-size inflation. India copied the playbook almost exactly (₹5–10 lakh → ₹15–20 lakh band).
- **Key difference:** Korea's speculation was domestic-only and had no offshore prop-firm escape hatch; India's displaced traders have US micro futures one marketing funnel away. Korea also had no simultaneous close-of-day microstructure reform compounding the volume loss.
- **Why it is useful:** It shows the end-state is stable but not free — Korea's options market never regained its old dominance, and liquidity migrated permanently to futures. Perspective, not prediction. (Sources: Jung 2013 MIT thesis; Eurex/KRX note via Institutional Asset Manager; Korean derivatives-journal volume data — see source list; some figures snippet-level.)

### Analogue 2: China, 2015 — the CSI 300 futures clampdown.
- **Episode:** After the 2015 crash, Chinese authorities throttled stock-index futures with punitive margins and position limits; volumes collapsed and the academic literature treats it as a natural experiment. The findings that matter here: index futures normally account for ~70% of price discovery in trending markets, and the post-restriction market became more sensitive to news short-term but with weakened long-run price discovery as liquidity drained (He et al. 2020; Su 2023).
- **Key similarity:** A regulator deliberately removing derivatives volume and discovering that price discovery was partly living in the derivatives tape.
- **Key difference:** China's restrictions were an emergency brake in a crisis, later partially reversed; India's are a considered, multi-year framework with no stated intention of reversal.
- **Why it is useful:** It quantifies the hidden cost — price discovery is a measurable casualty of throttled derivatives, which is exactly what India's August turnover number invites us to watch for.

## 8. What is known, unknown, and unknowable

### Known
- Options volumes fell ~51% in FY26; NSE August 2026 equity-derivatives turnover is the lowest since November 2023.
- CAS is live (3 August 2026), is permanent per the SEBI chairman, and has changed late-day behaviour: positions squared by 3:05–3:10, hedging activity withdrawn from the close.
- The rule set (contract sizes, one weekly expiry, upfront premiums, intraday monitoring, STT hike, ELM) is fully documented with dates.
- Individual participation fell for the first time since FY16; aggregate retail losses fell 18%; average loss per remaining trader rose 2%.
- The survivor side is concentrated: top-10 props, ~75% of prop profits, ~85% of options activity.
- BSE overtook NSE in notional F&O turnover in April 2026 on fee pricing; NSE retains ~66% of premium turnover.
- SIP flows are at record levels; SIP stoppage ratios are also elevated.

### Unknown
- How much of the August decline is CAS adaptation (transient) versus the structural reset (permanent) — September and October prints will start to separate the two.
- The size of the offshore migration into US futures prop accounts; no independent measurement exists, only vendor marketing.
- Whether cash-market event-day liquidity has measurably degraded — no public study yet ties the options decline to spread/depth deterioration around events.
- Whether BSE's April lead was a share shift or a fee-arbitrage artifact (NSE attributed part of it to expiry-day calendar quirks).
- The final shape of the CAS framework for non-F&O stocks (phase 2 timing).

### Unknowable
- The counterfactual: what retail losses, market volatility, and household-savings allocation would have looked like in FY26 without the reset.
- Whether the displaced speculative appetite is genuinely extinguished or merely dormant until the next bull leg — behaviour at the next market top, not now, will answer this.
- The point at which thinning derivatives liquidity would begin to impair the cash market enough to force SEBI to recalibrate — that threshold is political as much as technical.

---

## 9. Open questions

1. Does September turnover confirm August as the new baseline, or does late-day activity migrate back as participants learn the CAS window?
2. Where did the 4.6 million who exited in FY26 actually go — SIPs and delivery ownership, as the policy intended, or offshore leverage?
3. Do event days (RBI policy, state elections, large earnings) now show wider gaps and slower incorporation, measurably, versus the pre-reset regime?
4. Does BSE's fee-priced share lead force NSE to cut derivatives fees — turning a regulatory volume shock into an exchange price war?
5. Will SEBI act on the offshore prop-firm channel, and under what authority, given the FEMA/LRS grey zone the vendors themselves acknowledge?
