## Exemplar 1 (score 417, creator baseline = 100)

Milky Mist does not sell milk. Not even a single packet. They buy 400 million liters milk every year and convert all of it into paneer, cheese, curd and ice cream. Realized per liter of procured milk is ₹77.79. Highest among peers.

Milky Mist did not own the brand name. It was owned by the founder till last year who sold it to the company for ₹8 crore.

Biggest shareholder of Milky Mist is not the founder himself. It's his wife who holds 49.48%. Founder holds 39.38% only.

Milky Mist has 1317 permanent employees and their FY26 revenue was ₹3138 crore. That's more than ₹2.4 crore revenue per employee. Even IT companies or Jio can't beat this number.

They barely advertise. FY24 ad budget was ₹20.6 crore which is just 1% of their revenue. They have a different and unique style of marketing.

Milky Mist owns 41,459 refrigerators. They have given these to kirana stores. Whenever you visit a shop, you see their name. Free marketing.

ROE in FY26 was 32%. You will see this number, you will go crazy. But this is funded by debt. ROCE is only 11.73%. They have borrowed ₹1672 crore against equity of ₹463 crore. Debt to Equity is 3.61x.

100 days before the IPO opened today, Temasek acquired 5% of Milky Mist at roughly 10000 cr valuation. IPO is valued at the same price.

## Exemplar 2 (score 281, creator baseline = 100)

1992 → A 16-year-old drops out of school to save his family's failing business.
2026→ Same business files for a ₹2,000 crore IPO


This is Sathish Kumar's story. 

His family had already lost a power loom business, and their dairy business was also struggling. 

In 1992, his father decided to shut it down. But his son dropped out of school and took over instead.

He realized selling raw milk was a dead-end business. It spoiled quickly, profits were low, and prices were controlled. So, he decided to stop selling milk and start making paneer instead.

And from there, he just kept taking bigger bets

1/ Product diversification → 
They kept turning the same milk into higher-value products like cheese, curd, butter, ghee, yogurt, and ice cream. Today, Milky Mist is one of India's biggest paneer brands and the third-largest cheese brand.

2/ Distribution →
They started placing Milky Mist fridges in thousands of shops. Once the fridge was there, it became much easier to stock paneer, cheese, curd, ghee, and ice cream.

3/ Farmers → 
Over 67,000 of them supply them. the company supports them with vet care, cattle feed, and even financial help during tough times. That kind of trust and loyalty takes years to build.

4/ Hiring →
He didn't try to crack national expansion alone. he brought in K. Rathnam, someone who'd already helped scale Amul. 

Result? 
FY26 revenue crossed ₹3,200 crore and Temasek stepped in just before the IPO at close to a ₹9,000 crore valuation. 

From a business his father was ready to shut, to one investor are now lining up to buy into.

Great to see an Indian business reach these heights. hopefully we see many more like it in the coming decades :)

## Exemplar 3 (score 246, creator baseline = 100)

Fun facts: 

Back in 2017, the largest small-cap company in India was valued at just about ₹8,500 crore.
Fast forward to 2025, that number stands at ₹32274 crore (251st market cap company today) — a 3.5x in just eight years!

The largest midcap company was ₹29000 crore, today the smallest midcap (250th) company is ₹32482 crores market cap.

But here’s where it gets even more interesting…

In 2017, there were only 112 companies with a market cap above ₹5,000 crore.
Today, that number has moved to 693.

The 500th market cap company in India is valued at around ₹10900crore, yes more than USD1bn market cap. 

Behind these numbers lies a powerful story — of India’s entrepreneurial energy, economic momentum, and deepening capital markets.

However, as the market expands and more companies enter the big league, the real skill will lie in bottom-up stock selection — identifying sustainable compounders while avoiding big landmines that can destroy capital.

It’s not just the large caps leading the charge — the small and mid-sized champions are increasingly becoming the growth engines of the economy.

We are witnessing the rise of the ₹5,000 crore club — and it’s only getting bigger!
