## Exemplar 1 (score 562, creator baseline = 100)

If you're a 9-5 employee, you should know this about your CTC!!


Most people know EPF gets deducted from their salary.
Very few know it also comes with an insurance benefit and a pension benefit.

Here's how it works.

Every month you contribute 12% of your basic salary to EPF.
Your employer also contributes 12%.

And yes, the employer contribution is part of your CTC too.

Your 12% goes entirely into EPF where it currently earns 8.25% p.a. interest.

But your employer's contribution gets split:
→ 3.67% goes to EPF
→ 8.33% goes to EPS (If your starting base salary was less than 15k per month)

And max ₹75 goes to EDLI (Employee Deposit Linked Insurance)

Now what do you actually get from these?

1/ EDLI -
God forbid something happens to you while you're employed. Your nominee can receive an insurance payout of ₹2.5 - ₹7 lakh under EDLI. 
Most employees don't even know this benefit exists. To claim this make sure you have added nominee for this. 

2/ EPS
It's a pension scheme. Once you turn 58 you become eligible for a monthly pension based on your years of service. But you are eligible only if your basic salary was below ₹15,000 when you first became an EPF member.

If your basic salary is above ₹15,000 them the full employer contribution (8.33% + 3.67%) is credited to your EPF.

And this is where many employees should pay attention.

Sometimes due to payroll or technical errors, companies deduct EPS deductions even when the employee is not eligible.
This can create issues later when you try to withdraw. 

So, take 2 minutes today. Check your EPF passbook and understand where your employer contribution is actually going.

## Exemplar 2 (score 434, creator baseline = 100)

Someone shared this on reddit, and every Indian should know this 


They shared that after his father passed away it took their family 4 months just to figure out his bank accounts investments and policies.

They found FDs by accident, insurance details through an agent’s call, multiple bank visits paperwork, affidavits, succession papers

His father had invested properly the family just didn’t know where everything was.

That’s why every earning person should have one simple “In Case of Death” folder.

Sounds uncomfortable I know but if something happens to you your family should know how to access your finances.

And this is how should you organize this folder? 

1/ Personal documents
Aadhaar, PAN, passport, birth certificate, marriage certificate etc

2/ Bank + investment details
Bank accounts, FDs, mutual funds, stocks, demat accounts, SIPs, PPF, EPF NPS etc

3/ Insurance details
Health insurance, life, term insurance - policy docs, nominee details, agent's number. 

4/ Loans + liabilities
Home loan, car loan - anything pending, their insurance paper, if any.

5/ Property and legal documents
Registry, sale deed, society documents, rental agreement or any other document like will. 

6/ Important contacts
CA lawyer financial advisor insurance agent relationship manager

7/ Digital access
Email IDs, cloud storage, subscriptions and instructions on password access
Make sure you have added nominee in all investments, and most importantly, tell your family where this folder exists.
Because in India even accessing your own family member’s money after death can become a long paperwork-heavy process if things are not organized properly.

## Exemplar 3 (score 405, creator baseline = 100)

I cleared CA at 20.

I cleared CFA at 21.

I cleared ACCA at 22.

And one thing that helped me clear these exams early was this:

I did not just study hard.

I studied in a way that helped me score.

There is a big difference between studying for 12 hours and studying effectively for 12 hours.

In the last few weeks before any exam, I followed a few rules very strictly.

First, I removed distractions completely.

Phone away.

Social media off.

Random conversations avoided.

No unnecessary negativity before exams.

Your mind has limited bandwidth.

If you fill it with reels, notifications, messages and other people’s opinions, it will not perform at its best in the exam hall.

Second, I gave mock tests before exams.

Mock tests tell you what books cannot.

Which question takes more time.

Where you make silly mistakes.

Which topics are weak.

Whether you can complete the paper within time.

Third, I focused on completing the full paper.

Many students know the answer.

But they lose marks because they spend 20 minutes on a 5-mark question and then rush the last 30 marks.

In exams, time management is as important as knowledge.

Allocate time according to marks.

Fourth, I never carried unclear concepts into the exam hall.

Ask your teacher.

Discuss with friends.

Watch a good explanation.

But don’t ignore confusion.

And lastly, don’t study for society.

People will talk if you pass.

People will talk if you fail.

Your job is to give your 100%, make your parents proud, and keep moving forward.

One result cannot decide your future.

But one disciplined attempt can change your confidence forever.

So give this attempt everything you have.

Study effectively.

Protect your focus.

Attempt the full paper.

And walk out of the exam hall knowing that you did justice to your dreams.
