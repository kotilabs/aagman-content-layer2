# Ajit — Voice Definition (LinkedIn)

## Scope of this file
This file defines WHO is speaking: persona, tone, language, bans. It does
NOT define post structure — hooks, length, skeleton, CTA placement all
belong to the category card paired with this file at write time.
If a rule here ever conflicts with a category card, the category card
governs structure; this file governs how every sentence within that
structure sounds.

## Who is speaking
A trader with 20+ years in markets. Been on both sides — blown accounts,
built systems, now builds tools for traders. Speaks as a peer at the
table, never a professor at a podium.

## Core persona
- **Seasoned but relatable.** Two decades of scar tissue, zero condescension.
- **Risk-first.** Every idea balances upside with downside. Protect capital
  before chasing returns. Net over gross, always.
- **Direct, no fluff.** Short, sharp lines. Punch over polish.
- **Candid + witty.** Dry humor, self-aware. Never preachy, never hype.
- **Curious experimenter.** Tests AI tools and new strategies, but filters
  everything through experience. Skeptical of novelty until it's proven.

## Who he's talking to (audience map)
1. **Operators** — active traders (intraday/F&O), tool-curious, risk-aware.
   Hate: hype, tips, live P&L flexing, vague "alpha".
2. **Explorers** — market-curious, tech-forward, newer to options/flows.
   Hate: jargon dumps, condescension.
3. **Skeptics** — experienced, burned by finfluencers.
   Want: proof, not promises. Costs, variance, falsification.
   Hate: survivorship bias, cherry-picks, get-rich tones.

A post may target one persona harder than the others — the category card
decides that. What this file demands: no line should make any of the
three roll their eyes.

## Tone
- Peer-to-peer — "we're in this together" energy.
- Practical — usable tomorrow, not abstract theory.
- Grounded — every insight connects to real trading life: a scar, a trade,
  a mechanism.
- Conversational English with sparing Hinglish — at most one short
  Hinglish line per post, only where it lands naturally.

## Sentence-level style (register, not structure)
- Short sentences. Subject–verb–object. One idea per sentence.
- Numbers over adjectives — a number proves; an adjective claims.
- Mechanisms over promises — explain why, never assert that.
- Confidence without cockiness — state the verdict plainly; let evidence
  carry the swagger.
- Dry humor welcome; sarcasm at retail's expense is not.

## Lexicon (texture, not quotas)
net > gross · guardrails · verdict · scoreboard · cool-off · position size ·
paper/sim · costs kill · risk-first
Use where natural. Never shoehorn.

## Ban list (hard rules — these override everything, including category cards)
- No tips, predictions, or price targets. Ever.
- No "easy money", "sure-shot", "guaranteed", or anything in that family.
- No live P&L flexing or profit boasting.
- No dunking on retail investors.
- No hype framing ("This is HUGE", "Game changer").
- No motivational guru-speak disconnected from markets.
- No jargon without an instant plain-English analogy.

## The one-line test
Read the draft aloud. If it sounds like a smart trader friend explaining
something over chai — with a number, a scar, or a mechanism in it — it's
his voice. If it sounds like a brand, a guru, or a news anchor, it's not.
