## Structure (story-led, text-driven)

The deck is narrative journalism in slides. There is no per-slide scaffolding — no kicker, no headline, no hero stat, no speaker note, no design note. Each slide is one flowing paragraph of prose (roughly 60–90 words), and the story carries the momentum.

- Slide count: tight — usually 7–8. The essay holds the depth; the deck holds the pull.
- Cover slide: a full-sentence headline with a turn in it, plus a two-line subhead that states the central tension flatly. Curiosity, never clickbait. (Reference shape: "India's rich are discovering lawsuits can be an asset class. The regulator, not yet.")
- Opening story slide: lead with a concrete moment — a date, a decision, a place, an institution in action. Never invent a named person or a quote; anchor on real actors and events from the essay.
- Then: scale/context → the mechanism (how it actually works) → the numbers that prove it.
- The turn: one sentence, alone on its slide. The fact or realization the whole story pivots on.
- Final slide: the open question the essay takes up, plus a single plain pointer to the full piece on Substack ("link in bio" or equivalent). The pointer previews what the essay adds — never a bare "read more."

## Voice rules

Lead with the concrete, follow with the abstract. "Indonesia cut its quota 30%" before "states now set volume."
Numbers carry the story. Specific, sourced inline, never rounded into vagueness. If a slide has no number, it has a named mechanism instead (a permit, a quota, a tariff, a law).
Write like a person: no binary contrasts ("not X, it's Y"), no dramatic one-line fragments for effect, no rhetorical setups, no pull-quote-bait lines. The story is the interesting thing.
No em dashes anywhere in slide copy. No emojis. No exclamation marks.
Cut every transitional phrase ("Furthermore," "It's worth noting"). Slides are discontinuous; the reader supplies the connective tissue.
No adjectives where a number will do. No massive, huge, explosive, game-changing.
Vary paragraph weight: a dense evidence slide earns a shorter one after it. Rhythm matters.
Forbidden: rhetorical questions mid-deck, "imagine if…", "the future of X," hype openers, engagement-bait, and any buy/sell framing, price target, urgency, or return promise (SEBI RIA compliance).

## Output format

For each slide: the slide number and the prose paragraph. The cover slide gets the headline sentence plus the subhead. That is all — no production metadata in the draft.

Last slide (with companion essay): the essay's open question plus the single Substack pointer.
