# Markets Review: Why Did the US Join the Yen Intervention?

**Review date:** 2026-08-12
**Surfaces reviewed:** blog, promo carousel, infographic concepts.
**Surfaces not produced:** thread and LinkedIn carousel — noted, those sections skipped.

Overall assessment up front: the factual spine is unusually well disciplined — nearly every number traces cleanly to the research artifact, estimates are mostly labeled as estimates, and the two low-credibility claims (South Korea, the $59.7bn FIMA draw) were correctly excluded everywhere. The systemic problem is different: all three surfaces assert the bond-market-self-defense interpretation as settled fact, while the research artifact explicitly holds it as the leading of four competing readings with named counterarguments. That is the thread running through most of the findings below.

## Independent gaps

- **Gap:** The FIMA-sufficiency objection (the research's own strongest counter to the lead thesis) is engaged by no surface.
  - Why it matters: A skeptical reader's first question is "if the goal was keeping Treasuries off the market, why spend any euro reserves at all — why not just expand FIMA?" The piece that anticipates this question is stronger than the one that ignores it; right now the thesis looks airtight only because its best counterargument was left on the cutting-room floor.
  - Suggested addition or correction: One to two sentences in the spectrum section presenting the objection and the answer (the euro leg buys credibility and alliance commitment that plumbing alone cannot — argued, not asserted).
  - Severity: Should-fix

- **Gap:** The "disorderly vs. misalignment" fight is presented one-sidedly. Sobel's case gets its strongest support (tight spreads, business getting done) but the counter-evidence — Nikkei vol at the 97th percentile, the speed of the slide, Donnelly's joint-operations hit rate — is scattered or absent.
  - Why it matters: This is the piece's most intellectually honest section; leaving it unbalanced undercuts the credibility the spectrum format is meant to buy.
  - Suggested addition or correction: Add the vol-index sentence and note Donnelly's finding that joint operations have historically coincided with trend turns more often than solo ones.
  - Severity: Should-fix

- **Gap:** The ECB dimension is mentioned as atmosphere ("watched from the sidelines") but never analyzed. The US sold the euro area's currency, in size, without ECB participation or a G7 framework — a genuine break with intervention etiquette that cuts both ways: it supports the "ad hoc favor" reading over the "managed bloc" reading, and it leaves EUR/JPY exposure and European diplomatic reaction as an unpriced tail.
  - Why it matters: It's the most original unused angle in the research, and it directly disciplines the TFTC "first brick of a managed bloc" reading the piece entertains.
  - Suggested addition or correction: Two to three sentences in the "managed bloc" bullet: the absence of the ECB — whose currency was the instrument — is evidence *against* architecture, and what the ECB says next is itself a signal to watch.
  - Severity: Should-fix

- **Gap:** The Plaza/Louvre analogue is never used, though the "managed bloc" reading and the "Plaza-style turn" phrase both appear in the research.
  - Why it matters: 1985 is the natural historical anchor for "is this a dollar-policy turn?" — the question Interpretation B raises. One line would give readers the right benchmark (Plaza involved selling *dollars*, in size, with a communiqué — none of which happened here).
  - Suggested addition or correction: A single sentence in the trade-alignment bullet.
  - Severity: Optional

- **Gap:** The asymmetry of repeated intervention is unexamined: the ESF euro stockpile is finite, so the US cannot run this play indefinitely; and if the yen breaks back through ¥164 despite a *joint* operation, the credibility damage is now bilateral, not just Japanese.
  - Why it matters: The piece's close asks "how much more dollar strength is this Treasury willing to give up" — but the binding constraint may be inventory (euros) rather than tolerance, and the failure mode of a failed joint defense is worse than a failed solo one.
  - Suggested addition or correction: One sentence in the closing section acknowledging both limits.
  - Severity: Optional

- **Gap:** The Asian contagion channel Bessent explicitly cited (won, yuan, export-price competition) is quoted but not unpacked.
  - Why it matters: It's the official rationale most consistent with the trade-alignment reading, and the research's cross-asset section already does the work (yen crosses repricing, sterling weakest on the day).
  - Suggested addition or correction: Optional one-liner; only if the trade-alignment bullet is expanded.
  - Severity: Optional

## Verdict

Corrections required — 3 blockers across 3 surfaces: (1) blog Tanase quote unverifiable against the research artifact — verify at source or cut; (2) carousel slide 7 "never has to sell Treasuries" overstates FIMA's capped, episodic scope; (3) infographic Concept 2 visualizes an unsourced ~80 USD/JPY level for 2011. Behind the blockers sits one systemic should-fix: every surface states the bond-defense interpretation as fact where the research holds it as the leading of four readings — soften the framing devices (dek, lede, slide 6, infographic payoff lines) to match the nuance the blog's own spectrum section already carries. Factual discipline elsewhere is strong; fix the above and this is ready for SEO audit.
