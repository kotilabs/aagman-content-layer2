**What happened (the fact)**
The Q1FY27 earnings season is effectively over. CMIE data on listed companies (as of August 14) shows aggregate net sales holding up, and Moneycontrol's August 15 Weekender frames the season as "strong numbers, hard choices"; earlier HSBC analysis (August 7) found earnings had not cracked the way the market feared, with consensus expecting ~14% growth for 2026 and ~17% for 2027.
Source: Moneycontrol Pro Weekender, August 15, 2026 — https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/moneycontrol-pro-weekender-at-79-strong-numbers-hard-choices-14005913.html (Tier 1); HSBC analysis via Moneycontrol, August 7, 2026 — https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/earnings-haven-t-cracked-the-way-the-market-feared-hsbc-data-shows-13997885.html (Tier 1).

**Why this matters now**
With foreign investors net sellers all year and the index off highs, the equity market's floor rests on the domestic-earnings-resilience claim. The season just provided the actual data to test that claim — and the answer is conditional: aggregates held, but margin dispersion widened.

**Companies, sectors, indices most affected**
Broad market; specifically the sectors where consensus FY27 estimates remain at risk of downgrade (IT, consumption) versus those where upgrades are clustering (metals, select financials).

**What most quick commentary is missing**
Season wrap-ups count beats and misses. The analytically useful cut is estimate *revision breadth* — how many companies saw FY27 numbers move versus held — and margin decomposition between input-cost relief and demand softness. "Earnings didn't crack" and "earnings quality deteriorated" can both be true.
