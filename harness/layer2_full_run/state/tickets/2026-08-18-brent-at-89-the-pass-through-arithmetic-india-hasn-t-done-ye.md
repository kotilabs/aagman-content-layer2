**What happened (the fact)**
Brent traded near $89/barrel on August 17 (Reuters quoted $88.72 in early trade; ICE October futures $89.17 intraday), up roughly 6% on the week, as the US-Iran stalemate over Hormuz continued. WTI was in the low $80s.
Source: polyestertime oil wrap, August 17, 2026 (Tier 3 — flagged; figures corroborated by Interfax, August 17, 2026, and by the Moneycontrol bond-yield report above which cites the crude move). Retained because the price level is verified across multiple same-day reports; the underlying driver is documented in Tier 1 coverage of the Hormuz stalemate.

**Why this matters now**
India's crude import bill, OMC marketing margins, and the inflation print all move on a lag. A week at $89 with the rupee near record lows is a materially worse input-cost event than the same price with a stronger currency — the FX-amplified pass-through is what hits Q2 earnings.

**Companies, sectors, indices most affected**
OMCs (IOC, BPCL, HPCL), aviation (IndiGo), paints and tyres (Asian Paints, MRF), fertiliser subsidies and the fiscal arithmetic, CPI via fuel and transport.

**What most quick commentary is missing**
Coverage states the crude price and moves on. Nobody is publishing the current passthrough math: at what Brent-plus-rupee combination do OMC marketing margins go negative, when does the next CPI print register fuel, and where the fiscal burden lands if retail prices stay frozen. The June experience (Brent $94 spike) provided a live test case that went unanalyzed.
