india's june-quarter earnings season is over, and the headline reads well. the most important number of the season is the one nobody published.

by august 14, the tally looked like relief. listed non-financial companies grew net sales 22.5% year on year, nearly double the prior quarter's pace, and hsbc counted 73% of results in line with expectations or better, near the top of the band it has tracked since 2020.

then the footnote. aggregate manufacturing operating profit fell versus last year, until you remove petroleum products. ex-petroleum, the same dataset shows 20.2% growth. one sector's weight flipped the sign of the entire manufacturing profit pool. both readings are true; they answer different questions.

the season was really about revisions. a beats count measures results against expectations set before reporting began. revision breadth measures what analysts did to full-year numbers after seeing them. since june-end, upgrades clustered in basic materials, financials, industrials and consumer staples, powered partly by non-food credit growing 18.3% in june against roughly 10% late last year. downgrades landed in consumer discretionary, health care and utilities.

it sits in its own category. tcs won $9.5 billion of deals while revenue barely moved; infosys narrowed fy27 growth guidance to 1.5-3%. outcome-based ai contracts change when revenue is recognised, so pipelines and p&ls now measure different things.

the stakes are visible in the flows. the nifty trades about 15% below its january peak. foreign investors sold a record rs 2.87 lakh crore by mid-june, then turned: plus rs 17,227 crore in july, more in august. returning capital re-underwrites the earnings claim through revisions, and the net revision count for the market, upgrades in four sectors against downgrades in three, has never been published.

the full piece on the aagman substack this week goes sector by sector through the upgrade map, the petroleum distortion, and what the september estimate revisions will settle.
