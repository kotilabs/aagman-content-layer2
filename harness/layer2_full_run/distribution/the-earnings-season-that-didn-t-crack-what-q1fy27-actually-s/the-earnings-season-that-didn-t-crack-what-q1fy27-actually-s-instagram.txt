indian companies just posted their best quarter in years. the most important number is the one nobody published.

by august 14, the results tally looked like a relief. listed non-financial companies grew net sales 22.5% year on year, nearly double the previous quarter's pace. hsbc counted 73% of results in line with expectations or better, near the top of the band it has tracked since 2020.

then you open the footnote. manufacturing operating profit actually fell versus last year. remove one sector, petroleum products, and the same dataset shows 20.2% growth. same companies, same quarter, opposite signs. one sector's weight flipped the entire profit pool.

the headline also counts the wrong thing. a beat measures results against expectations set before the season began. what matters now is what analysts did to next year's numbers after seeing them. upgrades clustered in basic materials, financials, industrials, staples. downgrades landed in consumer discretionary, health care, utilities.

in it, tcs won $9.5 billion of deals and revenue barely moved. infosys trimmed its fy27 growth guidance to 1.5-3%. outcome-based ai contracts change when revenue gets recognised, so the pipeline and the p&l have stopped moving together.

why you care: the nifty sits 15% below its january peak. foreign investors sold a record 2.87 lakh crore rupees by mid-june, then turned buyer in july. that returning money is betting on earnings holding, and the verdict arrives quietly, in revision tables.

save this for september, when the big brokerages publish fresh fy27 estimates. if the numbers keep drifting down while the season "beat", you will know the headline lied.

the full piece on the aagman substack this week maps every upgrade and downgrade by sector, the petroleum distortion in one chart, and the aggregate count nobody has computed in public.
