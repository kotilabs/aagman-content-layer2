four announcements in one august week, and the interesting thing is that each is paid for differently.

india leased two mq-9b drones for ₹1,943 crore over 30 months, which works out to about ₹65 crore a month. the navy already owns 31 on paper, from a $3.99 billion order signed in october 2024. the lease is best read as interest paid on slow delivery.

adani proposed roughly ₹80,000 crore of coal-to-chemicals capex in odisha, plus two 3,000 mw nuclear plants and an aluminium complex. proposals, not sanctions. meanwhile the group has raised or approved over ₹50,000 crore of public equity in eighteen months. the raises are dated and disclosed; the projects are not. that gap is now measurable.

aheesa taped out a 28nm risc-v chip under meity's design incentive. design margin, no fab capex. and a study pegs india's launch cost at $13,302 per kg against $3,225 for the us, because five launches a year cannot spread fixed costs.

four instruments, four distinct failure modes. the underreported one is the drone lease's renewability. the full piece on the aagman substack this week works through what each failure mode would look like in the filings.
