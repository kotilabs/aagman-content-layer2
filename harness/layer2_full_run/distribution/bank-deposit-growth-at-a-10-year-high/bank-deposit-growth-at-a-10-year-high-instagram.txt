india's bank deposits grew 15.4% in july, fastest in a decade. the rbi's own dollar scheme drove most of the jump.

deposits are the money banks use to give loans. for two years, loans were growing faster than deposits, so banks kept raising fd rates to fight for your savings.

then in june the rbi opened a special window: banks collect dollars from nris, swap them with the rbi for rupees at a fixed rate, and those rupees land in the system as deposits. $52.3 billion came in before the window was shut early. growth jumped from 12.7% to 15.4% in a single fortnight, right as the dollars converted.

one thing the headline hides: deposits had actually fallen rs 3.87 trillion between april and mid-june. the record base of rs 269.4 trillion is a rebound plus an inflow, and nobody's saving habits changed.

what it means for you: if deposits stay flush, banks stop bidding up fd rates, and any rbi rate cut next year reaches your emi faster. credit is still growing at 19.3%, faster than deposits, so the squeeze has eased but it has not ended.

the window takes its last deposits on august 31. save this for early september, when the mid-august print shows whether 15% survives without the inflow.
