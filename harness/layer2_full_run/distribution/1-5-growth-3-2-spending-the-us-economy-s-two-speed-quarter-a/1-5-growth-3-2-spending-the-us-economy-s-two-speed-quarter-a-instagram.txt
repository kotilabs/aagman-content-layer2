the us economy grew 1.5% last quarter. americans spent like it grew 3.2%.

both numbers are real. they came out on the same day, in the same government release.

gdp is addition: consumer spending plus investment plus government plus trade. when the consumer alone added 2.12 points to a quarter that only grew 1.5, the shortfall had to hide somewhere specific.

it hid in three places. businesses ran down their inventories. imports jumped, mostly telecom gear and semiconductors for the ai buildout, and imports subtract from gdp by definition. and the government line fell because washington sold oil out of the strategic petroleum reserve, a bookkeeping move the statistics bureau itself says has no real effect on output.

none of those three is your salary, your rent, or your grocery bill.

here is the part that touches you. the average american saved 2.7 cents of every dollar in june, the fifth straight monthly decline and the lowest in four years. real spending is growing five times faster than real income. the 3.2% consumer is being paid for out of savings while paychecks barely move.

companies are still reporting 15% revenue growth, so the slowdown headlines and the economy in your daily life are genuinely running at two different speeds right now.

save this for august 26. that is when this gdp number gets its first revision, and the revision will show whether the drag was real or just timing. the full decomposition is on the aagman substack this week.
