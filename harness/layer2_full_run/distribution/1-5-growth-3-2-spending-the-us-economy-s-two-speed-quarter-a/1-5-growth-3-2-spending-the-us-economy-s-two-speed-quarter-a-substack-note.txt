there was no slowdown in the quarter that produced the slowdown headline, at least not where the headline implies. real gdp grew 1.5%, down from 2.1%, and consumer spending's contribution rose from 0.37 points to 2.12. the arithmetic is unforgiving: the drag had to sit somewhere, and it sat in inventories (minus 0.67), net exports (minus 1.01), and a strategic petroleum reserve accounting mechanic that bea itself says has no direct output effect (minus 0.14).

three facts worth holding together. real final sales to private domestic purchasers, the fed's own demand gauge, accelerated from 1.7% to 3.9%. the import surge was capital goods, telecom equipment and semiconductors, which reads as investment demand arriving at the port. and the june saving rate printed 2.7%, the lowest since june 2022, with real spending growing five times faster than real income.

the underreported detail: companies report nominal revenue, and nominal gdp rose 7.9%. that gap is the whole bridge between a 1.5% quarter and 15% blended s&p 500 revenue growth.

the live question is which side of the wedge the september fomc believes, and what the august 26 second estimate does to the drag lines.
