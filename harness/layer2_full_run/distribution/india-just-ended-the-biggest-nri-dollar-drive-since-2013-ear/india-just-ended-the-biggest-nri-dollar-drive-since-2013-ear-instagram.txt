india collected $56.8 billion of nri dollars to defend the rupee, then shut the window a month early.

here is how it worked. in june, the rbi let banks swap nri dollar deposits with itself and absorbed the entire hedging cost, so banks could offer up to 7.8% on a dollar deposit. the money came in fast: the first $20 billion took 38 days, the next $20 billion took 14, the last $16 billion took 13. double the 2013 haul, in a third of the time.

on august 5, the governor said there were no plans to close the window early. on august 14, the window was closed early.

why it matters to you: every swapped dollar lands in the system as rupees, which is part of why deposit growth just hit a 10-year high and liquidity sits near rs 3 lakh crore. cheaper money for banks and bonds, with the cost parked on the rbi's own balance sheet until 2029-2031. and the rupee itself went from 95.39 to 95.45 the whole time. reserves were bought. relief was not.

save this for the next time you see a record-forex-reserves headline. some of those dollars are borrowed, and they have to be given back. the full breakdown is on the aagman substack this week.
