india collected $56.8 billion of nri dollars to defend the rupee, then shut the window a month early.

on june 8 the rbi opened a special scheme. non-resident indians placed dollar deposits with indian banks, and the rbi absorbed the entire currency-hedging cost through a swap. that subsidy is why banks could offer up to 7.8% on a dollar deposit, a rate the market would never have supported on its own.

the money came in fast. the first $20 billion took 38 days. the next $20 billion took 14. then $16 billion landed in the final 13 days. by august 13, deposits alone had hit $52.3 billion, double what the same instrument raised over three months back in 2013.

on august 5, the rbi governor said there were no plans to close the window early. on august 14, the window was closed early.

here is the part that touches you. every swapped dollar lands in the banking system as rupees. surplus liquidity sits near rs 3 lakh crore, deposit growth hit 15.4%, and sbi research estimates the drive eventually channels rs 8-9 lakh crore into banks and government bonds. cheaper money for the system, with the cost parked on the rbi's own balance sheet until 2029-2031.

and the rupee? it moved from 95.39 to 95.45 over the scheme's whole life. fifty billion dollars bought reserves, not relief.

save this for the next time you see a headline about record forex reserves. some of those dollars are borrowed, and they have to be given back. the full breakdown is on the aagman substack this week.
