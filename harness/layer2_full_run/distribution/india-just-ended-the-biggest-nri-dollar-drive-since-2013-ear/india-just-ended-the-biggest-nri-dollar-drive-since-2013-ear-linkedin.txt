the rbi just closed its biggest dollar-collecting scheme since 2013. a month early. nine days after saying it had no plans to.

quick context. when the rupee weakens, india needs dollars. in june, the rbi opened a window: nris parked dollars in indian bank deposits, and the rbi covered the currency-hedging cost itself. that let banks offer up to 7.8% on a dollar deposit.

the money came in faster than anyone planned. the first $20 billion took 38 days. the next $20 billion took 14. the last $16 billion took 13. total: $56.8 billion, double the 2013 taper-tantrum haul, in a third of the time.

then on august 14, the rbi shut the window.

here is the part worth thinking about. over the scheme's entire life, the rupee moved from 95.39 to 95.45. fifty billion dollars of subsidised inflows, and the currency did not move. india bought a dollar stockpile, not a stronger rupee.

and every one of those swapped dollars has to be returned to banks between 2029 and 2031, at today's exchange rate.

one more thing, from 2013. when india ran this play during the taper tantrum, much of the "nri money" turned out to be overseas bank money lent to nris at up to 19x leverage, and india quietly paid about 5% for it. the same structures are reportedly back this year.

so the question the full piece takes up: if the rupee retests its record low before these swaps mature, whose reserves are they really? the answer is in the forward book, and it is readable if you know where to look. link in the first comment.
