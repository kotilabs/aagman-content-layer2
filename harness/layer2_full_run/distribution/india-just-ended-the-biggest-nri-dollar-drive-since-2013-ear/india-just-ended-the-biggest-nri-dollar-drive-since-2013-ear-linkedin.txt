the rbi just closed its biggest dollar-collecting scheme since 2013. a month early. nine days after saying it had no plans to.

the backstory, quickly. when the rupee weakens, india needs dollars. in june, the rbi opened a window: nris parked dollars in indian bank deposits, and the rbi covered the currency-hedging cost itself. that let banks offer up to 7.8% on a dollar deposit.

the money came in faster than anyone planned. the first $20 billion took 38 days. the next $20 billion took 14. the last $16 billion took 13. total haul: $56.8 billion, double what the same playbook raised in the 2013 taper tantrum, in a third of the time.

then on august 14, the rbi shut the window. deposits stop august 31.

two things worth knowing.

the money isn't free. for every dollar taken, the rbi must hand dollars back to banks between 2029 and 2031, at today's exchange rate. think gift cards sold for cash today. the bill arrives later, sized by wherever the rupee is then.

and while all this money arrived, the rupee went from 95.39 to 95.45. essentially nothing. india bought a dollar stockpile, not a stronger currency.

so why close early? either the scheme hit its target ahead of schedule, or carrying the hedge on tens of billions got expensive. both fit the facts. anyone claiming certainty is guessing.
