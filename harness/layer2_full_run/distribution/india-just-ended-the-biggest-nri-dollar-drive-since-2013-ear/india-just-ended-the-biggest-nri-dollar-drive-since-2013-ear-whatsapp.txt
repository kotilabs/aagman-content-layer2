the rbi closed its biggest nri dollar drive since 2013 a month early, nine days after the governor said there were no plans to.

what happened:
- the scheme let banks swap nri dollar deposits with the rbi, which absorbed the whole hedging cost, so banks could offer up to *7.8%* on dollar deposits
- the money flooded in: the first *$20 billion* took 38 days, the next *$20 billion* took 14, then *$16 billion* in 13 days. total *$56.85 billion*, of which *$52.3 billion* came through fcnr(b) deposits
- the rupee went from 95.39 to *95.45* the whole time: inflows bought reserves, not relief
- every swapped dollar has to be returned in 2029-2031 at today's exchange rate. and in 2013, mid-crisis, this same instrument raised *$26 billion* in three months; this year it did double that in ten weeks

takeaway: either the rbi got its dollars early, or the hedge was getting expensive. the governor ruled out early closure nine days before doing exactly that.
