brent crude is near *$89* a barrel (aug 17), up 6% in a week on the us-iran standoff over hormuz. the rupee closed at *95.61* per dollar, about 1% off its record low.

the numbers:

- $89 x 95.61 = *₹8,509 per barrel*, about ₹53.5 a litre of crude input
- in may 2022, $112 oil at ₹77.8 cost ₹8,728 a barrel. today's $89 costs within 3% of that
- in january the same barrel cost about ₹5,670; rupee crude cost is up 50% in seven months
- each +$1 in brent adds about ₹0.60 a litre; each ₹1 slide in the rupee adds about ₹0.56. the two dials are now equal in power

why the pump has not moved:

- delhi petrol is frozen at *₹102.12* since the ₹3 hike of may 15, the first revision since april 2022
- the freeze pushes the shock onto oil company margins; breakeven sits around brent $92-99
- above $100 in april-may, omcs were losing nearly *₹30,000 crore a month*

takeaway: a frozen pump price relocates inflation into omc margins and the fiscal gap (lpg under-recoveries of ₹53,700 crore vs ₹30,000 crore compensation).
