brent settled at $89 on monday, and the number worth sitting with is the rupee one. at 95.61 to the dollar, that barrel costs india ₹8,509. in may 2022, $112 oil cost ₹8,728. the currency has quietly re-created a 2022-scale input shock at a $23-lower headline price.

three facts worth holding together. delhi petrol has been frozen at ₹102.12 since may 15, so the shock currently lands on omc margins rather than the cpi. june's print caught the may hike within a single month, which prices the pump-to-print lag at about 30 days. and the sensitivity math says a ₹1 slide in the rupee now costs almost exactly what a $1 rise in brent costs, ₹0.56 versus ₹0.60 a litre.

the underreported detail: the breakeven arithmetic keeps petrol margins positive only up to roughly $92-99 at this exchange rate. june's sub-$80 relief window is already closed.

open question: does the august print on september 14 stay clean because the freeze held, and if brent re-tests $100, what breaks first, the pump price, the excise lever, or the lpg compensation math?
