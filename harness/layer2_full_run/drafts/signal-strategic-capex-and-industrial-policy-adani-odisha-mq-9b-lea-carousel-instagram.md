## Instagram Carousel — The Four Kinds of Money Behind India's Strategic Build-Out

---

### Slide 1

**India is financing strategic capacity four different ways at once.**

An ₹80,000 crore coal-gasification proposal in Odisha. A ₹1,943 crore drone lease in New Delhi. A 28nm chip taped out in Chennai. A launch-cost study nobody wanted to read.

Same week. Same theme. Four completely different instruments — and the instruments are the story.

---

### Slide 2

The proposal on the table: Karan Adani met Odisha's chief minister with a three-part roadmap — a bauxite-to-aluminium complex anchored by Dhamra port, two 3,000 MW nuclear plants, and coal-to-chemicals in Sundargarh at an estimated ₹80,000 crore.

Read it carefully: proposed, not sanctioned. No MoU text, no board approval, no clearance filings are public. Two 3,000 MW nuclear plants would be a scale no private Indian group has ever built.

---

### Slide 3

To judge an announcement, follow the equity.

Adani has tapped public equity five times in eighteen months: a ₹25,000 crore rights issue in 2025, a ₹15,000 crore Adani Enterprises QIP upsized on demand in July 2026, ₹8,500 crore and ₹3,500 crore at Adani Energy Solutions in May and July, and board approval for up to ₹15,000 crore more at Adani Power.

Over ₹50,000 crore raised or approved since 2025. The Odisha announcement lands on top of this pipeline — not instead of it.

---

### Slide 4

The drone lease tells the same story from the government's side.

On August 17, the Ministry of Defence signed a ₹1,943 crore, 30-month lease with General Atomics for two MQ-9B Sea Guardians. The Navy already flies two leased MQ-9Bs and waits on a $3.99 billion order for 31, signed in October 2024.

Divide it out: roughly ₹65 crore a month, for the pair, to rent surveillance while the owned fleet is pending. The lease is the interest paid on slow delivery.

---

### Slide 5

Here is the pivot.

This isn't an industrial boom. It's a financing structure.

Conglomerate balance sheets for heavy industry. Operating leases for defence. Design subsidies for silicon. Four tools, four failure modes — and for space, the instrument that doesn't exist yet.

---

### Slide 6

The chip that doesn't need a fab.

Chennai's Aheesa taped out VIHAAN-I, a fully indigenous 28nm broadband chip built on C-DAC's VEGA RISC-V core, in February 2026 under MeitY's Design Linked Incentive scheme. Twenty-eight nanometres is a node old enough to be cheap and new enough to matter — foundry capacity is abundant.

India is buying the design margin without the fab capex. The counterexample: a strategic win through subsidy design rather than balance-sheet risk.

---

### Slide 7

The launch ledger is the uncomfortable one.

India's estimated cost to orbit in 2025: $13,302 per kg — the highest among major spacefaring nations. Europe: $9,897. Russia: $6,682. China: $5,809. The US: $3,225. India's heaviest communication satellite, the 4,700 kg GSAT-N2, flew on a Falcon 9.

Per-kg cost is fixed costs divided by cadence — and India ran five launches in 2025. Frugal mission design cannot fix a small denominator. Each instrument breaks its own way: equity capex on leverage, leased defence on dependence, subsidised design on fragmentation, launch economics on absence.

---

### Slide 8

So the question is what converts first.

A signed Odisha MoU with land and timelines — or the next QIP? Financing has a paper trail; proposals don't. The equity raises are dated and disclosed. The ₹80,000 crore is not. Watch the order in which those documents appear.
