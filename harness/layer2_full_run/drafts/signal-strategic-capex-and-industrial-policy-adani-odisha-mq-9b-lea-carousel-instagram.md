### Slide 2 — The proposal on the table

- **Kicker:** ODISHA FILES
- **Headline:** Two 3,000 MW nuclear plants, an aluminium complex, and ₹80,000 crore of coal gasification — proposed, not sanctioned
- **Body:** Karan Adani met Odisha's chief minister with a three-part roadmap: bauxite-to-aluminium anchored by Dhamra port, 6 GW of nuclear baseload, and coal-to-chemicals in Sundargarh. No MoU text, no board sanction, no clearance filings are public. The estimate for the aluminium complex alone: ~$11.5 billion.
- **Hero stat:** 2 × 3,000 MW — the proposed nuclear capacity, a scale no private Indian group has built (Whalesbook; Discovery Alert, Aug 2026)
- **Design note:** Map outline of Odisha with three pins — Sundargarh (gasification), Dhamra (port/aluminium), nuclear sites marked "TBD." Status stamp: "PROPOSAL STAGE."
- **Speaker note:** Concrete first; the word "proposed" does the discipline work before any number impresses.

### Slide 3 — Follow the equity, not the announcement

- **Kicker:** THE FUNDING ENGINE
- **Headline:** Adani has tapped public equity five times in eighteen months
- **Body:** ₹25,000 crore rights issue (2025). ₹15,000 crore Adani Enterprises QIP, upsized on demand (July 2026). ₹8,500 crore plus ₹3,500 crore at Adani Energy Solutions (May and July 2026). Adani Power's board approved up to ₹15,000 crore more (22 July 2026). The Odisha announcement lands on top of this pipeline — not instead of it.
- **Hero stat:** ₹50,000+ crore of group equity raised or approved since 2025 (Multibagg.ai compilation of exchange disclosures, Jul 2026)
- **Design note:** Horizontal timeline of five raise events as ledger entries — date, instrument, amount. No imagery; the ledger is the visual.
- **Speaker note:** The evidence slide — the capex wave is real, but it runs on repeated equity taps, and that is the monitorable.

### Slide 5 — The drone lease

- **Kicker:** RENTED SURVEILLANCE
- **Headline:** India is paying ~₹65 crore a month to rent aircraft it has already agreed to buy
- **Body:** On 17 August the MoD signed a ₹1,943 crore, 30-month lease with General Atomics for two MQ-9B Sea Guardians (PIB). The Navy already flies two leased MQ-9Bs and waits on a $3.99 billion order for 31, signed in October 2024. The lease is the *interest* paid on slow delivery.
- **Hero stat:** ₹1,943 crore ÷ 30 months ÷ 2 aircraft — lease arithmetic from the PIB release, 17 Aug 2026
- **Design note:** Receipt-style graphic: item, term, monthly equivalent. A small silhouette of the Sea Guardian, monochrome.
- **Speaker note:** Reframes a defence headline as a financing instrument — opex bridging a procurement gap.

### Slide 6 — Pivot

- **Kicker:** THE ACTUAL STORY
- **Headline:** This isn't an industrial boom. It's a financing structure.
- **Body:** Conglomerate balance sheets for heavy industry. Operating leases for defence. Design subsidies for silicon. And, for space, the instrument that doesn't exist yet — aggregated launch demand. Four tools, four failure modes.
- **Hero stat:** 4 instruments: equity taps, opex lease, DLI design incentive, and (missing) demand aggregation — Layer 2 synthesis of this week's filings and releases

### Slide 7 — The chip that doesn't need a fab

- **Kicker:** DESIGN, SUBSIDISED
- **Headline:** Chennai's Aheesa taped out a fully indigenous 28nm network chip — and the economics are the point
- **Body:** VIHAAN-I pairs C-DAC's VEGA RISC-V core with broadband-access features, taped out in February 2026 under MeitY's Design Linked Incentive scheme (EE Times, 5 Feb 2026). Mature-node silicon is cheap to fabricate — foundry capacity is abundant. India is buying the design margin without the fab capex.
- **Hero stat:** 28nm — a node old enough to be cheap, new enough to matter for broadband CPE (EE Times; The Volt Post, Feb 2026)
- **Design note:** Clean die-shot-style abstract in saffron-grey. Node number "28nm" oversized in a corner.
- **Speaker note:** The counterexample — a strategic win achieved through subsidy design rather than balance-sheet risk.

### Slide 8 — The number nobody wanted

- **Kicker:** THE LAUNCH LEDGER
- **Headline:** India's cost to orbit: $13,302 per kg — the highest among major spacefaring nations
- **Body:** Europe: $9,897. Russia: $6,682. China: $5,809. USA: $3,225 (peer-reviewed study summarized by Civilsdaily, 16 Aug 2026). India ran five launches in 2025. Per-kg cost is fixed costs divided by cadence — frugal mission design cannot fix a small denominator. India's heaviest comsat, the 4,700 kg GSAT-N2, flew on a Falcon 9.
- **Hero stat:** $13,302/kg vs $3,225/kg — India vs USA, 2025 estimates (Civilsdaily, 16 Aug 2026)
- **Design note:** Vertical bar chart of the six nations' per-kg costs, India's bar in red, US bar in grey. GSAT-N2 callout in a footnote strip.
- **Speaker note:** The uncomfortable evidence slide — the gap is arithmetic, not a narrative problem.

### Slide 9 — Where each structure breaks

- **Kicker:** FOUR FAILURE MODES
- **Headline:** Every instrument has its own way of going wrong
- **Body:** Equity-funded conglomerate capex breaks on *leverage* if execution slips. Leased defence breaks on dependence — 30 months assumes the owned fleet arrives. Subsidised design breaks on fragmentation — one tapeout is not a pipeline. And launch economics break on absence — no heavy vehicle, no cadence, no curve to climb.
- **Hero stat:** 5 launches in 2025 — the cadence beneath India's per-kg cost (Civilsdaily, 16 Aug 2026)
- **Design note:** Continuation of slide 6's grid, now with each failure mode expanded to one sentence. Consistent typography.
- **Speaker note:** Converts the thesis into a watchlist — risk framed structurally, never as advice.
