## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: "Where the 1.5% Hides" — the drag decomposition waterfall
- Central insight: the consumer added more than the entire quarter's growth (+2.12 of +1.5 pts); the shortfall was forced into three specific, low-information lines — and none of them is business capex.
- Data: PCE +2.12 (Q1: +0.37); fixed investment +1.20 (of which buildout +1.28, Q1: +1.55); inventories −0.67; net exports −1.01; government −0.14; total = +1.5% (BEA NIPA 1.1.2, advance, 2026-07-30 via Phoebe Reads; BEA release).
- Visualization type: waterfall/contribution bridge chart, Q1→Q2 paired bars.
- Composition notes: reader sees the 1.5% headline bar first, then watches it decompose left to right — the tall green consumer bar, the mid-size investment bar, then three red bars doing the damage. Payoff strip under each red bar rates its "half-life": inventories (reverses), trade (import-led = demand signal), government (SPR accounting, BEA: no direct GDP effect). A side rail compares Q1 contributions (+0.37 consumer) to show the rotation.
- Sources: BEA GDP advance estimate 2026-07-30; NIPA 1.1.2 decomposition via Phoebe Reads 2026-07-30; SPR mechanic per BEA technical notes.

### Concept 2: "Two Speedometers" — headline GDP vs private domestic demand
- Central insight: the gauge the Fed watches — real final sales to private domestic purchasers — accelerated from +1.7% to +3.9% in the same quarter the headline slowed from 2.1% to 1.5%.
- Data: real GDP 2.1% → 1.5%; real final sales to private domestic purchasers 1.7% → 3.9%; real PCE ~3.2% (signal-record figure, consistent with +2.12 pt contribution); FOMC 9–3 hold at 3.50%–3.75% on 2026-07-29 (PaySpace, secondary).
- Visualization type: divergence — two paired gauge/line panels moving in opposite directions, with the 2.4-pt private-demand gap highlighted.
- Composition notes: two speedometer faces side by side; the left (headline) needle drops, the right (private demand) needle climbs. Below, a small vote-bar: 9 hold vs 3 dissent — the divergence institutionalized. Payoff line: which speedometer the September meeting believes is the open question, not the answer.
- Sources: BEA GDP advance estimate 2026-07-30; signal record for the 3.2%; PaySpace 2026-07-30 for the vote split.

### Concept 3: "The 3.2% Is Real. So Is the 2.7%." — how the consumer is funded
- Central insight: real spending is growing nearly five times faster than real income (+2.54% vs +0.52% y/y), with the saving rate at 2.7% — fifth straight monthly decline, lowest since June 2022.
- Data: saving rate 2.7% June 2026 (BEA PIO); real PCE +2.54% y/y vs real DPI +0.52% y/y (Phoebe Reads reading BEA PIO); June real PCE +0.4% m/m for a second straight month (BEA PIO); spending mix — services led by food services/accommodations and financial services; goods led by prescription drugs, light trucks, furniture (BEA GDP technical notes).
- Visualization type: ratio/flow — an income pipe vs spending pipe diagram with a shrinking savings reservoir between them.
- Composition notes: two pipes of visibly different widths feed a tank draining toward a marked 2.7% floor; a June 2022 reference tick shows when it was last this low. A small sidebar lists where the money actually went (services > goods, 8:1 in June dollars). Payoff: the 3.2% is not fake — it is finite. No alarmism; the composition panel shows what real funding stress would look like first.
- Sources: BEA Personal Income and Outlays June 2026 (2026-07-30); Phoebe Reads 2026-07-30; BEA GDP advance technical notes.

### Concept 4: "1.5% GDP, 15% Revenue" — mapping the wedge onto listed sectors
- Central insight: companies report nominal revenue against current-dollar GDP of +7.9%, not real +1.5% — and Q2's winning sector revenue lines (Energy, IT, Communication Services) read directly off the GDP components that grew.
- Data: S&P 500 blended Q2 revenue growth 15.0%, all 11 GICS sectors positive y/y, led by Energy/IT/Comm Services (FactSet 2026-08-07); current-dollar GDP +7.9% (BEA); petroleum-led export growth (BEA); buildout +1.28 pts (Phoebe Reads); blended EPS growth 50.4% headline vs 32.0% ex-Alphabet/Amazon one-time gains of $98B and $53.4B (FactSet).
- Visualization type: flow/map — GDP component boxes on the left wired to the sector revenue lines they feed on the right.
- Composition notes: left column = Q2 GDP components (consumer +2.12, buildout +1.28, petroleum exports, housing +0.05); right column = the sector revenue scoreboard with leaders marked. A honesty ribbon across the top carries the 50.4% → 32.0% ex-two-companies adjustment so "earnings quality" is visually priced in. Payoff: the wedge is not GDP-vs-earnings; it is real-vs-nominal and volume-vs-price.
- Sources: FactSet Earnings Season Update 2026-08-07; BEA GDP advance estimate 2026-07-30; Phoebe Reads 2026-07-30.

### Concept 5: "The Drag Has a Half-Life" — ranking what reverses
- Central insight: inventories, trade, and SPR-driven government drag are the three most reversible lines in the GDP identity; the buildout's deceleration (+1.55 → +1.28) is the only deceleration that would compound if real.
- Data: inventories −0.67 (wholesale-led); net exports −1.01 (capital-goods imports); government −0.14 (SPR sales, no direct GDP effect per BEA); buildout +1.28 vs +1.55 Q1; residential −0.30 → +0.05 (BEA via Phoebe Reads).
- Visualization type: ranking/matrix — drag components on an x-axis of "signal content" and y-axis of "likelihood of persistence."
- Composition notes: three red chips cluster in the low-persistence corner; the buildout chip sits alone in the watch zone. Small annotation: imports subtracting because companies are buying foreign capital equipment is a symptom of the investment boom, not its opposite. Payoff: what to watch in the August 26 second estimate and September 30 annual revision.
- Sources: BEA GDP advance estimate 2026-07-30 (incl. technical notes); Phoebe Reads 2026-07-30.

## What to avoid

- **The 6.25% GDP deflator as a headline visual.** Tempting because it is dramatic; dropped as a lead because the deflator is distorted by a 25.1% annualized jump in export prices that households do not pay, and core PCE decelerated in the same release. Used only as context inside Concept 4's nominal-vs-real framing.
- **The S&P 500 record level (7,814.88) as a standalone graphic.** The number traces to secondary market commentary, not an exchange data feed, and a price-level graphic drifts toward market- cheering. It stays out of the recommended set; if used at all, it belongs as a contextual footnote.
- **"Resilient consumer" celebratory composition.** The saving-rate data (2.7%, fifth monthly decline) makes a one-sided consumer-strength visual misleading; any consumer concept must carry the funding gap, which is why Concept 3 is built as tension, not tribute.
- **FOMC dissent drama graphic.** The 9–3 split is a fact worth one vote-bar inside Concept 2; a standalone "Fed civil war" visual would be narrative ahead of data and off-voice.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with Concepts 1, 3, and 4 as the core set — together they execute the brief exactly: Concept 1 decomposes where the drag sits (investment vs inventories vs trade vs government), Concept 3 prices the consumer's 3.2% against its funding, and Concept 4 maps the divergence onto listed-sector revenue lines with an explicit earnings-quality adjustment. Concepts 2 and 5 are strong alternates if a second or third asset is wanted; Concept 2 is the most quotable single frame.
