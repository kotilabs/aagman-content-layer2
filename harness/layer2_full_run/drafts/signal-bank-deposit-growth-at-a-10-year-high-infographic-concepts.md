## Phase 1 — Extracted data points

1. **15.4% YoY deposit growth as of July 31, 2026 vs 12.7% YoY as of July 15, 2026.** Shows a 270-bps acceleration in a single fortnight. Structurally interesting because no organic savings behaviour moves a ₹260+ trillion base that fast — it fingerprints a mechanical inflow. Source: RBI data via CNBC-TV18 (https://www.cnbctv18.com/economy/bank-credit-growth-hits-2-year-high-in-july-as-deposit-growth-touches-decade-high-rbi-19969994.htm) and RBI WSS via KarmSakha / ICICIdirect.
2. **₹11 trillion (~$115.25 bn) deposit increase over three fortnights to July 31, 2026, after a ₹3.87 trillion decline April 1–June 15.** Shows the "record" was a V-shape: the system lost deposits for ten weeks, then regained three times that in six. Source: RBI data via Reuters / The Economic Times (https://m.economictimes.com/nri/invest/rbi-to-close-fcnrb-forex-swap-facility-early-after-strong-dollar-inflow/articleshow/133241787.cms).
3. **$52.3 bn FCNR(B) swap-window inflows ($56.846 bn across all three channels) between June 8 and August 13, 2026; window closed early.** The mechanical driver sitting underneath data point 1. Source: ET citing RBI (same URL).
4. **$36.7 bn of FCNR(B) inflows June 5–July 31 maps onto the same weeks the deposit growth print jumped.** The timing overlap is the causal story. Source: ET (same URL).
5. **Credit growth 19.3% YoY vs deposit growth 15.4% YoY as of July 31, 2026.** Even at a decade-high, deposits still trail credit by ~4 percentage points — the funding constraint eased but did not close. Source: RBI data via CNBC-TV18.
6. **2013 analogue: ~$34 bn mobilised in total (~$26–27 bn via FCNR(B)) during the taper tantrum vs $56.846 bn total ($52.3 bn FCNR(B)) in 2026.** Scale benchmark across cycles. Sources: Zolve (https://zolve.com/blog/rbis-7-dollar-deposit-for-nris-everything-to-know-before-september-30/), Finnovate, Quantum AMC.
7. **Pre-scheme weakness: FCNR(B) inflows of just $166 mn in April 2026, −39% YoY; NRI deposits down ~26% to $14.35 bn Apr 2025–Jan 2026.** Shows the scheme reversed a collapsing channel, not a healthy one. Sources: Wiretel (RBI data), News18 (Macquarie).

The qualifier belongs inline next to the superlative wherever it appears, not in a footer.
- Data point 6: hard historical figures, but from secondary explainers (Zolve, Finnovate, Quantum AMC), not the 2013 RBI circular directly. Label as approximate ("~$34 bn"). Note for consistency: 2013's ~$34 bn is an all-channels figure (FCNR(B) plus bank overseas borrowings); compare all-channels-to-all-channels or FCNR(B)-to-FCNR(B), never mixed.
- Data point 7: hard RBI-derived data, secondarily reported. Timeframes explicit.
- Misread risk: the 15.4% can be misread as organic household deposit strength. The strongest sourced statement is that traders attribute the jump "largely" to FCNR(B) conversion (Reuters via ET); the FCNR(B)-vs-organic split is not published and must stay an open question in-frame.

No visual may render a continuous growth line or a weekly inflow series — that would fabricate data points. Charts are built from the labeled points and milestones we actually have.
- Simpler meaningful comparisons available: "12.7% → 15.4% in one fortnight" (velocity), "deposits −₹3.87 tn then +₹11 tn" (V-shape), "$56.8 bn in 2026 vs ~$34 bn in 2013" (all-channels scale).

Data is strong, sourced, and naturally visual. Proceed to concepts.

## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: The Fortnight — how deposit growth jumped 270 bps in two weeks
- **Central insight:** India's decade-high deposit print (fastest in over a decade — provisional RBI data) was driven largely by the RBI's dollar-swap window — how much is organic, the data does not yet say.
- **Data:** Two labeled deposit-growth points only — 12.7% YoY (Jul 15) → 15.4% YoY (Jul 31). Two cumulative FCNR(B) milestone callouts only — $36.7 bn mobilised by Jul 31; $52.3 bn by Aug 13. Context markers: FCNR(B) inflows were just $166 mn in April 2026 (−39% YoY) before the window; window opened Jun 8; mobilisation cut-off Aug 31; early closure announced Aug 14.
- **Visualization type:** Annotated two-point step graphic on a calendar axis (April → mid-August 2026). The deposit series renders as exactly two large labeled points joined by an explicit "jump" marker — no interpolated line, no fortnightly fills. FCNR(B) renders as two cumulative milestone flags pinned to Jul 31 and Aug 13 — no weekly bars.
- **Composition notes:** Reader first sees the two points and the jump between them; the eye then drops to the "before" marker (April 2026: $166 mn trickle) and the two cumulative flags showing what flowed in between. Payoff is a forward-looking endnote, not the closure: "The window is closed. The test is the next print — the mid-August fortnight, reported early September: does growth hold near 15% without the inflow?" Provisional-data and reporting-convention qualifiers sit inline beside the "decade high" label itself.
- **Sources:** RBI via CNBC-TV18 (Jul 31 print, convention note), RBI WSS via KarmSakha and ICICIdirect (Jul 15 print), ET/Reuters (cumulative inflows, closure, trader attribution), Wiretel/News18 (April 2026 pre-scheme weakness).

### Concept 2: The V-Shape — ₹269.4 trillion record, built in six weeks
- **Central insight:** The record deposit base followed a ₹3.87 trillion spring decline — the system's funding position swung by ~₹15 trillion in under four months.
- **Data:** −₹3.87 tn (Apr 1–Jun 15); +₹11 tn (three fortnights to Jul 31); record ₹269.4 tn total deposits; the surge is attributed by traders largely to FCNR(B) dollar conversion (reported attribution, not established causality — the annotation text itself carries "attributed to").
- **Visualization type:** Two-segment schematic of system deposits, April 1 → July 31, 2026: a muted decline segment (−₹3.87 tn, with start and trough levels implied) and an accent-colour surge segment (+₹11 tn), joined at the mid-June trough. Only the sourced aggregates are drawn — the shape is schematic between the three anchored points (start, trough, record), with the segments visually marked as such, not a fabricated daily series.
- **Composition notes:** The reader's takeaway is the contrast in slope, not the level. A small annotation marks "window opens Jun 8" at the trough and a second carries the attribution wording. The record level is labeled only at the end.
- **Sources:** Reuters via ET (both flow figures and record level; attribution).

### Concept 3: Still Behind — credit 19.3%, deposits 15.4%
- **Central insight:** Even a decade-high deposit print (fastest in over a decade — provisional RBI data) leaves credit growing ~4 points faster — the funding constraint eased but did not close.
- **Data:** Credit 19.3% YoY, deposits 15.4% YoY (Jul 31, 2026); NBFC credit +32.23% YoY as fastest segment (Jun 30, 2026); gross bank credit ₹21,92,836 crore, +18.62% YoY vs +9.48% a year earlier.
- **Visualization type:** Divergence / paired-bar comparison. Two horizontal bars for the July 31 prints, with a ghost bar showing the 9.48% credit growth of a year earlier.
- **Composition notes:** The eye lands on the gap between the two bars, not the bars themselves. A one-line basis note sits directly under the ghost bar: "Year-ago comparison is against Jun 27, 2025 under RBI's revised month-end reporting convention — not strictly like-for-like." Ends with a quiet note: deposit growth must hold after Aug 31 for the gap to keep narrowing.
- **Sources:** RBI via CNBC-TV18 (all four figures, including the convention note).

### Concept 4: 2013 vs 2026 — the same lever, pulled twice
- **Central insight:** The RBI has now mobilised dollar inflows at larger scale than in the 2013 taper tantrum — but from strength, not crisis.
- **Data (consistent all-channels pairing):** 2026: $56.846 bn across FCNR(B), ECB, and OFCB channels (Jun 8–Aug 13), of which $52.3 bn FCNR(B); reserves ~$707 bn; scheme pre-emptive and closed early. 2013: ~$34 bn total across both concessional windows, Sep–Nov 2013 (~$26–27 bn via FCNR(B)); rupee in freefall, "Fragile Five" context. Figures labeled approximate on the 2013 side; pairing is total-to-total, with FCNR(B)-only figures ($52.3 bn vs ~$26–27 bn) shown as a secondary row.
- **Visualization type:** Two-column comparison card ("Then / Now"), one row per channel-basis so no mixed comparison appears.
- **Composition notes:** Numbers first, context second; the contrast is in the circumstances row (crisis defence vs pre-emptive strength), not the headline mobilisation row. Explicitly framed as perspective, not precedent.
- **Sources:** ET (2026 figures); Zolve, Finnovate, Quantum AMC (2013 figures, labeled approximate).

## What to avoid

- **A bare "15.4% — 10-year high!" stat card.** Tempting, matches the tweet, but drops the FCNR(B) context and would amplify exactly the misreading the research flags. Subsumed into Concept 1 with the context built in.
- **Any continuous growth line or weekly inflow series.** The research holds two growth prints and cumulative inflow totals only; rendering finer granularity would fabricate RBI data points. All concepts above are respecified to use only labeled points, schematic segments, and cumulative milestones.
- **Jefferies' $80–100 bn counterfactual.** An estimate of what *would have* flowed without early closure — a hypothetical, not data; visualizing it invites an anchor the RBI has already removed.
- **Rupee level chart (96.96 → 95.17).** Real figures, but the signal is about deposits, not FX; including it dilutes the single story.
- **Bank-by-bank deposit-franchise comparison.** The research has no bank-level FCNR(B) split; any such visual would be invented. Dropped.
