## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: "The $89 That Costs Like $112" (lead concept)
- Central insight: In rupee terms, today's $89 Brent costs India within 2.6% of what $112 Brent cost in May 2022 — the rupee did $23 of the damage.
- Data: Aug 17, 2026 — Brent $89 × ₹95.61 = ₹8,509/bbl; May 22, 2022 — Brent $112.16 × ₹77.82 = ₹8,728/bbl; Jan 2026 — ~$63 × ~₹90 = ~₹5,670/bbl. Delhi petrol: ₹96.72 (May 2022, post-excise-cut) vs ₹102.12 (Aug 2026, frozen).
- Visualization type: comparison — two matched "price tag" panels (2022 vs 2026) each showing Brent × USD/INR = ₹/bbl, with a shared horizontal ₹/bbl scale so the eye sees the two tags land at nearly the same height; a small third tag for Jan 2026 shows how fast the climb was.
- Composition notes: reader sees the two big rupee numbers first (₹8,509 vs ₹8,728) and assumes a typo; the eye then drops to the dollar prices ($89 vs $112) and the rupee rows (95.61 vs 77.82) to resolve the paradox. Payoff line: "Same shock, smaller number. The currency moved, not the headline."
- Sources: ET 2026-08-17; HDFC Sky 2026-08-17; Exchange-Rates.org; The Core fuel-price history; CarDekho (Delhi pump).

### Concept 2: "Two Dials, One Bill" — the sensitivity pair
- Central insight: For India's crude bill and OMC margins, a ₹1 move in the rupee now costs almost exactly what a $1 move in Brent costs — the two dials are equal in power.
- Data: +$1 Brent = +₹0.60/litre crude-linked and ~+$2B/year on the import bill; +₹1/USD at $89 = +₹0.56/litre and ~₹16,900 crore/year; bill base ~$169B at current levels on 1.8–2B barrels.
- Visualization type: ratio / balance — a two-dial gauge or a balanced scale, Brent on one side, USD/INR on the other, equal weights marked with the four sensitivity numbers.
- Composition notes: reader expects the crude dial to dominate; the near-equal weights (0.60 vs 0.56) deliver the surprise. Include the formula strip (₹/litre = Brent × USD/INR ÷ 158.99) in small type so the arithmetic is auditable. Payoff: "Nobody quotes the second dial."
- Sources: ET 2026-08-08 (volumes, $2B per $1); computed per research artifact §4 (label computations as estimates).

### Concept 3: "Where the ₹102.12 Goes" — the pump-price stack and the breakeven line
- Central insight: Strip Delhi's ₹102.12/litre down to crude, taxes, and costs, and the OMC cushion at $89/₹95.6 is roughly ₹2–6/litre — breakeven sits at Brent ≈ $92–99.
- Data: Delhi petrol ₹102.12; VAT 19.4% and excise ₹11.9 stripped → ~₹73.6 to the OMC/dealer chain; ~₹4–4.5 dealer commission; ₹53.5 crude-linked; residual ~₹15–16 shared between refining and marketing margin; breakeven band $92–99 (₹95.6) / ₹99–103 ($89). Cross-check: April's ₹24.40/L under-recovery at ~$114; May's ₹30,000 cr/month combined bleed.
- Visualization type: stacked bar (price decomposition) with a marker line — the residual block highlighted, and a thin "breakeven" band drawn across at the level where the residual hits zero.
- Composition notes: this is the operator's core ask — the breakeven arithmetic made visible. Every block labeled as fact (taxes, pump price) vs estimate (cost split, breakeven band) with a legend; the estimate legend is mandatory for compliance. Payoff: the gap between today's marker and the breakeven band is visibly thin.
- Sources: CarDekho/Business Today (pump price); ClearTax (excise, tax structure); MoPNG via Multibagg (April under-recovery); NiftyTrader (May bleed); research artifact §4 (estimate, assumptions shown).

### Concept 4: "The Gated Inflation" — pump-to-print timeline
- Central insight: Fuel inflation enters the CPI only when someone opens the gate — the May 15 hike printed in June's CPI within a month; a frozen pump means the August print (Sept 14) shows the pressure as OMC losses instead.
- Data: May 15, 2026 +₹3/L (first revision since April 2022) → CPI 3.93% (May) → 4.38% (June, sharp transport rise) → 4.45% (July); pump unchanged Aug 1–18 at ₹102.12; August CPI due Sept 14; LPG ₹53,700 cr under-recovery vs ₹30,000 cr compensation as the un-gated fiscal parallel.
- Visualization type: timeline with a gate motif — a horizontal calendar (Apr–Sep 2026), pump-price step changes as red steps, CPI prints as a rising line, a literal "gate" icon on the Aug print showing the held-back pressure rerouting to two side channels: OMC margins (down arrow) and fiscal (LPG gap bar).
- Composition notes: reader follows the one-month lag visually (May step → June jump) and then hits the frozen stretch; the rerouted arrows answer "so where did the inflation go?" Payoff: "Frozen isn't zero. It's relocated."
- Sources: Multibagg/Sahi 2026-05-15; MoSPI via Univest/Finnovate/Zee; CarDekho; Business Today 2026-02-01.

### Concept 5: "April Told Us the Answer" — the revealed breakeven
- Central insight: India already ran the experiment — April's $114 average crude produced a stated ₹24.40/litre petrol under-recovery, and the ₹3 hike plus sub-$80 crude rebuilt margins by June; that brackets the breakeven in the low-to-mid $90s.
- Data: Apr — ~$114 avg crude, ₹24.40/L under-recovery (MoPNG); May 8 — Brent $101.73, ₹30,000 cr/month bleed; May 15 — +₹3 hike; Jun — sub-$80, margin recovery flagged by JPMorgan/Kotak; Jul 8 — ceasefire collapse, $78.02; Aug 17 — $89.
- Visualization type: timeline / divergence — Brent line (Apr→Aug) against a shaded margin band (negative → recovered → compressing), with the three policy events (excise cut Mar 27, hike May 15, refinery-margin cap April) marked as vertical flags.
- Composition notes: the reader watches the margin band flip sign twice in five months and lands on today's compressing band. Payoff: the breakeven band isn't a model — it's bracketed by what already happened.
- Sources: MoPNG via Multibagg 2026-04-09; NiftyTrader 2026-05-13; Univest/PSU Connect 2026-06-22; S&P Global 2026-07-08; ET 2026-08-17.

## What to avoid

- **A "vessels stranded" Hormuz map** (419 vessels, Straits monitor): visually tempting, but the count comes from a Tier 3 machine tracker, throughput context is unknown, and the signal's wedge is arithmetic, not geopolitics. Dropped.
- **Anything using the ₹104.99/L diesel under-recovery or the ₹2.07 lakh/kl ATF figure**: both disputed (MoPNG basis unclear; IOC issued a correction). Excluded from all concepts.
- **A "rupee crash" chart framed around the March 99.82 print**: conflicts with the 96.566 verified record; would assert an unverified number. Dropped.
- **OMC stock-price panels**: would read as advisory-adjacent; the concepts stay on costs, prices, and fiscal flows.
- **Import-bill "+61%" framing (KNN headline)**: contradicts Commerce Ministry data (+26%); the correct number is less dramatic and is the one to use.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with Concepts 1, 2, and 3 as a set: Concept 1 is the hook (the 2022 equivalence), Concept 2 is the mechanism (equal dials), Concept 3 is the payoff (the breakeven line and the thin cushion). Concept 4 is a strong alternative if a timeline format is preferred over the price stack. Concept 5 works best as supporting evidence inside Concept 3 rather than standalone. All estimated figures (breakeven band, per-litre sensitivities, cost split) must carry the estimate legend and the stated assumptions from research artifact §4.
