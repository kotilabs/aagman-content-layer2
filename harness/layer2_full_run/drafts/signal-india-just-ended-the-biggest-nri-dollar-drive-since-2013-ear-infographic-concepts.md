## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: "Closed a month early — because it worked too well, or cost too much?"
- Central insight: The RBI preponed the cutoff of its biggest dollar drive since 2013 after $56.85bn came in — and the two readings of that decision (oversubscription success vs. carry-cost discomfort) are both visible in the same four numbers.
- Data: $56.85bn total as of Aug 13 (CNBC-TV18); mobilisation velocity $20bn/38d → $20bn/14d → $16bn/13d (NDTV Profit/SBI Research); Governor's Aug 5 "no premature closure" vs. Aug 14 announcement (Moneycontrol); USD/INR 95.39 → 95.45, −0.2% over the scheme's life (Moneycontrol).
- Visualization type: divergence — a dual-axis timeline. Left axis: cumulative inflow curve (Jun 8 → Aug 13, with the three velocity legs annotated and steepening). Right axis: USD/INR line, essentially flat across the same window. Two event flags pierce both series: Aug 5 (Governor rules out early closure) and Aug 14 (closure announced).
- Composition notes: The reader's eye lands first on the steepening inflow curve — the success story. Then it drags right along the flat rupee line and hits the two flags nine days apart. The payoff is the tension itself: inflows accelerating, currency unmoved, assurance reversed. A small caption strip at the bottom carries the two interpretations as competing labels, not a verdict: "Target overshot" / "Hedge leg got expensive."
- Sources: CNBC-TV18 (2026-08-14); NDTV Profit (2026-08-15); Moneycontrol (2026-08-17).

### Concept 2: "How the RBI became the hedge leg"
- Central insight: The swap was tenor-matched and at par, so the RBI absorbed the entire forward-hedging cost — every swapped dollar is an RBI forward liability, and tens of billions now mature in a cluster in 2029–2031.
- Data: swap mechanics (dollars to RBI today, rupees to bank, dollars returned at the same rate in 3–5 years); $52.3bn swapped FCNR(B) deposits as of Aug 13 (CNBC-TV18); 2029–2031 maturity cluster (research mechanism section); ~Rs 3 lakh crore average surplus liquidity and 15.4% deposit growth as the rupee-leg consequence (Moneycontrol/NDTV Profit).
- Visualization type: flow chart with a time dimension. Top half, "Today (Jun–Aug 2026)": NRI dollar → authorised-dealer bank → RBI (at-par swap), with the rupee leg flowing back into the banking system ($52.3bn one way, ~Rs 3 lakh crore surplus the other). Bottom half, "2029–2031": the arrow reverses — banks return rupees, RBI must deliver the dollars back at the same rate regardless of where USD/INR trades.
- Composition notes: The eye follows the money clockwise: dollar in, rupee out, then — the payoff — the greyed-out future panel where the flow reverses into the maturity cluster. A single annotation marks what makes this swap different from 2013's: "at par, tenor-matched — RBI holds the hedge." No cost number is invented; the visual simply makes the invisible liability visible.
- Sources: CNBC-TV18 (2026-08-14); Moneycontrol (2026-08-17); NDTV Profit (2026-08-15).

### Concept 3: "2013 vs 2026 — same instrument, twice the size, different price"
- Central insight: The 2026 drive mobilised roughly twice the 2013 FCNR(B) haul in two-thirds the time, but where 2013 fixed its subsidy at 3.5%, 2026's at-par structure leaves the true cost open until the rupee's path is known.
- Data: 2013 — ~$34bn total, ~$26bn FCNR(B), 3.5% p.a. concessional (~3.0% below market), effective all-in ~5.00% for 3y dollars (~4.35% over USTs), window ran to its announced November end (Moneycontrol/Narayan); 2026 — $52.3bn FCNR(B) in ten weeks, rates up to 7.8% offered after the ceiling removal, window closed a month early (CNBC-TV18/Moneycontrol).
- Visualization type: comparison — two side-by-side vertical panels with matched scales. Paired bars: total mobilised ($34bn vs $56.85bn), FCNR(B) share ($26bn vs $52.3bn), duration (~3 months vs 10 weeks). Below the bars, a "who pays the hedge" row: 2013 "fixed 3.5% — cost known on day one" vs 2026 "at par — cost known only at maturity."
- Composition notes: Symmetry is the design device — identical layouts force the eye to compare cell by cell. The payoff cell is the last row: the left panel's closed number (5.00%) against the right panel's open one (a dashed outline, labelled "depends on USD/INR at maturity"). Educational framing only; no "better/worse" colouring.
- Sources: Moneycontrol / Ananth Narayan, SPJIMR (2022-07-26); CNBC-TV18 (2026-08-14); Moneycontrol (2026-08-17).

### Concept 4: "The engineering tells — read the plumbing, not the press release"
- Central insight: A week before closing the window, the RBI amended its PSL directions to ring-fence credit extended against these deposits — and that quiet operational notice, plus reported leverage of 9x–19x, says more about the closure's motive than the official reason does.
- Data: Aug 7 PSL exclusion terms (fresh 3–5y FCNR(B)/NRE deposits out of Adjusted Net Bank Credit, effective immediately, capped at the CRR/SLR-exempt amount) (Moneycontrol, 2026-08-07); leverage range ~9x to ~19x, labelled market-reported (Finnovate); the 2013 lesson — much of that "$26bn in NRI money" was overseas bank money lent to NRIs (Moneycontrol/Narayan); $31.2bn of reserves recouped by Aug 7 (NDTV Profit).
- Visualization type: annotated timeline. A single horizontal line from Jun 8 (facility opens) to Dec 31 (ECB/OFCB leg still open), with dated event markers: Jun 8 open → Jul 17 $17.4bn → Jul 31 $36.7bn → Aug 5 assurance → Aug 7 PSL ring-fence → Aug 13 $56.85bn → Aug 14 closure → Aug 31 mobilisation end → Sep 11 swap end. The Aug 7 marker is visually weighted (larger, distinct) because it is the tell hiding in plain sight.
- Composition notes: The reader scans a familiar chronology until the eye catches the oddly-placed Aug 7 marker — a lending-regulation amendment sitting inside a deposit-drive story. A callout explains what PSL ring-fencing does in one sentence. The payoff is juxtaposition: the public line ("strong response") dated Aug 14 against the plumbing action dated Aug 7. All market-reported figures carry their estimate labels on the graphic itself.
- Sources: Moneycontrol (2026-08-07); Moneycontrol (2026-08-17); Finnovate (2026-08-17, market-reported); NDTV Profit (2026-08-15); Moneycontrol/Narayan (2022-07-26).

## What to avoid

- **The $106.6bn forward book as a headline visual.** It is the most dramatic number in the file and the weakest-sourced — market-reported, not RBI-published. It appears in Concepts 1 and 4 only as labelled context, never as a standalone graphic. A full infographic built on it would be claim-vs-data misalignment.
- **"Rupee at record lows" drama chart.** The May 96.14/96.34 prints are real, but a standalone collapse chart is urgency bait and pre-dates the scheme; it belongs only as backdrop framing, which Concept 1 already provides through the flat in-scheme line.
- **SBI's $80–85bn final projection as a visual.** It is a forecast, not a fact, and visualising a projection next to realised flows invites misreading. Kept out of all concepts; usable only as a text footnote.
- **Leveraged-return figures (17–27%).** Second-hand, structure-dependent, and easily read as a promise of returns. Dropped entirely.
- **Anything implying what NRIs, banks, or investors should do.** All four concepts are educational: mechanism, history, and tension. No advice, no urgency, no "what this means for your deposits."

## Final recommendation

Proceed with Concepts 1, 2, 3, and 4. Concept 1 is the flagship (success vs. carry-cost tension, all hard data). Concept 2 is the mechanism explainer with the longest shelf life. Concept 3 supplies the historical-perspective mandate with fully sourced 2013 numbers. Concept 4 is the most editorially distinctive — the PSL ring-fence as the tell — and is safe provided the leverage figures stay labelled as market-reported.
