## Instagram Carousel — The Earnings Season That Didn't Crack

**India's June-quarter results just came in strong across the board. The most important number of the season is the one nobody published.**

---

### Slide 1

By August 14, the last big results were in, and the tally looked like a relief. Listed non-financial companies had grown net sales 22.5% year on year, nearly double the previous quarter's pace. HSBC's count showed 73% of results in line with expectations or better, near the top of the band it has tracked since 2020. Through an energy shock and a record-weak rupee, corporate India had held.

---

### Slide 2

Then you open the footnote. Aggregate manufacturing operating profit came in below last year, until you remove one sector. Strip out petroleum products and the same dataset shows growth of 20.2%. Both readings come from the same table. A single sector had flipped the sign of the entire manufacturing profit pool.

---

### Slide 3

This season was about revisions, not beats. A beats count measures results against expectations set before the season began. Revision breadth measures what analysts did to full-year numbers after seeing them. A company can clear a lowered bar and still watch its future get cut.

---

### Slide 4

Since June-end, the upgrades clustered in specific places: basic materials, financials, industrials, consumer staples. Underneath them sits one engine, non-food credit growing 18.3% year on year in June, against roughly 10% in late 2025. The upgrades have a checkable source.

---

### Slide 5

The downgrades landed in consumption's other half: consumer discretionary, health care, utilities. Staples were upgraded in the same weeks, and Titan, Hero MotoCorp and Tata Motors' commercial vehicles posted strong quarters. Premium demand held while the mass end took the cuts.

---

### Slide 6

In IT the gap is sharpest. TCS won $9.5 billion of deals in the quarter, and revenue barely moved. Infosys narrowed its FY27 growth guidance to 1.5–3% after posting double-digit growth. Outcome-based AI contracts change when revenue is recognised, not how much exists. Deal wins and reported revenue have stopped moving together.

---

### Slide 7

The stakes sit in the flows. The Nifty trades about 15% below its January peak. Foreign investors sold a record ₹2.87 lakh crore by mid-June, then turned: plus ₹17,227 crore in July, more in August. That returning capital underwrites revisions, not beats.

---

### Slide 8

So did the market-wide FY27 estimate actually rise or fall? Nobody has published the net count. Upgrades in four sectors, downgrades in three, and the consensus still assumes roughly 14% growth for 2026 and 17% for 2027. The aggregate sits uncomputed in public.

---

### Slide 9

You can compute it yourself, roughly. Watch the September estimate revisions from the big brokerages. If FY27 earnings estimates for the Nifty keep drifting down while the season "beat", the market is underwriting a story the spreadsheets have already left. If estimates stabilise, the season was as good as its headline.

---

### Slide 10

The season held. The scoring method is the question now, and the re-scoring happens quietly, in revision tables, over the next two quarters. Read those, not the wrap-ups.
