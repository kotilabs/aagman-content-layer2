## Instagram Carousel — "The Earnings Season That Didn't Crack"

**Surface:** carousel_instagram (standalone) · **Slides:** 10 · **Signal:** the-earnings-season-that-didn-t-crack-what-q1fy27-actually-s

---

### Slide 1
- **Kicker:** Q1FY27, WRAPPED
- **Headline:** The earnings season didn't crack. The scorecard did.
- **Body:** India's June-quarter results season is over. The aggregate reads beautifully — and the aggregate is the least interesting number in it.
- **Hero stat:** 22.5% — YoY net sales growth, listed non-financial companies (CMIE, Aug 14, 2026)
- **Design note:** Dark background, single oversized "22.5%" in cream; small red crack line running under the number, not through it.
- **Speaker note:** The hook names the shift — from reading the headline number to questioning how the season is being scored.

### Slide 2
- **Kicker:** THE GOOD PRINT
- **Headline:** The top line held. It more than held.
- **Body:** Non-financial sales grew 22.5% YoY, up from 11.7% the previous quarter. HSBC counts 73% of results in-line or better — near the top of its 50–75% band tracked since 2020. Demand survived an energy shock.
- **Hero stat:** 73% of Q1FY27 results met or beat expectations (HSBC, Aug 7, 2026)
- **Design note:** Two-column stat block: "22.5%" vs "73%", both sourced in fine print beneath.
- **Speaker note:** Establish the bull case honestly and at full strength before touching the footnote.

### Slide 3
- **Kicker:** THE FOOTNOTE
- **Headline:** Manufacturing profit fell. Until you remove one sector.
- **Body:** Aggregate manufacturing operating profit came in *below* last year. Strip out petroleum products and the same dataset shows +20.2% growth. Both statements are true. Only one is useful.
- **Hero stat:** +20.2% — manufacturing operating profit growth ex-petroleum, vs a decline with it (CMIE via Moneycontrol, Aug 15, 2026)
- **Design note:** Split-screen visual: left side grey with a down arrow labelled "with petroleum", right side green with "+20.2%" labelled "without".
- **Speaker note:** Introduce margin dispersion through the petroleum distortion — the season in miniature.

### Slide 4
- **Kicker:** THE PIVOT
- **Headline:** This isn't a beats story. It's a revisions story.
- **Body:** A beats count measures results against expectations set before the season. Revision breadth measures what analysts did to FY27 numbers after seeing them. A company can beat a lowered bar and still get its future cut.
- **Hero stat:** "73% in-line or better" describes the past. Revision breadth describes the future. (HSBC note, Aug 7, 2026 — beats; author's framing — revisions)
- **Design note:** Minimal — one line of text on near-empty background. Let the pivot breathe.
- **Speaker note:** The explicit thesis slide; everything before was evidence, everything after is the map.

### Slide 5
- **Kicker:** WHERE UPGRADES WENT
- **Headline:** Metals, banks, factories, staples.
- **Body:** Since June-end, full-year estimate upgrades clustered in basic materials, financials, industrials and consumer staples. The mechanism underneath: non-food credit growing 18.3% YoY in June — against roughly 10% in late 2025.
- **Hero stat:** 18.3% — non-food credit growth, June 2026 (HSBC, Aug 7, 2026)
- **Design note:** Four labelled tiles (commodities / financials / industrials / staples) with up-arrows; credit-growth sparkline below.
- **Speaker note:** Show that positive revision breadth has a identifiable engine — input costs and the credit cycle.

### Slide 6
- **Kicker:** WHERE DOWNGRADES LANDED
- **Headline:** The cuts went to consumption's other half.
- **Body:** The largest downward revisions hit consumer discretionary, health care and utilities — even as staples got upgraded and Titan, Hero MotoCorp and Tata Motors CV posted strong quarters. Premium demand held. The mass-discretionary estimate base is where the red ink sits.
- **Hero stat:** Downgrade sectors: consumer discretionary, health care, utilities (HSBC, Aug 7, 2026)
- **Design note:** Same tile layout as Slide 5, flipped — three down-arrow tiles; contrast does the storytelling.
- **Speaker note:** Complicate "consumption is resilient" — the split is the finding, not a caveat.

### Slide 7
- **Kicker:** THE IT PARADOX
- **Headline:** TCS won $9.5 bn of deals. Revenue barely moved.
- **Body:** Outcome-based AI contracts change *when* revenue is recognised, not just how much exists. Infosys narrowed FY27 growth guidance to 1.5–3% after a double-digit-growth quarter. Brokerages now warn AI-deflationary deals could pressure revenue for years.
- **Hero stat:** $9.5 bn TCS Q1 deal wins; Infosys FY27 guidance narrowed to 1.5–3% (News18, Jul 9; CNBC-TV18, Jul 23, 2026)
- **Design note:** A full pipeline funnel graphic feeding into a visibly narrower revenue pipe.
- **Speaker note:** The structural case — IT's issue is the revenue model, not the quarter.

### Slide 8
- **Kicker:** ONE LINE
- **Headline:** The pipeline and the P&L now measure different things.
- **Body:** (Empty by design.)
- **Hero stat:** — 
- **Design note:** Text-only slide, maximum whitespace, headline centred. The pause in the deck's rhythm.
- **Speaker note:** A sparse slide to let the IT mechanism land before the market-context slide adds stakes.

### Slide 9
- **Kicker:** WHY IT MATTERS
- **Headline:** A market down 15% is underwriting this season.
- **Body:** The Nifty sits 15.2% below its January peak. Foreign investors sold a record ~Rs 2.87 lakh crore by mid-June — then returned: +Rs 17,227 crore in July, +Rs 12,921 crore more in August. Returning capital doesn't underwrite beats. It underwrites revisions.
- **Hero stat:** Rs 2.87 lakh crore — record FPI outflow by mid-June 2026 (NSDL via Business Standard); streak broke in July (BW Businessworld, Jul 30, 2026)
- **Design note:** Nifty drawdown line from January peak to ~24,400, with FPI flow bars turning green at July beneath it.
- **Speaker note:** Attach consequences — the earnings-resilience claim is load-bearing precisely because positioning is fragile.

### Slide 10
- **Kicker:** THE OPEN QUESTION
- **Headline:** Nobody has published the net count.
- **Body:** Did FY27 upgrades outnumber downgrades across the whole market, or did the aggregate estimate quietly fall? That number — revision breadth, not beats — decides whether returning foreign money finds confirmation or a trap. Watch the revisions.
- **Hero stat:** Known: upgrades in 4 sectors, downgrades in 3. Unknown: the net. (HSBC via Moneycontrol, Aug 7, 2026)
- **Design note:** A ledger page with two columns — one visibly longer than the other, totals blurred out.
- **Speaker note:** Close on the unresolved variable the whole argument turns on; complete story, hanging question, no CTA.
