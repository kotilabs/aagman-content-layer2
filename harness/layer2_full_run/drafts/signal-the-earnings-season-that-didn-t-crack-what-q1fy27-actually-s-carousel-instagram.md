## Instagram Carousel — The Earnings Season That Didn't Crack

---

### Slide 1

**The earnings season didn't crack. The scorecard did.**

India's June-quarter results season is over, and the aggregate reads beautifully: non-financial net sales up 22.5% year on year, and 73% of results in line or better.

But the aggregate is the least interesting number in this season. The interesting one is hiding in a footnote.

---

### Slide 2

First, give the good print its due.

Sales growth of 22.5% is up from 11.7% the previous quarter. HSBC's count of in-line-or-better results sits near the top of the band it has tracked since 2020. Through an energy price shock and a weak rupee, demand held.

Whatever comes next in this story, this part is true.

---

### Slide 3

Now the footnote.

Aggregate manufacturing operating profit came in below last year. Strip out petroleum products, and the same dataset shows growth of 20.2%.

Both statements are true. Only one is useful. A single sector flipped the sign of the entire manufacturing profit pool — which tells you the season's story is dispersion, not direction.

---

### Slide 4

That is why this isn't a beats story. It's a revisions story.

A beats count measures results against expectations set before the season. Revision breadth measures what analysts did to full-year numbers after seeing them. A company can beat a lowered bar and still get its future cut.

The 73% figure describes the past. Revisions describe the future.

---

### Slide 5

Since June-end, upgrades clustered in basic materials, financials, industrials and consumer staples.

Underneath that is a specific engine: non-food credit growing 18.3% year on year in June, against roughly 10% in late 2025. Input costs and the credit cycle are doing the work — and the upgrades have an identifiable, checkable source.

---

### Slide 6

The cuts went to consumption's other half.

The largest downward revisions hit consumer discretionary, health care and utilities — even as staples were upgraded and premium demand held. "Consumption is resilient" and "consumption is being downgraded" are both true, of different Indias.

And in IT: TCS won $9.5 billion of deals while revenue barely moved. Infosys narrowed FY27 guidance to 1.5–3% after a double-digit-growth quarter. Outcome-based AI contracts change when revenue is recognised, not just how much exists. The pipeline and the P&L now measure different things.

---

### Slide 7

Here is why any of this matters.

The Nifty sits about 15% below its January peak. Foreign investors sold a record roughly ₹2.87 lakh crore by mid-June — then returned: plus ₹17,227 crore in July, and more in August.

Returning capital doesn't underwrite beats. It underwrites revisions. The earnings-resilience claim is load-bearing precisely because positioning is fragile.

---

### Slide 8

So here is the number nobody has published.

Did FY27 upgrades outnumber downgrades across the whole market, or did the aggregate estimate quietly fall? Known: upgrades in four sectors, downgrades in three. Unknown: the net.
