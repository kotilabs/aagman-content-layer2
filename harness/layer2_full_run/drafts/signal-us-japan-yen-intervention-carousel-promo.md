# Promo Carousel: Why Did the US Join the Yen Intervention?

**Source blog:** `drafts/signal-us-japan-yen-intervention-blog.md`
**Slides:** 10
**Voice:** FT leader column meets Matt Levine. Dry, declarative, numbers first.

---

## Slide 1 — The Hook

- **Kicker:** 31 July 2026
- **Headline:** The US Treasury just bought yen. It had not done that in 28 years.
- **Body:** Japan's currency hit a 40-year low, Tokyo spent roughly $59bn in a single day defending it — and then Washington joined in. First joint yen purchase since 1998. The question isn't what they did. It's why the US bothered.
- **Hero stat:** ¥163.99 — the lowest reported intraday print, weakest yen since 1986 (thesun.my, 3 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Opens with the shift, not the topic — the anomaly is US participation, so the headline names it.

---

## Slide 2 — The Trigger

- **Kicker:** The setup
- **Headline:** The yen fell for a simple reason: 1.0% versus 3.50–3.75%.
- **Body:** The Bank of Japan's rate is the highest since 1995. The Fed's is 250–275 basis points higher. Borrow yen cheap, buy dollars, earn the spread — the carry trade. That gap pushed the yen down for years, through every solo intervention Tokyo tried.
- **Hero stat:** ~250–275bp — the US–Japan rate gap driving the carry trade (OMFIF, 8 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Establishes the mechanical cause before introducing the response, so intervention reads as symptom-treatment.

---

## Slide 3 — Japan Went First, Alone

- **Kicker:** Thursday, 30 July
- **Headline:** Tokyo sold as much as $58.97bn in one day. Reportedly the largest single-day currency defense on record.
- **Body:** Bank of Japan data showed the scale of the solo operation in New York hours. Japan's war chest is its foreign reserves — and those reserves sit largely in US Treasuries. Remember that. It's the whole story.
- **Hero stat:** $58.97bn — Japan's solo yen-buying, 30 July (BoJ data via SunCrypto, 5 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Plants the reserves-in-Treasuries fact as a deliberate cliffhanger for the mechanism slide.

---

## Slide 4 — Then Washington Joined

- **Kicker:** Friday, 31 July
- **Headline:** The US leg was small, funded in euros, and photographed before it was announced.
- **Body:** The New York Fed sold euros — not dollars — to buy yen, executed through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley (FT, confirmed by Reuters). The size emerged via a Reuters photo of Treasury Secretary Bessent's notepad at Camp David: "To Do: Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil."
- **Hero stat:** "$5–10 bil" — the to-do list item on Bessent's notepad (Reuters photo, 2 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Concrete operational detail does the work — euros and the notepad are the two facts readers will repeat.

---

## Slide 5 — Why Euros Matter

- **Kicker:** The design choice
- **Headline:** Selling dollars would have said "we want a weaker dollar." Selling euros said nothing of the kind.
- **Body:** The Treasury funded its leg out of the Exchange Stabilization Fund's euro holdings. That supports the yen without adding dollar supply — and caps the operation at the size of the euro stockpile. Hence billions, not tens of billions. The Federal Reserve, which split past US interventions 50/50, apparently sat this one out entirely (OMFIF).
- **Hero stat:** 2 — prior US currency interventions this century (2000, 2011), both split Treasury/Fed 50/50 (OMFIF, 8 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Reads the funding choice as deliberate posture — a signal constrained on purpose.

---

## Slide 6 — The Pivot

- **Kicker:** The real reason
- **Headline:** The strongest reading: this is bond-market defense, not currency policy.
- **Body:** Defending the yen costs dollars. Japan raises dollars by selling US Treasuries — and Japan is one of the largest foreign holders of US debt. Every unilateral intervention is new Treasury supply into a market where the 10-year yield has already climbed from 4.1% to 4.6% this year.
- **Hero stat:** 4.1% → 4.6% — the 10-year Treasury yield, early 2026 to July (OMFIF; CRFB)
- **Speaker note:** The thesis slide — names the argument explicitly, in the "This isn't X. It's Y." form.

---

## Slide 7 — The Plumbing

- **Kicker:** Enter FIMA
- **Headline:** The day after intervening, both sides moved so Japan can finance the next yen defense without selling Treasuries into a stressed market.
- **Body:** Japan's finance ministry announced it will finance future interventions through the Fed's FIMA repo facility — pledging Treasuries as collateral for dollar loans instead of selling them. Bessent publicly called for the facility to be expanded. An emergency Covid-era tool is becoming standing intervention infrastructure — with limits: $60bn per institution per day, priced above private repo. Enough for episodic defense, not a currency war.
- **Hero stat:** $60bn/day — the FIMA repo cap per institution (Bloomingbit, 5 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The tell — the FIMA move is the strongest evidence for the bond-defense thesis.

---

## Slide 8 — The Tolerance Signal

- **Kicker:** What it reveals
- **Headline:** The US accepted a weaker dollar against the yen because a weaker yen cost it more.
- **Body:** Bessent called the yen "very undervalued" and warned it could drag down other Asian currencies. Trump called it "a signal of friendship." Both can be true. Neither explains why the help was structured to keep Treasuries off the market. A 4.6% ten-year yield is now a constraint Washington will spend euro reserves on.
- **Hero stat:** 5.1%+ — the 30-year Treasury yield, 21 July 2026 (CRFB)
- **Speaker note:** Converts the mechanism into the interpretive payoff — tolerance for dollar strength has a price, and we just saw it.

---

## Slide 9 — What It Doesn't Fix

- **Kicker:** The catch
- **Headline:** The yen bounced six yen. Then it started drifting back.
- **Body:** From ¥164 to ¥155.23 in two sessions — then back toward ¥158 within a week. The BoJ held at 1.0% the same week (8–1, one dissent for a hike). The next decision is 17–18 September. UBS's verdict: the yen is now "supported more by intervention risk than by domestic monetary fundamentals." Traders aren't buying yen. They're declining to short it.
- **Hero stat:** ¥163.99 → ¥155.23 → ~¥158 — bounce and partial retrace in one week (163.99 print: thesun.my, 3 Aug; 157/158 legs: OMFIF, 8 Aug 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The honesty slide — separates what intervention did from what it can't do.

## Slide 10 — The Open Question + CTA

- **Kicker:** What to watch
- **Headline:** The yen floor now rests on two governments' willingness to keep buying it.
- **Body:** Intervention risk is a workable position. It is not a stable one. If US yields keep rising for their own domestic reasons, how much more dollar strength is this Treasury willing to give up — and whose currency is next on the notepad?
- **Hero stat:** 17–18 September — the BoJ meeting that could change the math (Jiji Press, 11 Aug 2026)
