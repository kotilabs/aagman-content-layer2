## Research Artifact: The Earnings Season That Didn't Crack — What Q1FY27 Actually Showed Under the Index

**Signal ID:** the-earnings-season-that-didn-t-crack-what-q1fy27-actually-s
**Date of research:** 2026-08-18
**Editorial wedge:** Earnings Interpretation (revision breadth and margin dispersion, not beats/misses)

---

### 1. Signal restatement

India's Q1FY27 earnings season (April–June 2026 quarter, reported July–mid-August 2026) is effectively over, and the headline reads well: aggregate net sales for listed non-financial companies grew 22.5% year-on-year per CMIE data as of August 14, and HSBC counts 73% of results as in-line or better. But the surface resilience sits on top of a more complicated interior. Aggregate manufacturing operating profits actually fell year-on-year once you include petroleum; excluding petroleum they grew 20.2%. Estimate upgrades since June-end are clustering in basic materials, financials, industrials and consumer staples, while downgrades are landing in consumer discretionary, health care and utilities — and IT faces a structural repricing question from AI-deflationary contracts. This matters now because the equity market is in correction territory (Nifty ~15% off its January 2026 peak) after the largest FPI selling year on record, and the bull case for a floor rests almost entirely on the claim that domestic earnings are resilient. The season tested that claim and returned a conditional answer: aggregates held, dispersion widened.

### 4. Mechanism

Three mechanisms matter for reading this season correctly.

**1. Beats/misses vs revision breadth.** A beats count (HSBC's 73%) measures results against expectations as they stood at the *start* of the season. Revision breadth measures what analysts did to forward (FY27/FY28) numbers *after* seeing the results. A company can beat a lowered bar and still see its forward estimates cut — and the aggregate beats count will not register it. This season, the upgrades are concentrated where input costs or credit cycles are moving favourably (metals/basic materials on commodity tailwinds, financials on 18.3% credit growth), while downgrades are landing where demand or pricing is questioned (consumer discretionary, health care, utilities) — and IT sits in a special category where the issue is not this quarter's print but the revenue model itself.

**2. Margin dispersion and the petroleum distortion.** Aggregate manufacturing operating profit fell YoY, but that aggregate is dominated by petroleum/refining, where crude and product-price swings can swamp the rest of the sector. Ex-petroleum, operating profit grew 20.2%. This is the season in miniature: the same dataset supports "margins fell" and "margins expanded" depending on one sector's weight. Margin *dispersion* — the spread between sectors gaining from input-cost relief (softening copra for Marico, commodity tailwinds for metals) and sectors facing demand softness or pricing pressure — widened even as the aggregate stayed roughly intact.

**3. AI repricing of IT revenue.** Outcome-based AI contracts change *when* revenue is recognised, not just how much there is. A deflationary deal can carry a healthy pipeline number while compressing near-term billed revenue and margins. That is why TCS can post $9.5 bn of deal wins and still see flat dollar revenue conversion flagged by brokerages, and why Infosys can narrow guidance after a "double-digit growth" quarter. The pipeline and the P&L are measuring different things now.

**The market context makes this load-bearing.** After a record ~Rs 2.87 lakh crore of FPI selling by mid-June and a 15% index drawdown, domestic earnings resilience is the remaining pillar of the equity story. July–August saw FPI flows turn positive, which raises the stakes further: returning foreign capital will re-underwrite the earnings claim, and it underwrites revisions, not beats.

### 5. Competing interpretations

**Interpretation A — "Earnings resilience is real; buy the correction."**
- Who: HSBC's framing (earnings as one leg of a broader India case alongside positioning and relative volatility); domestic-bull commentary around the July FPI turn.
- Supporting evidence: 22.5% non-financial sales growth; 73% in-line-or-better; ex-petroleum manufacturing operating profit +20.2%; credit growth 18.3%; PV sales +15–25%; upgrades in four sectors.
- Contradicting evidence: consensus growth (~14%/17%) still trails Korea/Taiwan; HSBC itself flags further downgrades later in the year; MoSPI survey shows private capex intentions falling.
- Why it might be wrong: it leans on the beats count and the ex-petroleum restatement; if IT AI deflation or consumption softness forces a second leg of FY27 cuts, "the market will look through downgrades" becomes a much harder claim at current valuations.

**Interpretation B — "Aggregates held but earnings quality deteriorated; dispersion is the story."**
- Who: the operator's editorial angle; consistent with the Weekender's own petroleum footnote and the IT warnings.
- Supporting evidence: headline manufacturing operating profit down YoY; downgrades concentrated in consumer discretionary/health care/utilities; Infosys guidance trim; brokerage warnings of multi-year AI deflation in IT.
- Contradicting evidence: ex-petroleum profits grew 20.2% — most of the "deterioration" is one volatile sector; staples (a consumption proxy) saw upgrades, not downgrades.
- Why it might be wrong: "quality" is doing a lot of work. If the petroleum drag normalises and credit-led financials keep delivering, the dispersion story could resolve upward rather than downward.

**Interpretation C — "This is a sector rotation, not an earnings cycle."**
- Who: implied by HSBC's sector pattern (materials/financials/industrials up; discretionary/health care/utilities down) and the SMID-over-large-cap growth expectation.
- Supporting evidence: upgrades and downgrades are cleanly sorted by input-cost and credit-cycle exposure, not by a uniform demand shock; SMIDs carry stronger net-income growth expectations than large-caps.
- Contradicting evidence: IT's AI repricing is not a rotation — it is a structural change to a sector that is a large index weight; capex-order-book strength (HAL, PowerGrid) coexists with falling aggregate capex intentions, which is a level story, not rotation.
- Why it might be wrong: rotation framing understates the possibility that the index-level FY27 number is simply too high.

**Interpretation D — "The season is a placeholder; the AI/energy/jobs test hasn't arrived yet."**
- Who: the Weekender's own editorial conclusion ("the real test ... is only beginning").
- Supporting evidence: outcome-based AI contracting is early; Nvidia-centred global capex chain is a single point of sentiment failure; Middle East energy/logistics pressure was absorbed this quarter but not necessarily next.
- Contradicting evidence: markets price what's visible; dismissing a 22.5% sales print as a placeholder ignores real current demand.
- Why it might be wrong: it risks being unfalsifiable — every season can be called a placeholder.

### 6. Company and sector implications

- **IT services (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Tech Mahindra):** downgrade risk is structural, not cyclical. Infosys already narrowed FY27 guidance to 1.5–3% CC; brokerages turned cautious post-Q1. Channel: outcome-based AI contracts → delayed/deflated revenue recognition → estimate cuts even with healthy deal pipelines. Second-order: IT is a large Nifty weight and a middle-class employment engine, so IT estimate cuts transmit to consumption expectations.
- **Metals / basic materials (Hindalco and peers):** upgrade cluster. Channel: commodity/input-cost tailwinds plus global metals pricing; FPI sector-flow data shows metals among July's inflow leaders. Watch: the same commodity beta cuts both ways.
- **Financials (banks, NBFCs):** upgrades supported by 18.3% non-food credit growth (June 2026) — a sharp acceleration from ~10% in late 2025. Second-order: gold-loan NBFCs benefit from high gold prices; public banks face margin questions as loan pricing transparency rises (Moneycontrol's own sector coverage flags both).
- **Consumption is split, not uniformly weak:** staples upgraded (Marico's volume growth with copra tailwinds), discretionary downgraded per HSBC — yet Titan, Hero MotoCorp and Tata Motors CV posted strong quarters. The honest read: premium/premiumising demand held; the mass-discretionary estimate base is where cuts are landing.
- **Capex/industrials:** HAL (~Rs 90,000 crore expected FY27–28 inflows), Bharat Forge (defence/aerospace pivot), Kaynes (OSAT/space/defence), PowerGrid (Rs 1.75-lakh-crore work-in-hand, AI/EV-pulled grid demand), Blue Star (data-centre pipeline). Counterweight: MoSPI's survey of *aggregate* private new-asset capex falling in FY27 — breadth is widening while the aggregate stays cautious.
- **Health care and utilities:** quietly the largest downgrade sectors per HSBC — under-covered in most wrap-ups.
- **Index level:** with the Nifty ~15% off its January peak and FPIs tentatively returning (+Rs 30,000+ crore across July–August), the revision breadth in the sectors above determines whether returning flows find confirmation or a trap.

### 7. Historical analogues or structural memory

**Analogue 1: The FY13–FY14 earnings season around the taper tantrum (2013).**
- Similarity: heavy foreign outflows and a falling currency forced the market onto a domestic-fundamentals claim; aggregate earnings held up better than feared while dispersion between leveraged/cyclical and defensive sectors widened.
- Difference: in 2013 the macro shock (rates, currency) was the driver; in 2026 the index stress is valuation-and-flows driven while the macro data (credit, PV sales, GDP ~6.4% IMF estimate) is improving. IT's business-model question has no 2013 equivalent.
- Use: perspective, not prediction — it shows that "aggregates held" during a flow crisis is necessary but not sufficient; the market stabilised only when revisions stopped falling, not when beats were counted.

**Analogue 2: The FY20 pre-COVID downgrade grind (2019–20).**
- Similarity: a long stretch where each season's beats count looked acceptable while forward estimates were cut for consecutive quarters — revision breadth was negative long before the aggregate EPS number admitted it. Financials eventually led the repair when credit costs normalised.
- Difference: today's downgrade set (discretionary, health care, utilities) is narrower, and credit growth is accelerating rather than decelerating; 2019 had a consumption-and-NBFC credit crunch, 2026 has an 18.3% credit expansion.
- Use: it is the cleanest demonstration that beats/misses counts lag revision breadth as a signal — exactly the analytic point of this piece.

### 8. What is known, unknown, and unknowable

**Known (source-attributed):**
- Aggregate Q1FY27 sales growth of 22.5% (CMIE, Aug 14) and the petroleum-driven margin split (−YoY aggregate, +20.2% ex-petroleum).
- 73% in-line-or-better (HSBC) and the specific upgrade/downgrade sector lists.
- Consensus growth expectations (~14% 2026, ~17% 2027) and their position below Korea/Taiwan.
- Credit growth (18.3% June), PV sales swing, FPI flow magnitudes and the July turn, the Nifty's 15.2% drawdown.
- Infosys guidance narrowing and TCS deal wins/conversion lag; brokerage AI-deflation warnings.

**Unknown (knowable but not yet available):**
- Full-season revision *breadth* numbers (net upgrades minus downgrades across the coverage universe) — HSBC's note describes direction by sector, but a complete count across all listed companies is not public in the sources reviewed.
- Whether petroleum/refining margins normalise in Q2FY27, which mechanically decides the aggregate manufacturing margin print.
- The actual revenue-recognition impact of outcome-based AI contracts on FY27 IT numbers — companies have not yet quantified it in disclosures reviewed.
- Whether July–August FPI buying persists through the next global risk event.

**Unknowable (at this horizon):**
- The pace at which AI repricing works through Indian IT's contract base — this is a multi-year structural question, not a quarterly one.
- Whether the market will in fact "look through" further downgrades, as HSBC predicts — that depends on positioning and global risk appetite, not on earnings data.
- The terminal margin structure of AI-era IT services.

### 9. Open questions

1. What does full-season revision breadth look like in net terms — did FY27 upgrades (materials, financials, industrials, staples) outnumber downgrades (discretionary, health care, utilities, IT drift), or did the aggregate estimate fall?
2. Does the ex-petroleum/+20.2% margin print survive Q2, or was Q1 the input-cost-relief peak?
3. How much FY27 IT revenue will be re-recognised or deflated by outcome-based AI contracts — and when do companies start disclosing it?
4. Does the MoSPI falling-capex-intentions survey or the HAL/PowerGrid order-book story prove right about FY27 investment — breadth or aggregate?
5. Do returning FPI flows (July–August) re-underwrite large-caps (where growth expectations are lower) or chase the SMID growth HSBC says is stronger — and what does that do to index-level resilience?
