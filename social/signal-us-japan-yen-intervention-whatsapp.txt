the us and japan confirmed a coordinated yen-buying intervention on august 3, the first joint operation since 1998.

the numbers:
- japan spent an estimated *¥8.45tn (~$59bn)* on july 30, a record single day, plus *¥5.33tn* in the joint round on july 31. two-day total: *~¥13.8tn (~$85-95bn)*
- usd/jpy peaked at *163.73*, the yen's weakest in 40 years, then fell to 155.20
- the us side, run through the new york fed, was much smaller. jpmorgan notes treasury's fx reserves are finite; unconventional measures could extend capacity to ~$187bn

why would washington spend its reserves supporting another currency? self-interest. japan holds roughly *$1 trillion* of us treasuries. funding solo intervention meant selling those into a bond market already strained by record issuance. better to spend fx reserves than watch your largest creditor turn seller.

both finance ministers used the same phrase: "will not hesitate." a 1% boj and a 3.50-3.75% fed produced the 40-year-low yen. intervention treats the symptom, not the spread.
