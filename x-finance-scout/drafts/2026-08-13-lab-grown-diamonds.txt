Diamonds were never rare. Scarcity was the product.

De Beers spent a century controlling supply and teaching the world that love costs two months' salary. Then reactors started growing the same carbon crystal for a tenth of the price, and the spell broke.

The numbers are brutal:
- Natural prices: lowest this century, down 50%+ since 2022
- Lab-grown: 45% of US engagement rings, from 5% in 2019
- De Beers: written down $6.8B, realised price down 32% in a year, now up for sale

But here's the part everyone misses: the value chain didn't move. In April, India exported more lab-grown diamonds than natural ones — 1.4M vs 1.3M carats. Surat, which polishes 90% of the world's diamonds, just switched what runs through its wheels. Same workers, same skill, different carbon source.

Losers: De Beers shareholders, and anyone who bought a natural stone as an "investment".
Winners: every couple that got a 2.4-carat ring on a 1.3-carat budget.

The moat was never the diamond. It was the story. And stories don't survive factories.
