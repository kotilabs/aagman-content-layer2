## Verified facts

- Natural diamond prices at lowest level this century; down >50% since 2022 (BullTheory tweet, consistent with trade press).
- Lab-grown = ~45% of US engagement rings, up from 5% in 2019 (BullTheory; corroborated by Draco Diamond/industry coverage).
- Wholesale lab-grown prices down ~74-90% since 2020; synthetic retails at ~10% of a comparable natural stone (Forbes Africa, 1 Aug 2026).
- Average lab-grown engagement center stone grew 1.31 ct (2019) → 2.45 ct (2025) — buyers upsized rather than pocketed savings (BriteCo data via Draco).
- De Beers: H1 2026 realised rough price $105/ct vs ~$154/ct a year earlier (-32%); Q2 2026 revenue -45% YoY; 2025 underlying EBITDA loss $511M; Anglo American has written De Beers down $6.8B over 3 years and is in advanced sale talks; Lightbox (its lab-grown containment brand) shut May 2025.
  - Sources: discoveryalert.com.au production analysis, De Beers H1 2026 report coverage (minestomarket.news), Draco Diamond collapse series.
- **India angle**: India's polished lab-grown exports surpassed natural in April 2026 — 1.4M carats LGD vs 1.3M carats natural (GJEPC data via trade coverage). Surat processes ~90% of the world's polished diamonds; Surat units pivoted to lab-grown, which trade press credits with preventing layoffs during the US tariff shock (thesecretariat.in, Oct 2025 — Green Lab Diamonds: exports "nearly doubling YoY", ~6,000 workers).

## Angle

Everyone frames this as "diamond prices crash". The real story: scarcity was the product, not the stone. De Beers sold a century-old story ("A Diamond Is Forever", 1947) enforced by supply control; a machine made scarcity optional and the story collapsed. The underreported twist — the value chain didn't move geographically. Surat just switched what runs through its polishing wheels; India now exports more lab-grown than natural. Winners: couples (bigger stone, same budget) and Surat's workers. Losers: De Beers shareholders and anyone who treated a natural stone as an "investment".
