## Verified facts (web search, 14 Aug 2026)

- **The $30B monthly merch deficit is the 2026 norm, not a spike**: Jan $34.68B (ET Now/Reuters), Apr $28.4B (ICRA), May $28.2B (NDTV Profit), Jun $30.43B vs $19.1B a year earlier (intoglo), Jul ~$32B (tweet, commerce ministry quick estimates). A year ago the run-rate was ~$19-22B.
- **Gold/silver are the swing factor**: FY26 gold+silver import bill $84.03B, +33.7% YoY; precious metals were 43% of the FY26 deficit surge ($21.19B of $48.7B) (Financial Express). FY26 merch deficit $333.2B vs $284.5B FY25. Gold at record highs (futures >$4,500/oz this week per market press).
- **Crude**: 60% of April's deficit widening came from gold + net crude (ICRA). Brent ~$87-88 this week amid Iran-war supply fear (Kotak close-bell note).
- **Exports are fine**: May merch exports record $45.2B (+18% YoY); Q1 FY27 total exports $232.73B, +11.4%. FY26 total exports $860B.
- **Consumer linkage**: rupee ~94.7/USD (was >95 in May); RBI raised FY27 CPI forecast to 5.1% citing energy pass-through; May retail inflation 3.93%, 16-month high (McKinsey GEI).

## Angle

July isn't news — the repricing is. India's monthly merch deficit has stepped ~$10B wider permanently, and the marginal driver isn't factories importing inputs, it's households buying record-price gold (fear hedge) plus the oil bill. Exports at records means the deficit isn't a competitiveness story; it's an inflation-psychology story. The trade data is a mirror of household fear.
