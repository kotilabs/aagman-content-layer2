**Slide 1 (cover)**

US debt just crossed $40 trillion. The number that matters is not the debt. It is the bill.

Public-held debt is at 99.4 percent of GDP, touching the 100 marker.
The interest line has already crossed a different threshold.

**Slide 2**

On August 24, 2026, the Treasury's daily ledger showed total public debt of $40.04 trillion, up about $970 billion since January, more than $4 billion a day. Debt held by the public stood at $32.27 trillion, against second quarter GDP of $32.48 trillion. That is 99.4 percent. The 100 percent milestone this week is, on official arithmetic, a few weeks early.

**Slide 3**

The milestone is the least interesting number in the release. Net interest hit a $1,247 billion annual rate in the second quarter, a record. That is 16.1 percent of federal outlays, one dollar in six. It is 38.2 percent of receipts, nearly 40 cents of every revenue dollar. In fiscal 2025 it was 3.15 percent of GDP, a three-decade high.

**Slide 4**

For scale: net interest now exceeds the entire defense budget, $1,247 billion against $1,198 billion. The last time the interest share of GDP was this high, the early 1990s, it helped force real deficit reduction. This time the share is the same but the deficit behind it is 5.8 percent of GDP, with no recession to justify it.

**Slide 5**

The mechanism is a conveyor belt. Paper issued at 2020 and 2021 coupons keeps maturing and gets refinanced at the Treasury's current average rates: 3.758 percent bills, 3.309 percent notes, 3.442 percent bonds as of July. Every roll replaces cheap paper with current-rate paper. That is why the interest line kept rising even as deficits narrowed. The convergence has years to run.

**Slide 6**

What keeps it arithmetic rather than explosive: the average rate on the stock, about 3.4 percent, sits below nominal GDP growth near 4 percent, and the world still buys Treasuries for reasons beyond yield. What keeps it honest: that only holds while creditors believe the primary deficit is managed. Solvency is not the question. The politics of the interest line is.

**Slide 7**

The same feed tied the debt milestone to consumer collapse, citing 12.9 percent credit card delinquencies. That figure could not be verified. The verified data: household debt service at 11.16 percent of income, stable, and bank charge-off rates on credit cards down from 4.19 percent in early 2025 to 3.82 percent. Stress, if real, is distributional, not aggregate.

**Slide 8**

The debt is a stock, the deficit is a flow, the interest is the bill. Only the bill touches the budget, and the bill is at a record.

**Slide 9**

History gives two readings. In 1946, public debt peaked near 106 percent of GDP and fell to 23 percent by 1974, ground down by nominal growth and captive creditors. In the early 1990s, an interest share like today's forced actual consolidation. Now: no captive creditors, no peace dividend, no productivity boom, and a much wider starting deficit.

**Slide 10**

The answer prints in documents. The third quarter GDP release confirms the 100 percent crossing or not. The next two interest prints show whether $1,247 billion was the top or a waypoint. Refunding statements show whether buybacks become quiet yield management. And the next NY Fed household report verifies or retires the 12.9 percent figure. Watch the bill, not the milestone.
