**Slide 1 (cover)**

India ran an $85.7 billion trade hole last quarter and reported almost no deficit. The plug is one industry.

India's Q1 FY27 current account printed at $3.1 billion, nearly flat to a year ago.
The merchandise deficit underneath it widened by $16.8 billion. The composition is the story.

**Slide 2**

In mid-August, the Reserve Bank of India published its balance of payments data for Q1 FY27. The current account deficit read $3.1 billion, against $2.9 billion a year earlier. Two lines up the same table, merchandise trade showed a deficit of $85.7 billion, up from $68.9 billion. Imports had risen 20 percent to $218.0 billion while exports grew 17 percent to $132.2 billion. The gap between those growth rates is mostly one commodity: crude, priced through a war.

**Slide 3**

The arithmetic then ran like this. Minus $85.7 billion in goods. Plus $52.2 billion in net services exports. Plus $41.4 billion in net transfers, mostly remittances, up 34 percent in a year. The residual: minus $3.1 billion. Services alone covered 61 percent of the goods deficit. Services plus remittances covered 109 percent of it. The headline number is whatever those two flows leave behind.

**Slide 4**

The remittance line was the surprise. Bank of Baroda's chief economist called the 34 percent jump contrary to expectations. The mechanism is exchange rate arithmetic: the rupee fell about 7 percent this year, so each dollar wired home converts into more rupees, pulling remittances forward. A real flow with a cyclical trigger. It strengthens when the currency weakens, and fades if the currency stabilises.

**Slide 5**

The capital account went the other way: from a $7.4 billion surplus to a $5.0 billion deficit, as portfolio investors pulled $9.6 billion. Add both accounts and the quarter printed an $8.1 billion balance of payments deficit, drawn straight from reserves. Then came the June 5 package: banks raising fresh NRI dollar deposits could swap them with the RBI at par, the RBI absorbing the full 280 to 300 basis point hedging cost, deposits exempt from reserve requirements. NRIs were offered 5.5 to 7.1 percent in dollars, tax free.

**Slide 6**

In eleven weeks, $72.8 billion arrived, $65.4 billion of it through the NRI deposit channel. Portfolio flows returned, reserves rebuilt to about $707 billion, and on August 14 the RBI closed the window a month early. The same instrument drew about $26 billion in the 2013 taper tantrum. This time the carry was thin, and the binding constraint was hedging cost. The RBI priced it to zero, and the deposits come due between 2029 and 2031.

**Slide 7**

None of this inflow is the market financing India; it is the sovereign renting out its balance sheet, at a subsidised price, on a schedule.

**Slide 8**

So the honest reading is a fork: a managed episode with exports growing on both sides, or an external account that now clears on one export sector with no second hedge. The essay works through both readings, the 2013 precedent, and the specific prints that will settle it: the RBI quarterly statement's services composition, the August and September customs data against July's $32 billion, and the final swap tally when the window closes on August 31.
