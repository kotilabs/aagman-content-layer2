**Slide 1 (cover)**

India's trade deficit blew out by $17 billion. The reported deficit barely moved.

Q1 FY27 current account: $3.1 billion, near flat to last year.
The composition underneath is one export engine doing almost everything.

**Slide 2**

The RBI's June quarter data shows a current account deficit of $3.1 billion, against $2.9 billion a year ago. Two lines above it, the merchandise trade deficit read $85.7 billion, up from $68.9 billion. Imports rose 20 percent to $218.0 billion. Exports grew 17 percent to $132.2 billion. The difference is mostly crude oil, priced through a war.

**Slide 3**

The arithmetic: minus $85.7 billion in goods, plus $52.2 billion in net services exports, plus $41.4 billion in remittances, up 34 percent in a year. The residual is the $3.1 billion headline. Services alone covered 61 percent of the goods deficit. Services plus remittances covered 109 percent of it.

**Slide 4**

Remittances were the surprise, called contrary to expectations by Bank of Baroda's chief economist. The rupee fell about 7 percent this year, so every dollar wired home buys more rupees. That pulls remittances forward. A real flow with a cyclical trigger, and it fades if the rupee stabilises.

**Slide 5**

The capital account swung from a $7.4 billion surplus to a $5.0 billion deficit as portfolio investors pulled $9.6 billion. The quarter cost $8.1 billion in reserves. Then the June package: NRI dollar deposits swapped with the RBI at par, the RBI absorbing the full hedging cost, so NRIs were offered 5.5 to 7.1 percent in dollars, tax free.

**Slide 6**

Eleven weeks later: $72.8 billion in, $65.4 billion of it NRI deposits. Reserves rebuilt to about $707 billion, and the RBI closed the window a month early. The same instrument drew about $26 billion in 2013. This time it drew two and a half times that, because hedging cost was the constraint and the RBI priced it to zero. The deposits come due between 2029 and 2031.

**Slide 7**

None of this is the market financing India; it is the sovereign renting out its balance sheet, at a price, on a schedule.
