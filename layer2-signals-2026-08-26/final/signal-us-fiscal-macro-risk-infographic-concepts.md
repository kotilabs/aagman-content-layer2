## Recommended concepts (if any)

### Concept 1: "The Bill Passed the Pentagon"
- Central insight: in Q2 2026 the US government's interest bill ($1,247bn annualized) became larger than the entire national defense budget ($1,198bn) — the clearest single comparison of what crowding out means.
- Data: both SAAR levels, Q2 2026; supporting pair: interest 3.15% of GDP FY2025 (three-decade high); FY2025 deficit -5.77% of GDP.
- Visualization type: comparison — two bars (interest vs defense) with the crossing point marked, plus a small inset of interest/GDP 2022–2025 showing the climb.
- Composition notes: the eye goes to the taller red interest bar overtopping the familiar defense bar; the inset explains it was not always so. Payoff: "one in six federal dollars now services the past."
- Sources: FRED A091RC1Q027SBEA, FDEFX, FYOIGDA188S, FYFSGDA188S.

### Concept 2: "One Dollar in Six"
- Central insight: the interest bill's weight inside the budget — 16.1% of outlays, 38.2% of receipts — is the mechanism the 100% headline gestures at.
- Data: the two ratios (Q2 2026) with their denominators ($7,763.7bn outlays, $3,261.4bn receipts); net interest $1,247bn.
- Visualization type: ratio/stack — a budget dollar broken into shares, and a revenue dollar broken into shares.
- Composition notes: two simple 100-block stacks side by side; the interest block highlighted in each. Payoff: the asymmetry between "one in six spent" and "nearly 40 cents of every dollar raised."
- Sources: FRED A091RC1Q027SBEA, FGEXPND, W055RC1.

### Concept 3: "The Milestone Is Early"
- Central insight: the viral "debt exceeds 100% of GDP" claim is directionally right but arithmetically early — and which debt you quote moves the answer by 23 percentage points (public-held 99.4% vs total 122.6%).
- Data: public-held $32.27tn vs Q2 GDP $32.48tn = 99.4% (2026-08-24); total $40.04tn; total/GDP 122.6% (Q1); the +$970bn / $4bn-a-day accretion since January.
- Visualization type: comparison/dual-gauge — two gauges (public-held vs total) against the same 100% line.
- Composition notes: one gauge needle touching 100 from below, the other far past it; a footnote on the daily accretion. Payoff: the reader learns to ask "which debt?" every time the milestone appears.
- Sources: TreasuryDirect (2026-08-24); FRED GFDEGDQ188S, GDP, FYGFDPUN.

### Concept 4: "The Conveyor"
- Central insight: the interest bill compounds because legacy 2020–21 coupons keep maturing into a 3.3–3.8% re-pricing schedule — a mechanism, not an event.
- Data: July 2026 average rates (Bills 3.758%, Notes 3.309%, Bonds 3.442%); net interest path Q3 2024 $1,146bn → Q1 2026 $1,219bn → Q2 2026 $1,247bn.
- Visualization type: flow/timeline — a conveyor of maturing low-coupon paper being replaced at current rates, with the interest line rising alongside.
- Composition notes: left side labeled "issued 2020–21," right side "refinanced 2026," the rate tags on each belt segment; the interest line climbing in the background. Payoff: why the bill rises even when deficits don't.
- Sources: Treasury MSPD via fiscaldata (Jul 2026); FRED A091RC1Q027SBEA.
