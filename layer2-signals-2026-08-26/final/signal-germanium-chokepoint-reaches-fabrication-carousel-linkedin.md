**Slide 1 (cover)**

China found a way to squeeze Taiwan's chip supply chain without banning anything. Customs simply got slower.

Germanium and quartz shipments to Taiwan have faced clearance delays since 2025.
The first market reaction was to claim more than the evidence shows.

**Slide 2**

On August 20, Nikkei Asia reported that several Taiwanese optical manufacturers have faced lengthy customs checks and delays obtaining germanium and quartz materials from Chinese suppliers since last year. Some suppliers on the mainland had been summoned and questioned about their customers. One executive said his company had already lost orders because longer lead times meant missed delivery schedules. Nobody announced a ban. Clearance just started taking longer.

**Slide 3**

The legal chassis underneath this is older. Beijing has required export licenses for gallium and germanium since August 1, 2023, and since December 2024 has generally denied licenses for those materials in dual-use shipments to the United States. That gives the system two dials: formal denial, used against the US, and clearance speed, now documented against Taiwan. The second dial is deniable, reversible, and hard to litigate.

**Slide 4**

The verified impact sits in three places. Optical manufacturers, where germanium goes into infrared lenses and fiber preforms, report industrywide lead-time extensions. One chip equipment executive, where high-purity quartz is irreplaceable, says his company's product lead time has stretched by months. Aerospace suppliers report trouble with Chinese permanent magnets. What no source confirms is any wafer fab cutting output.

**Slide 5**

The price data draws the real map. Chinese domestic germanium fell 9.2 percent in August to $3,104 a kilo, giving back most of July's 27.8 percent surge. American warehouse germanium held at $6,350. The Western premium widened to 104.6 percent, from 82.9 percent in July. The squeeze is in physical availability outside China, not in the headline price. Warehouse stocks and lead times move first; prices follow.

**Slide 6**

Supply cannot ride to the rescue. Germanium is not mined; it is recovered as a byproduct of zinc smelting, roughly 130 to 140 tonnes a year globally, about 80 percent of it in China. US germanium metal shipments through September 2025 were already nearly 80 percent below the same period in 2023, per USGS figures cited by Metal Tech News. Taiwan's answer, a domestic pilot line through its industrial research institute, targets three years out.

**Slide 7**

The market's first draft of this story overshot. A trading desk note on August 23 turned the Nikkei report into a claim that germanium constraints are hitting actual Taiwan fab output, built a short case against TSMC on it, and disclosed that positioning. The verified record supports delays for optics, equipment, and magnets. It does not support stalled wafer lines. Those are different stories with different consequences.

**Slide 8**

One sentence is the whole distinction: the chokepoint moved from prices to lead times, but not, so far as anyone has shown, from lead times to lost wafer output.

**Slide 9**

There is precedent for reading this calmly. In 2010, China informally held rare-earth shipments to Japan through customs procedures during the Senkaku dispute, never announcing a ban. The episode spooked markets, then accelerated Japan's diversification and ended in a WTO ruling against China's quotas. Weaponized supply worked once. The 2026 version is narrower and more careful, which suggests Beijing learned the same lesson.

**Slide 10**

The settlement of this story arrives in documents, not commentary. Taiwan's Q3 import statistics will show whether delays became a volume drop. September commentary from chip equipment and quartz suppliers either carries allocation language or it does not. And any fab that discloses materials-inventory coverage settles the output claim in one line. Until those print, the honest position is the boring one: the pressure is real, the targeting is deliberate, and the largest claim is the one without evidence.
