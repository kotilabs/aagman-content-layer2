US national debt crossed $40 trillion this week. The more instructive fact is that the viral "100% of GDP" milestone is, on official arithmetic, a few weeks early.

Treasury's daily ledger puts debt held by the public at $32.27 trillion against a $32.48 trillion economy: 99.4 percent. Total debt, $40.04 trillion, passed 100% years ago. Which "debt" you quote moves the answer by 23 percentage points.

The number that deserves the attention is the bill. Net interest reached a $1,247 billion annual rate in the second quarter, a record. That is 16.1 percent of all federal outlays, one dollar in six, and 38.2 percent of receipts, nearly 40 cents of every revenue dollar. It now exceeds the entire defense budget of $1,198 billion. In fiscal 2025 it was 3.15 percent of GDP, a three-decade high.

The mechanism is a conveyor, not a cliff. Paper issued at 2020 and 2021 coupons keeps maturing and refinancing at current average rates of 3.31 to 3.76 percent. The interest line rises even if the deficit does nothing. The last time the interest share of GDP sat here, the early 1990s, it helped force real fiscal consolidation. Today's deficit behind that share is 5.8 percent of GDP, with no recession to justify it.

Who bears the cost: every future budget, as the discretionary share compresses arithmetically.

The open question: do the next two quarterly prints show $1,247 billion as the conveyor's top, or a waypoint?
