# Standalone Carousel — Telecom Tariff Hikes and Pricing-Power Consolidation

**Signal ID:** telecom-tariff-hikes-sector
**Format:** Standalone carousel (self-contained argument; no external link required)
**Slides:** 10

---

## Slide 1 — Hook

**Kicker:** August 2026

**Headline:** The cheapest way to keep an Airtel SIM just went up 17%. Nobody called it a tariff hike.

**Body:** Airtel deleted its ₹299 prepaid plan this month. The ₹349 plan is now the floor. It is the second time in twelve months the entry price has moved — ₹249 to ₹299 last August, ₹299 to ₹349 now. When you raise prices by deleting the cheap option, you never have to announce a hike at all.

**Hero stat/quote:** Airtel entry plan: ₹249 (pre-Aug 2025) → ₹299 (Aug 2025) → ₹349 (Aug 2026). +40% in 24 months — *Gadgets360, 5 Aug 2026*

**Design note:** A staircase of three price tags ascending left to right, the first two greyed out with strikethrough. Airtel red on black. No logos beyond the prices — the numbers are the brand.

**Speaker note:** Establishes the mechanism in one concrete move: the floor-raise, not the headline hike.

## Slide 2 — The headline number

**Kicker:** The cycle

**Headline:** A 12–15% hike across all three operators is expected within 100 days.

**Body:** Jio, Airtel and Vi are expected to raise prepaid tariffs by 12–15% by roughly November, according to multiple reports tracking the sector. Postpaid follows later. The structure matters more than the quantum: heavier data users pay more, and the entry tier is already being repriced in advance. Jefferies called this hike in January — and even with the timing slipping past its June call, the script held. When the sell side can pre-book a price increase, it is not really a market price.

**Hero stat/quote:** Jefferies, January 2026: mobile tariffs expected to rise 15% around June 2026, two years after the last hikes — *as reported by NDTV Profit, 8 Jan 2026*

**Design note:** A calendar strip from July to November with three operator colour dots (blue, red, yellow-green) landing in the same window. Thin timeline, generous white space.

**Speaker note:** Shows coordination by calendar, not by conspiracy.

## Slide 3 — How the market got here

**Kicker:** 2016 → 2026

**Headline:** India went from a dozen telecom operators to three and a half. That is the whole story.

**Body:** Jio's 2016 launch — free voice, near-free data — burned the industry down. Aircel, Reliance Communications, Tata Teleservices, Telenor, MTS: gone. Vodafone and Idea merged to survive. What remains: Jio (499 million wireless subscribers), Airtel (481 million), a wounded Vi (199 million), and BSNL (93 million) losing ground. Price wars end when there is no one left to fight.

**Hero stat/quote:** Operators before Jio: 10–15. Operators now: 3 private + BSNL — *TRAI subscription data, April 2026*

**Design note:** A funnel graphic: a crowd of ten-plus operator logos at the top collapsing into four dots at the bottom. Desaturated logos for the dead, full colour for the survivors.

**Speaker note:** Consolidation is the precondition; everything else follows from it.

## Slide 4 — The profits problem

**Kicker:** The justification, examined

**Headline:** The case for the hike is "tariff repair." The repaired numbers say otherwise.

**Body:** The official rationale: 5G cost ₹1.5 lakh crore in spectrum alone, and returns must recover. But the repair already happened. Airtel just reported ₹8,167 crore in quarterly profit, up 37%, on ARPU of ₹264. Jio made ₹7,764 crore on ARPU of ₹215.6 — and is IPO-bound. Combined FY26 net profit of the two leaders: roughly ₹64,000 crore, per former government secretary E.A.S. Sarma's letter to the DoT — an advocacy document, so treat the exact figures as single-sourced. The 5G build is 99.9% done by district coverage. The next hike is not funding construction. It is funding returns.

**Hero stat/quote:** Q1 FY27: Airtel profit ₹8,167 cr (+37%); Jio profit ₹7,764 cr (+9%) — *company results, Jul–Aug 2026. FY26 combined ~₹64,000 cr: Sarma letter via Countercurrents, 5 Jul 2026 (single source)*

**Design note:** Two profit bars rising against a flat, thin line labelled "5G districts left to cover: 0.1%." Institutional blue and red on white; FT-chart restraint.

**Speaker note:** Separates the capex-recovery era from the margin-expansion era with their own reported numbers.

## Slide 5 — The ratchet

**Kicker:** The mechanism

**Headline:** Tariffs in India do not cycle. They ratchet.

**Body:** 2019: hike. 2021: hike. 2024: hike. 2026: hike (expected). Each round is absorbed with barely a murmur of churn, because there is nowhere to churn to. No increase has ever been given back. Airtel added 4.9 million subscribers in a single month this year while being the most expensive operator. In a market where connectivity is load-bearing infrastructure — UPI, Aadhaar, OTPs — demand does not negotiate.

**Hero stat/quote:** ARPU today: Airtel ₹264, Jio ₹216, Vi ₹190. ARPU at the price-war trough (~2018–19): under ₹100 — *TRAI / company filings*

**Design note:** A step chart that only ever steps up, each step labelled with a year. No downward line anywhere on the slide — the absence is the point.

**Speaker note:** Names the core dynamic: one-way pricing in an essential service.

## Slide 6 — The people priced out

**Kicker:** The boundary

**Headline:** 150 million Indians use phones that don't need data. They still have to pay for it.

**Body:** Feature-phone and voice-only users — roughly 150 million, overwhelmingly rural, elderly and low-income — must buy bundled plans whose data they never touch. Consumer groups calculate the effective price of entry-level packs at ₹94–99 per GB, the highest unit rate in the market. Premium users pay a fraction of that. The cheapest customers are subsidising the richest data economics. A ₹349 floor is not a pricing detail for this group; it is a tax on staying reachable.

**Hero stat/quote:** ~150 million non-data users; entry packs at ₹94–99 per effective GB — *TRAI / consumer-group submissions, June 2026*

**Design note:** A single feature phone silhouette on a large empty field, with the ₹94–99/GB figure set in oversized type beside it. No gradient, no gloss.

**Speaker note:** Pivots the argument from investors to distribution — the emotional and political centre.

---

## Slide 7 — The regulator's counter-move

**Kicker:** TRAI vs the trio

**Headline:** The regulator wants a cheap voice-only plan. All three operators are fighting it.

**Body:** TRAI's draft amendment would force operators to sell a voice-and-SMS-only voucher at every validity period, priced proportionately below the bundled plan. At the June open house, Jio, Airtel and Vi opposed it in unison — technically impractical, fraud-prone, anti-consumer, they said. They said the same in 2024, when TRAI overruled them and mandated the first voucher. Operators complied by offering it only at 84-day and 365-day validities, barely cheaper than the bundle. The 2026 amendment exists to close that loophole.

**Hero stat/quote:** TRAI mandated voice-only vouchers in 2024. Operators offered them only at long validities, without proportionate price cuts — *MediaNama, 16 June 2026*

**Design note:** A tug-of-rope composition: TRAI on one side, three coloured dots pulling together on the other. The rope is a price tag.

**Speaker note:** The unbundling fight is the real battle — it attacks the cross-subsidy, not the price level.

## Slide 8 — The state's conflict of interest

**Kicker:** Follow the creditor

**Headline:** The government needs the hike as much as the operators do.

**Body:** Vodafone Idea owes the state ₹64,046 crore in reassessed AGR dues, frozen until 2041. Vi's cash EBITDA covers less than half its finance costs; analysts say it needs ARPU of ₹340 by FY29 — nearly double today's ₹190 — to survive on its own cash. Its ₹25,000 crore bank lifeline is conditioned on tariff hikes. Every hike therefore protects the recoverable value of public money. The state is the affordability guardian, the sector's biggest creditor, a Vi shareholder, and the owner of BSNL — all at once.

**Hero stat/quote:** Vi cash EBITDA ₹2,358 cr vs finance costs ₹5,632 cr (Q3 FY26); AGR dues ₹64,046 cr frozen to 2041 — *company filings / Multibagg.ai*

**Design note:** A four-way org chart with "Government of India" at the centre, arrows labelled regulator / creditor / shareholder / operator-owner. Neutral grey, one red arrow for the conflict.

**Speaker note:** The most structurally important slide: explains why forbearance persists.

## Slide 9 — The macro read

**Kicker:** Why it matters beyond telecom

**Headline:** A phone-bill hike is an inflation print, an IPO pricing, and a fiscal line item in one.

**Body:** The 2024 hike added about 0.2 percentage points to core inflation; a 15% round lands with June CPI already at 4.38% and complicates the RBI's path. Jio's imminent IPO will be priced off post-hike ARPU — the hike is partly a listing document. And higher access costs tax the next 100 million users that India's consumer-internet growth story is priced on. Telecom pricing is no longer a sector story. It is macro policy conducted by three companies.

**Hero stat/quote:** July 2024 hike: ~+0.2pp core inflation. June 2026 CPI: 4.38% — *ET, 3 Jul 2024 / MoSPI*

**Design note:** Three thin panels — a CPI line, an IPO ticker placeholder, a rising staircase of entry prices — sharing one axis labelled "the same hike."

**Speaker note:** Lifts the sector story into macro, which is where the audience lives.

## Slide 10 — Close

**Kicker:** The open question

**Headline:** The price war built India's internet. The peace will be billed to its poorest users.

**Body:** For a decade, the consumer surplus of the Jio era — the cheapest data in the world — was funded by operator losses and a body count of competitors. That bill has come due, and the consolidated industry is collecting it from the bottom of the pyramid upward. The only live checks left are a regulator testing the limits of its own mandate, and a state that cannot decide whether it is referee or creditor. Watch the voice-only voucher fight. That, not the hike percentage, decides what kind of market this is.

**Hero stat/quote:** ₹349 today. If the 15% lands on the floor: ~₹400 to keep a basic connection alive.

**Design note:** Return to the slide-1 staircase, now with a fourth, ghosted step at ~₹400 marked with a question mark. Same visual language, one ominous addition.

**Speaker note:** Closes the loop on the opening image and hands the reader a single thing to watch.
