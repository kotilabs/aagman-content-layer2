airtel removed its ₹299 prepaid plan in early august. ₹349 is now the minimum cost of keeping a sim active, with no hike announced.

the numbers:
- the entry floor has gone ₹249 to ₹299 to ₹349 in two years, a *40%* rise in the minimum cost of a connection
- jio, airtel and vi are expected to raise prepaid tariffs *12-15% by november*. if it lands on the floor: ~60%
- airtel and jio made *~₹64,000 crore* net profit in fy26

why they can:
- three private operators remain of the 10-15 that existed before jio's 2016 launch; customers have nowhere to port
- trai practises tariff forbearance: it does not set prices
- the 5g build is *99.9% complete*, so this hike funds margins, not construction

who pays most: ~150 million feature-phone users on bundled plans pay an effective *₹94-99 per gb*, the highest unit rate in the market, for data they never use.

the full piece gets into what doesn't fit here: how deleting the cheapest plan replaced the headline hike, the government's four-way conflict as regulator, creditor, vi shareholder and bsnl owner, and the trai voice-only voucher rule all three operators opposed in june.
