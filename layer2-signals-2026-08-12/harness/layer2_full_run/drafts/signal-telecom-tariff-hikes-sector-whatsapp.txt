airtel removed its ₹299 prepaid plan in early august. ₹349 is now the minimum cost of keeping a sim active, a 17% rise at the entry point. the floor was ₹249 a year ago, so the minimum is up 40% in twelve months without a single announced hike.

jio, airtel and vi are expected to raise prepaid tariffs *12-15% by november*. if the hike reaches the floor, the entry price will be up ~60% in two years.

context:
- three private operators remain of the 10-15 that existed before jio's 2016 launch; trai practises tariff forbearance and does not set prices
- airtel and jio reported a combined *~₹64,000 crore* net profit in fy26
- the 5g build is *99.9% complete*, so incremental tariff now funds margins, not construction
- ~150 million feature-phone users on bundled plans pay an effective *₹94-99 per gb*, the highest unit rate in the market, for data they do not use (consumer-group submissions to trai, june 2026)

the one open regulatory item: trai's draft rule mandating proportionately cheaper voice-and-sms-only vouchers at every validity. all three operators opposed it in june.
