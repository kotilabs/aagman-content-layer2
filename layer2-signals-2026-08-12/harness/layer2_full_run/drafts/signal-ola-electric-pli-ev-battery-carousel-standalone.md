# Standalone Carousel — Ola Electric's ₹7,240 Crore PLI Unlock

## Slide 1

- **Kicker:** India's battery subsidy, five years late
- **Headline:** ₹7,240 crore. Commissioning done, investment done — one gate still unverified.
- **Body:** The Ministry of Heavy Industries has given Ola Electric's cell subsidiary a full five-year incentive window through 2031 — the largest award under India's ₹18,100 crore battery scheme, and the first that is actually close to paying out.
- **Hero stat:** ₹7,240 crore — cumulative PLI ceiling approved for Ola Cell Technologies, August 12, 2026 (BSE filing via Moneycontrol)
- **Speaker note:** Opens with the concrete number and the tension that carries the whole deck: approval versus payout.

---

## Slide 2

- **Kicker:** What the scheme was supposed to do
- **Headline:** 50 GWh of Indian cells. Approved 2021. Delivered by October 2025: 1.4 GWh.
- **Body:** The ACC PLI scheme offered per-kWh subsidies, declining over five years, to anyone who would build gigafactories in India — 25% domestic value addition in two years, 60% in five, ₹225 crore invested per GWh — and its evaluation criteria picked first-time cell makers over the two experienced battery manufacturers, Exide and Amara Raja. Four years in, 2.8% of the target capacity existed.
- **Hero stat:** 1.4 GWh commissioned of a 50 GWh target — 2.8% (IEEFA/JMK Research, Jan 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Establishes the scheme's delivery gap so the Ola extension reads as salvage, not generosity.

---

## Slide 3

- **Kicker:** What the scheme had paid out
- **Headline:** ₹5,180 crore invested. ₹0 disbursed.
- **Body:** As of May 31, 2026, every ACC PLI beneficiary combined — Ola, Reliance's two entities, and ACC Energy Storage, the entity that won Rajesh Exports' 5 GWh bid — had claimed not a single rupee. Subsidies only flow after commissioning and verified localisation. Everyone was still in gestation.
- **Hero stat:** Zero incentive claims across all beneficiaries as of May 31, 2026 (Lok Sabha written reply, July 21, 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The zero-payout fact is the scheme's defining statistic; it sets up why Ola's window matters.

---

## Slide 4

- **Kicker:** The pivot
- **Headline:** This isn't a deadline extension. It's a rescued revenue stream.
- **Body:** Ola had blown its original milestones. Its auditor flagged a ₹57 crore liquidated-damages provision; the scheme penalises delay at 0.1% of the performance security per day. The revision waives the overhang and restores a *full* five-year quarterly window to 2031 — instead of a truncated stub on a fixed clock.
- **Hero stat:** "More than an extension… converting an earlier milestone overhang into a five-year, quarterly PLI opportunity" — Bhavish Aggarwal (Fortune India, Aug 12, 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The explicit thesis slide: the mechanism (waived penalties + full window) is the story, not the headline crore figure.

---

## Slide 5

- **Kicker:** Why it's pure upside
- **Headline:** Management had already written the PLI off.
- **Body:** "We hadn't factored any incentives into our business projections after overshooting the original timelines," Aggarwal said on August 12. No analyst model carried the ₹7,240 crore. Once disbursements begin — scheduled for next quarter — the stream lands directly in the cell segment's margins.
- **Hero stat:** ₹7,240 crore ≈ 21x Ola's Q1 FY27 net loss of ₹336 crore (StartupTalky, Aug 7, 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The "not in projections" admission is unusually verifiable for a CEO claim — use it to size the stream against the P&L.

---

## Slide 6

- **Kicker:** The capacity beneath the promise
- **Headline:** 2.5 GWh installed. 3.5 GWh being installed. 6 GWh by quarter-end.
- **Body:** Ola is the only ACC beneficiary with commissioned capacity — in fact, the only meaningful cell production in India, against roughly 178 GWh of announced — not committed — capacity nationwide. The revised December 2026 milestone is one it now expects to beat.
- **Hero stat:** ~1.4 GWh — India's total commissioned cell capacity as of early 2026, vs ~178 GWh announced (IMARC, May 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Grounds the subsidy in physical fact: whatever India has built in cells, Ola built it.

---

## Slide 7

- **Kicker:** The economics
- **Headline:** At full tilt, the ceiling is worth roughly $8–9 per kWh.
- **Body:** ₹7,240 crore spread across five years of a 20 GWh line works out to about ₹0.72 per watt-hour. Against Chinese LFP cells around $55/kWh, that is support of roughly 15% of sales value — not enough to make Indian cells cheap, but enough to make the learning curve survivable. But that ceiling assumes full utilisation from day one; the plant is at 2.5 GWh installed today, and the scheme's declining rates front-load value into the early years Ola will spend ramping. Realized incentives will very likely land far below ₹7,240 crore.
- **Hero stat:** ~15% of sales value — implied support vs ~$55/kWh LFP benchmark (derived; tender analysis per AGAIC Power, Jul 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Translates the crore headline into unit economics; labels the derivation honestly.

---

## Slide 8

- **Kicker:** The dependency it is meant to break
- **Headline:** 75% of the batteries in India's BEVs come predominantly from China.
- **Body:** India's cell import dependence is near 100%, and its battery import bill has risen eightfold from $384 million. A 20 GWh domestic line doesn't shrink that bill soon — it changes its composition, from finished Chinese cells to imported cathode, separator and electrolyte feeding an Indian plant. That upstream dependence is not just strategic: later-year payouts gate on 60% domestic value addition by year five, a localisation level the imported material chain may not reach inside the window.
- **Hero stat:** 8x — growth in India's battery import bill from a $384 million base (IEEFA, May 2026)
- **Speaker note:** The strategic rationale, stated without nationalist gloss: localisation moves the import bill upstream first.

---

## Slide 9

- **Kicker:** Who else is watching
- **Headline:** Reliance holds 15 GWh of awards. Rajesh Exports may lose 5 GWh. The template now exists.
- **Body:** MHI has shown it will extend rather than enforce. That matters to Reliance — whose first-round 5 GWh is delayed even as it talks of 120 GWh at Jamnagar — and to a scheme still holding 10 GWh unallocated, with Rajesh Exports' 5 GWh under review after a SEBI order alleging revenue inflation.
- **Hero stat:** 40 GWh of 50 GWh awarded; 10 GWh still unawarded after Hyundai's exit and the 2024 re-tender (Lok Sabha reply, July 21, 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Widens from company event to policy precedent — the extension re-prices every other award in the scheme.

---

## Slide 10

- **Kicker:** The open question
- **Headline:** The scheme has paid nothing to anyone, ever. Next quarter changes that — or the window is just a longer leash.
- **Body:** Approval is arithmetic; disbursement is verification. Watch the Oct–Dec 2026 quarter: does the first quarterly payment land, at what localisation-verified rate, and does Ola's cell output — feeding a vehicle business whose revenue fell 45% last quarter — absorb a 20 GWh line before the window closes in 2031?
- **Hero stat:** Q3 FY27 (Oct–Dec 2026) — scheduled start of the first quarterly PLI disbursement (Moneycontrol, Aug 12, 2026)
- **Speaker note:** Closes on the falsifiable test, not a verdict: the deck's whole argument hangs on whether zero becomes non-zero.
