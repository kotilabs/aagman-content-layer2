## Recommended concepts

### Concept 1: The Staircase (hero)

- **Central insight:** India's mobile tariffs do not cycle — they ratchet. The cleanest evidence is Airtel's entry floor: ₹249 → ₹299 → ₹349 in 24 months, a 40% rise in the price of staying connected, achieved by deleting plans rather than announcing hikes.
- **Data to include:**
  - Airtel entry plan: ₹249 (pre-Aug 2025) → ₹299 (Aug 2025) → ₹349 (Aug 2026), each step annotated with the mechanism ("plan repriced" / "plan discontinued").
  - Ghosted fourth step at ~₹400 labelled "if the expected 12–15% hike lands on the floor."
  - Side rail: hike cadence 2019 / 2021 / 2024 / 2026 with "none ever reversed."
- **Visualization type:** Ascending step chart (single series, no downward lines anywhere), with a ghosted future step.
- **Composition notes:** The staircase dominates the frame, rising left to right. Each step is a price tag, not a bar — the object being bought is the point. The ghost step carries the only question mark in the graphic. Caption line: "The cost of keeping a basic Airtel SIM alive."
- **Sources:** Gadgets360, 5 Aug 2026; Trak.in, 6 Aug 2026.
- **Hero stat:** ₹349 — up 40% from ₹249 in 24 months.

### Concept 2: Three Balance Sheets, One Hike

- **Central insight:** The same 15% hike means three different things: margin for Airtel, an IPO multiple for Jio, and survival for Vi. One price move, three balance-sheet functions.
- **Data to include:**
  - Airtel: ARPU ₹264, quarterly profit ₹8,167 cr (+37%) — "the hike is margin."
  - Jio: ARPU ₹215.6, profit ₹7,764 cr, 533M subs, IPO imminent — "the hike is the listing document."
  - Vi: ARPU ₹190, cash EBITDA ₹2,358 cr vs finance costs ₹5,632 cr, needs ₹340 ARPU by FY29 — "the hike is oxygen."
- **Visualization type:** Three-column comparison with a common horizontal "ARPU" axis, each column carrying one verdict line beneath the numbers.
- **Composition notes:** Equal-width columns, colour-coded per operator. The ARPU axis runs across all three so the ₹264/₹216/₹190 spread reads instantly. Vi's column shows the EBITDA-to-finance-cost gap as a small red bar visibly shorter than its cost bar — the only red in the graphic.

### Concept 3: The 150 Million at the Edge

- **Central insight:** The hike debate shifts from ARPU recovery to affordability: ~150 million feature-phone and voice-only users pay the market's highest effective unit price (₹94–99 per GB on entry packs) for data they cannot use, while TRAI's fight for a proportionately priced voice-only plan is opposed by all three operators.
- **Data to include:**
  - ~150 million basic/feature-phone users (TRAI).
  - Effective per-GB price: entry packs ₹94–99 vs premium packs (a fraction thereof).
  - Timeline of the voucher fight: TRAI mandates voice-only voucher (2024) → operators offer it only at 84/365-day validities → TRAI draft 13th Amendment (2026) → all three operators oppose (15 Jun 2026).

### Concept 4: The State's Four Hats

- **Central insight:** The government is simultaneously telecom's regulator, its largest creditor (₹64,046 cr of Vi AGR dues frozen to 2041), a Vi shareholder, and the owner of BSNL — so tariff policy is quietly fiscal policy, and forbearance has a balance-sheet explanation.
- **Data to include:**
  - Creditor hat: Vi AGR dues ₹64,046 cr, frozen till 2041; deferred spectrum receivables across all three operators.
  - Shareholder hat: government equity in Vi post-conversion.
  - Regulator hat: TRAI's draft voice-only voucher amendment; the sub-₹99 affordability push.
  - Operator hat: BSNL, 93M subscribers, still losing users.
  - The binding constraint: Vi's ₹25,000 cr bank raise conditioned on tariff hikes — every hike protects public money.
- **Visualization type:** Hub-and-spoke diagram — "Government of India" hub with four labelled spokes, each carrying one number; a fifth arrow loops from "tariff hike" back into the hub.
- **Composition notes:** Neutral institutional palette with the loop-back arrow in a single accent colour — it is the mechanism (hikes defend the state's own claims). Keep each spoke to one line of text; density kills this graphic.
- **Sources:** Multibagg.ai (4 May / 29 Jun 2026), tele.net.in (3 Jun 2026).
- **Hero stat:** ₹64,046 crore — the public claim that quietly depends on your phone bill rising.

### Concept 5: From a Dozen to Three (context strip)

- **Central insight:** Pricing power is the end-state of a decade of consolidation: 10–15 operators before Jio's 2016 entry, three private players plus BSNL today — and the survivors now price in lockstep on a two-year cadence.
- **Data to include:**
  - Pre-2016 operator count (10–15) vs today (Jio 499M, Airtel 481M, Vi 199M, BSNL 93M wireless subs).
  - The eliminated/absorbed: Aircel, RCom, Tata Teleservices, Telenor, MTS; Vodafone+Idea merger (2018).
  - Hike cadence markers: 2019 / 2021 / 2024 / expected 2026 (12–15%).
  - ARPU arc: under ₹100 at the price-war trough (~2018–19) → ₹264 (Airtel) today.

## What to avoid

- **Any visual built on the X/Twitter post.** The cluster anchor could not be fetched or verified. Use it as a distribution note in copy at most, never as a data source.
- **Claiming collusion.** Parallel pricing in a transparent oligopoly is legal and predictable. Visuals should show *coordination by calendar* (same-window hikes) and let the reader conclude; never depict smoke-filled-room framing or the word "cartel."
- **The "₹100,000 crore plunder" framing from the Countercurrents letter.** It is an advocacy document. Its profit table is usable (with attribution); its rhetoric is not a data point.
- **Treating the 12–15% hike as announced fact.** It is reported and expected, with Jefferies' January call as corroboration — but no operator has published new tariffs. All visuals must use "expected" language and avoid stating post-hike prices as fact (₹400 floor only as a conditional ghost).
- **A "Jio IPO conspiracy" visual.** The IPO-hike sequencing is a legitimate analytic point, but a dedicated graphic would rest on inference. Keep it to one line inside Concept 2.
- **Rural-wage equivalency framings** ("a day's wages for a phone bill"). No verified wage data was gathered; emotive equivalences without sourcing violate the artifact's evidence standard.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with **Concept 1 (The Staircase) as the hero graphic** and **Concept 2 (Three Balance Sheets) as the analytical companion**. Concept 1 carries the argument in a single glance — a ratchet that only moves up — and every number in it is hard-verified. Concept 2 supplies the "why now" with Q1 FY27 results that are days old.

Concept 3 is the strongest *political* story and should be the third panel if the format allows; its regulatory timeline is unique to this signal and no competitor content will have it. Concept 4 is the most original analysis but the hardest to execute honestly — hub-and-spoke diagrams invite clutter; attempt only with disciplined single-number spokes. Concept 5 works strictly as a footer strip under Concept 1 or 2, never standalone.

Sequence for a single-scroll vertical: 1 → 2 → 3, with 5 as the connective strip and 4 held for a follow-up piece on the state's creditor conflict.
